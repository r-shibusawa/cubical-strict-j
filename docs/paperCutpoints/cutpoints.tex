\title[Abstract domains and cut points in the sameness lattice]{Abstract domains and cut points in the lattice of two-out-of-three classes: joins of closure operators on a lattice and of cut-point topologies on a control-flow graph}
\author{Ryota Shibusawa}
\thanks{Corresponding author. E-mail: \texttt{r-shibusawa@daiichi-koudai.ac.jp}}
\address{Daiichi Institute of Technology}
\email{r-shibusawa@daiichi-koudai.ac.jp}
\keywords{closure operator, abstract interpretation, abstract domain, reduced product, Grothendieck topology, presheaf topos, control-flow graph, cut point, two-out-of-three, join of localisations}
\subjclass[2020]{18F10 (primary), 06A15, 68Q55, 18B25 (secondary)}

\begin{abstract}
An abstract domain, a closure operator $\rho$ on a complete lattice,
determines the class $W_\rho$ of pairs $x\le y$ it collapses, an element
of the lattice of two-out-of-three classes.
$W_\rho\cap W_{\rho'}$ is the class of the reduced product,
$W_\rho\vee W_{\rho'}$ is the class of pairs with $y\le(\rho\rho')^kx$ for
some $k$, and it is the class of the least common abstraction iff
the alternation $\rho\rho'\rho\rho'\cdots$ is finite at every point;
cancellation is needed when meets are not preserved. On the path category of a directed graph, a set $S$ of vertices gives the
topology whose sheaves are determined at $S$. For two sets $S,T$ the join
of the two inverted classes is the class of the join topology iff the
alternations between $S$ and $T$ along paths avoiding $S\cap T$ are
bounded; for a finite graph, iff no strongly connected component of the
graph minus $S\cap T$ meets both sets. The join
topology is that of $S\cap T$ iff no infinite backward walk avoiding
$S\cap T$ alternates between the sets. When the alternation is
bounded by $a$, exactly $a+1$ rounds of alternating reconstruction from
the values at $T$ and at $S$ give the reconstruction from $S\cap T$.
The results extend to flows with idempotent transfer functions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In one sentence: for two abstractions of the same concrete lattice, the
join of the two classes of ``pairs the abstraction cannot tell apart'' is
generated by alternating the two abstractions and cancelling, and it is
the class of the least common abstraction exactly when the alternation is
finite at every point; for the abstractions of a flow on a control-flow
graph by its values at two sets of cut points, the finiteness is a
condition on closed walks tested in linear time, the common sheaves are
the flows determined at the common cut points exactly when no infinite
backward walk alternates between the two sets, reconstructing
alternately from the two sets reaches the reconstruction from the common
cut points in exactly $a+1$ rounds for the bound $a$ on the alternation,
and all of this persists for flows with idempotent transfer functions,
whose fixed points act as additional cut points.

Let $\mathcal C$ be a category. A \emph{sameness} on $\mathcal C$ is a
class of morphisms containing the identities and closed under composition
and two-out-of-three; samenesses form a complete lattice $\Same(\mathcal
C)$, the meet being the intersection and the join the least sameness
containing both \cite{Same}. Every localisation of $\mathcal C$
contributes the class $W$ of maps it inverts, and the question studied in
\cite{Topjoin,Finitejoin,Stabjoin,Fijoin} is whether $W$ sends the join
of two localisations to the join of the two classes. For localisations
given by closure operators on subobjects (Lawvere--Tierney topologies,
hereditary torsion theories, nuclei) the answer of \cite{Fijoin} is a
finite-iteration criterion: in a Grothendieck topos or a Grothendieck
abelian category, $W_c\vee W_{c'}=W_{\bar c}$ for the least closure
operator $\bar c$ above $c$ and $c'$ iff $(cc')^k=\bar c$ for some $k$.

This paper applies the criterion in two settings suggested by the
semantics of programs. The first is the lattice of abstract domains of
abstract interpretation \cite{CC79}: an abstract domain on a complete
lattice $L$ of concrete properties is an upper closure operator
$\rho$ on $L$, the closure operators form a complete lattice
$\uco(L)$ \cite{Ward}, the reduced product of two domains is their meet
and their least common abstraction is their join \cite{GR97}. The
category is the poset $L$ itself, so that the maps are the inequalities
$x\le y$, and the class $W_\rho$ consists of the inequalities that $\rho$
collapses, $\rho x=\rho y$: the refinements $y$ of $x$ that the analysis
cannot see. General abstract domains are not nuclei, they need not
preserve binary meets (intervals, octagons, reachability under a
transition relation), and the criterion of \cite{Fijoin} for nuclei on a
frame does not apply to them as it stands. Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice}
treats arbitrary closure operators on a complete lattice: the meet is
always preserved, the join is the class of pairs $x\le y$ with $y\le
(\rho\rho')^kx$ for some $k$, it is the class of $\rho\sqcup\rho'$ iff the
alternation reaches $\rho\sqcup\rho'$ at a finite stage at every $x$,
and, unlike the case of nuclei, the two-out-of-three cancellation is
needed to generate the join from the alternating chains (already on the
four-element Boolean lattice). Section~\ref{sec:reach} spells this out for
the reachability closures $S\mapsto R^*(S\cup I)$ of transition relations:
the join of the two classes is bounded alternation of the two relations,
it is the class of the union relation iff the alternation depth is
finite from every set of states, which holds for finite state spaces with
bound the number of states and fails on $\mathbb N$ for the even and the
odd steps.

The second setting is the presheaf topos on the path category of a
directed graph $G$, a control-flow graph: a presheaf is a flow of sets
along the control flow, and a set $S$ of vertices, a set of cut points in
the sense of Bourdoncle \cite{Bourdoncle}, determines the Grothendieck
topology $J_S$ whose sheaves are the presheaves determined by their values
at $S$, $\Sh(J_S)\simeq\Psh(S)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:cut} says that
$W_{J_S}\vee W_{J_T}=W_{J_S\vee J_T}$ iff the number of alternations
between visits to $T$ and visits to $S$ along paths that avoid $S\cap T$
is bounded; for a finite graph, iff no closed walk avoiding $S\cap T$ meets
both $S$ and $T$, i.e.\ no strongly connected component of $G$ with
$S\cap T$ removed meets both, a condition decided in linear time; the
bound is then $\min(|S\setminus T|,|T\setminus S|)$ and the join topology
is $J_{S\cap T}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:topjoin} identifies the join topology
in general: it is $J_{S\cap T}$ iff no infinite backward walk avoiding
$S\cap T$ visits both sets infinitely often, a condition weaker than
bounded alternation which coincides with it on finite graphs.
Theorem~\ref{thm:iter} describes the iterated reconstructions
$(a_Sa_T)^kX$ as limits over the paths with $k$ alternations and shows
that, when the alternation is bounded by $a$, the sheafification for the
join is $(a_Sa_T)^{a+1}$, with $a+1$ sharp: for cut-point topologies the
finite-step factorisation of the join sheafification asked for in
\cite{Fijoin} holds with exactly the number of steps of the closure
operators. The theorem interpolates between two known
facts: on a finite category every pair of topologies has a preserved join
\cite{Finitejoin}, which is the acyclic case, and on the poset
$\mathbb N$ the topologies of the even and of the odd numbers do not
\cite{Topjoin}, which is the infinite path with unbounded alternation. A
third section places the cut-point topologies among the ideal
topologies, the essential subtoposes of Kelly and Lawvere \cite{KL89}:
an ideal topology generated by idempotents is a cut-point topology on
the Karoubi envelope (Lemma~\ref{lem:kar}), and the three theorems
extend to flows with idempotent transfer functions
(Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}), where an idempotent loop in one cut set
whose vertex is in the other acts as a common cut point; for finite
action monoids the ideal topologies are indexed by sets of idempotent
actions, resets, and the reading of the closure is ``every future
through a reset lands in the invariant''.

\section{Closure operators on a complete lattice}\label{sec:lattice}

Let $L$ be a complete lattice, regarded as a category with a morphism
$x\to y$ iff $x\le y$. A \emph{closure operator} on $L$ is a map
$\rho\colon L\to L$ that is extensive ($x\le\rho x$), monotone and
idempotent; its set of fixed points $\Fix\rho=\rho(L)$ is closed under
arbitrary meets, and $\rho x$ is the least fixed point above $x$. The
closure operators on $L$ form a complete lattice $\uco(L)$ under the
pointwise order \cite{Ward}, which is the reverse inclusion of fixed-point
sets; the meet of $\rho,\rho'$ is $x\mapsto\rho x\wedge\rho'x$, with fixed
points the meets $a\wedge b$, $a\in\Fix\rho$, $b\in\Fix\rho'$, and the join
$\rho\sqcup\rho'$ is the closure operator with fixed points
$\Fix\rho\cap\Fix\rho'$. In abstract interpretation \cite{CC79,GR97},
$\rho\le\rho'$ means that $\rho$ is more precise, the meet is the reduced
product and the join the least common abstraction.

\begin{lem}[standard]\label{lem:iter}
For closure operators $\rho,\rho'$ and $x\in L$ define $x_0=x$,
$x_{\alpha+1}=\rho\rho'x_\alpha$ and $x_\lambda=\bigvee_{\alpha<\lambda}
x_\alpha$ at limits. The sequence is increasing, stabilises at some
ordinal, and its value is $(\rho\sqcup\rho')x$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
If $x_{\alpha+1}=x_\alpha=:z$ then $\rho'z\le\rho\rho'z=z$ gives $\rho'z=z$
and then $\rho z=\rho\rho'z=z$, so $z\in\Fix\rho\cap\Fix\rho'$; every common
fixed point above $x$ is above all $x_\alpha$, so $z$ is the least one.
\end{proof}

\begin{defi}
For a closure operator $\rho$ on $L$ put
\[
W_\rho:=\{x\le y:\ \rho x=\rho y\}=\{x\le y:\ y\le\rho x\},
\]
the inequalities collapsed by $\rho$, i.e.\ the refinements $y$ of $x$
that $\rho$ does not distinguish from $x$.
\end{defi}

$W_\rho$ is a sameness on the poset $L$: it contains the identities, is
closed under composition ($x\le y\le z$ with $z\le\rho y\le\rho\rho x=\rho
x$), and under both cancellations ($x\le y\le z$ with $z\le\rho x$ gives
$y\le\rho x$ and $z\le\rho x\le\rho y$). No meet preservation is used. If
$\rho\le\rho'$ then $W_\rho\subseteq W_{\rho'}$: a less precise analysis
collapses more.

\begin{thm}\label{thm:lattice}
Let $\rho,\rho'$ be closure operators on a complete lattice $L$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $W_\rho\cap W_{\rho'}=W_{\rho\sqcap\rho'}$: the reduced product
distinguishes $x\le y$ iff one of the two abstractions does.
\item[(ii)] $W_\rho\vee W_{\rho'}=\{x\le y:\ y\le(\rho\rho')^kx\text{ for
some }k\ge0\}$ in $\Same(L)$; for such a pair, with $z=(\rho\rho')^kx$,
both $x\le z$ and $y\le z$ are alternating chains of steps in $W_{\rho'}$
and $W_\rho$, so every element of the join is obtained from two alternating
chains by one cancellation.
\item[(iii)] $W_\rho\vee W_{\rho'}=W_{\rho\sqcup\rho'}$ iff for every $x\in
L$ there is $k$ with $(\rho\rho')^kx=(\rho\sqcup\rho')x$: the alternation
of Lemma~\ref{lem:iter} stops at a finite stage at every point.
\item[(iv)] $\rho\rho'=\rho\sqcup\rho'$ (one alternation suffices
uniformly) iff $\rho\rho'$ is idempotent iff $\rho$ maps $\Fix\rho'$ into
$\Fix\rho'$.
\item[(v)] The compositional closure of $W_\rho\cup W_{\rho'}$, the class of
alternating chains, can be strictly smaller than the join; it equals the
join when $\rho$ and $\rho'$ preserve binary meets.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
(i) $x\le y$ lies in $W_{\rho\sqcap\rho'}$ iff $y\le\rho x\wedge\rho'x$ iff
$y\le\rho x$ and $y\le\rho'x$.

(ii) Let $D$ be the right-hand side. $D$ is a sameness: if $y\le(\rho
\rho')^kx$ and $z\le(\rho\rho')^ly$ then $z\le(\rho\rho')^{k+l}x$; if
$x\le y\le z$ and $z\le(\rho\rho')^kx$ then $y\le(\rho\rho')^kx$ and
$z\le(\rho\rho')^kx\le(\rho\rho')^ky$. It contains $W_\rho$ and
$W_{\rho'}$ ($y\le\rho x\le\rho\rho'x$, $y\le\rho'x\le\rho\rho'x$). So the
join $J$ of $W_\rho$ and $W_{\rho'}$ is contained in $D$. Conversely let
$x\le y\le z:=(\rho\rho')^kx$, and put $x_0=x$, $x_{2i+1}=\rho'x_{2i}$,
$x_{2i+2}=\rho x_{2i+1}$, so that $x_{2k}=z$. The chain $x=x_0\le
x_1\le\cdots\le x_{2k}=z$ has its steps alternately in $W_{\rho'}$ and
$W_\rho$, so $(x,z)\in J$. The chain $y=y\vee x_0\le y\vee x_1\le\cdots\le
y\vee x_{2k}=z$ (the last equality as $y\le z$) has the same property: if
$x_{j+1}=dx_j$ with $d\in\{\rho,\rho'\}$, then $y\vee x_{j+1}\le d(y\vee
x_j)$, both $y$ and $dx_j$ being below $d(y\vee x_j)$; so $(y,z)\in J$.
Then $(x,y)\in J$ by cancellation from $(x,z)$ and $(y,z)$.

(iii) $W_{\rho\sqcup\rho'}=\{x\le y:y\le(\rho\sqcup\rho')x\}$ contains $D$,
since $(\rho\rho')^kx\le(\rho\sqcup\rho')x$. If every fibre top
$(\rho\sqcup\rho')x$ is some $(\rho\rho')^kx$ the two classes agree;
if for some $x$ no $(\rho\rho')^kx$ equals $(\rho\sqcup\rho')x$, then
$(x,(\rho\sqcup\rho')x)$ lies in $W_{\rho\sqcup\rho'}$ but not in $D$,
as $(\rho\sqcup\rho')x\le(\rho\rho')^kx$ would force equality.

(iv) If $\rho\rho'$ is idempotent and $a\in\Fix\rho'$, then
$\rho'\rho a\le\rho\rho'\rho a=\rho\rho'\rho\rho'a=\rho\rho'a=\rho a$, so
$\rho a\in\Fix\rho'$. If $\rho$ preserves $\Fix\rho'$ then
$\rho\rho'x\in\Fix\rho'$, so $\rho\rho'\rho\rho'x=\rho\rho\rho'x=\rho\rho'x$.
An idempotent $\rho\rho'$ is a closure operator above $\rho$ and $\rho'$ and
below every closure operator above both, so it is $\rho\sqcup\rho'$;
conversely $\rho\sqcup\rho'$ is idempotent.

(v) Example~\ref{ex:square} gives a strict inclusion. If $\rho,\rho'$
preserve binary meets, let $x\le y\le z=x_{2k}$ with the chain $x_j$ of
(ii) and put $u_j=y\wedge x_j$, so $u_0=x$, $u_{2k}=y$ and $u_j\le
u_{j+1}$. If $x_{j+1}=dx_j$ then $u_{j+1}=y\wedge dx_j\le dy\wedge
dx_j=d(y\wedge x_j)=du_j$, so $u_j\le u_{j+1}$ lies in $W_d$ and
$x=u_0\le\cdots\le u_{2k}=y$ is an alternating chain. (When $L$ is a
frame this is the poset case of Theorem~A of \cite{Fijoin}.)
\end{proof}

\begin{ex}\label{ex:square}
Let $L=\{0<1,2<3\}$ be the four-element Boolean lattice, $\rho$ the closure
with $\Fix\rho=\{0,3\}$ ($\rho1=\rho2=3$) and $\rho'$ the closure with
$\Fix\rho'=\{1,3\}$ ($\rho'0=1$, $\rho'2=3$). Then $W_\rho=\{1\le3,2\le3\}$,
$W_{\rho'}=\{0\le1,2\le3\}$ (plus identities), $\rho\rho'0=3$, so
$0\le2$ lies in the join by (ii); but the only non-identity step out of
$0$ in $W_\rho\cup W_{\rho'}$ is $0\le1$, and $1\not\le2$, so $0\le2$ is
not an alternating chain: it is obtained from $0\le3$ and $2\le3$ by
cancellation. Here $\rho(1\wedge2)=0\ne3=\rho1\wedge\rho2$.
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=small,column sep=small]
& 3 & \\
1\arrow[ur,"W_\rho"] & & 2\arrow[ul,"W_\rho\cap W_{\rho'}"'] \\
& 0\arrow[ul,"W_{\rho'}"]\arrow[ur,dashed,"\text{join only}"'] &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\end{ex}

\begin{rem}
In a finite lattice the alternation of Lemma~\ref{lem:iter} stops after
at most $|L|$ steps, so (iii) always holds there and the content of the
theorem is (ii) and (v): the join is generated by alternating chains
\emph{and} cancellation. On an infinite lattice (iii) can fail; the
simplest instance is in Section~\ref{sec:reach}.
\end{rem}

\section{Reachability closures}\label{sec:reach}

Let $X$ be a set of states, $R\subseteq X\times X$ a transition relation
and $I\subseteq X$ a set of initial states. On the complete lattice
$L=\cP(X)$ put
\[
\rho_{R,I}(S):=R^*(S\cup I),
\]
the set of states reachable from $S$ or from $I$ along $R$: the collecting
semantics of ``run $R$ from $S$''.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:reach}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\rho_{R,I}$ is a closure operator on $\cP(X)$ with fixed points
the $R$-closed sets containing $I$ (the inductive invariants); it
preserves binary intersections only in degenerate cases.
\item[(ii)] $\rho_{R,I}\sqcap\rho_{R',I'}$ is $S\mapsto R^*(S\cup I)\cap
R'^*(S\cup I')$ and $\rho_{R,I}\sqcup\rho_{R',I'}=\rho_{R\cup R',I\cup
I'}$; for $k\ge1$, $(\rho_{R,I}\rho_{R',I'})^kS$ is the set of states
reachable from $S\cup I'$ by a path of at most $k$ blocks, each an
$R'$-path followed by an $R$-path, together with the states reachable from
$I$ by an $R$-path followed by at most $k-1$ such blocks (the initial
states of the first system enter only after the $R'$-phase of a block).
\item[(iii)] $W_{\rho_{R,I}}\vee W_{\rho_{R',I'}}$ is the class of pairs
$S\subseteq T$ with $T\subseteq(\rho_{R,I}\rho_{R',I'})^kS$ for some $k$,
i.e.\ every state of $T$ is reachable in the sense of (ii) with a bounded
number of blocks, and it equals
$W_{\rho_{R\cup R',I\cup I'}}$ iff the alternation depth of $(R\cup
R')$-reachability is finite from every $S$. If $X$ is finite this holds
with at most $|X|$ blocks.
\item[(iv)] On $X=\mathbb N$ with $R=\{(2i,2i+1)\}$, $R'=\{(2i+1,2i+2)\}$
and $I=I'=\emptyset$, $(\rho_R\rho_{R'})^k\{0\}=[0,2k-1]$ for $k\ge1$
(the first $R'$-step from $0$ is empty), which never reaches
$\rho_{R\cup R'}\{0\}=\mathbb N$, so $W_{\rho_R}\vee W_{\rho_{R'}}\subsetneq
W_{\rho_{R\cup R'}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
(i) Extensive, monotone and idempotent since $R^*$ is reflexive and
transitive; the fixed points are the sets closed under $R$ and containing
$I$. For $x\xrightarrow{R}z\xleftarrow{R}y$ with $x\ne y$ one has
$z\in\rho\{x\}\cap\rho\{y\}$ but $\rho(\{x\}\cap\{y\})=\rho\emptyset=R^*I$.
(ii) The meet is pointwise; the common fixed points of the two closures
are the sets closed under $R$ and $R'$ containing $I\cup I'$, the fixed
points of $\rho_{R\cup R',I\cup I'}$, which is therefore the join. For the
iterates, $\rho_{R,I}\rho_{R',I'}(A)=R^*R'^*(A\cup I')\cup R^*I$; writing
$B_k(A)$ for the states reachable from $A$ by at most $k$ blocks, so that
$B_1B_k=B_{k+1}$ and $B_k(A)\subseteq B_{k+1}(A)$ as blocks may be empty,
induction on $k$ gives $(\rho_{R,I}\rho_{R',I'})^kS=B_k(S\cup I')\cup
B_{k-1}(R^*I)$. (iii) is Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice}(ii),
(iii); if $X$ is finite, every block that changes the set adds a state,
so the iteration of Lemma~\ref{lem:iter} stops after at most $|X|$ blocks.
(iv) is a direct computation, and Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice}(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
Example~\ref{ex:square} is of this form: $X=\{p,q\}$, $R=\{(p,q),(q,p)\}$,
$I=\emptyset$ and $R'=\emptyset$, $I'=\{p\}$. The pair
$\emptyset\subseteq\{q\}$ is collapsed by the join (from $\emptyset$ the
second system reaches $p$ and the first then $q$) but by no alternating
chain of collapses, the only refinement of $\emptyset$ collapsed by one of
the two systems being $\{p\}\not\subseteq\{q\}$.
\end{rem}

\begin{rem}\label{rem:granger}
The iterated reduction of Granger \cite{Granger} and of Cousot, Cousot and
Mauborgne \cite{CCM11}, which alternately refines the two components of a
pair $(a,b)$ in a product of abstract domains and is the Nelson--Oppen
procedure when the domains are logical theories, computes the reduced
product, the \emph{meet} $\rho\sqcap\rho'$, by a decreasing iteration on
the product domain. The alternation $\rho\rho'\rho\rho'\cdots$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:iter} computes the \emph{join}, the least common
abstraction, by an increasing iteration on $L$. The two are not to be
confused: the first terminates in one step in the sense of (i) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice}, the second is governed by (iii).
\end{rem}

\section{Cut-point topologies on the path category of a graph}\label{sec:cut}

Let $G$ be a directed graph, finite or infinite, with vertex set $V$, and
let $\cP(G)$ be its path category: objects the vertices, morphisms the
finite paths, identities the empty paths. A presheaf $X$ on $\cP(G)$ is a
set $X(c)$ for each vertex and a restriction $X(c)\to X(d)$, $x\mapsto
x\cdot f$, for each path $f\colon d\to c$, a flow of sets along the control
flow; a subpresheaf is a family of subsets closed under restriction. Write
$\cE=\Psh(\cP(G))$, a Grothendieck topos.

For a set $S\subseteq V$ of vertices, cut points, let $D_S$ be the set of
paths that pass through a vertex of $S$, the empty paths at vertices of $S$
included. $D_S$ is a two-sided ideal of $\cP(G)$ (closed under composition
with arbitrary paths on either side) and idempotent, $D_S\circ D_S=D_S$,
because $f=f\circ\mathrm{id}_s$ for a path $f$ through $s\in S$. For a
vertex $c$ let $D_S(c)$ be the sieve of paths into $c$ through $S$.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:JS}
The sieves containing $D_S(c)$, for all $c$, form a Grothendieck topology
$J_S$ on $\cP(G)$. Its sheaves are the presheaves $X$ with $X\cong
\Ran_i(X|_S)$ for the inclusion $i$ of the full subcategory of $\cP(G)$ on
the vertices of $S$, so that restriction gives $\Sh(J_S)\simeq\Psh(S)$,
where $\Psh(S)$ denotes the presheaves on that full subcategory (its
morphisms are all paths of $G$ between vertices of $S$, not only the edges
inside $S$), and its closure operator on
subpresheaves $A\subseteq X$ is
\[
c_S(A)(c)=\{x\in X(c):\ x\cdot f\in A\text{ for every path }f\colon s\to c
\text{ with }s\in S\}.
\]
For two sets $S,T$ one has $c_Sc_T=c_{D_S\circ D_T}$, where
$c_D(A)(c)=\{x:x\cdot f\in A\ \forall f\in D(c)\}$ for an ideal $D$; so
$(c_Sc_T)^k=c_{(D_S\circ D_T)^k}$, and $D_S\circ D_T$ consists of the paths
with a visit to $T$ followed by a visit to $S$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
Stability: the pullback of $D_S(c)$ along $g\colon e\to c$ is $\{h:gh\in
D_S(c)\}\supseteq D_S(e)$. Transitivity: let $R$ be a sieve on $c$ with
$h^*R\supseteq D_S(\mathrm{dom}\,h)$ for all $h\in D_S(c)$; for
$f=u\circ v\in D_S(c)$ with $u\colon s\to c$, $s\in S$, we have $u\in
D_S(c)$ and $\mathrm{id}_s\in D_S(s)\subseteq u^*R$, so $u\in R$ and
$f\in R$. Restriction $\Psh(\cP(G))\to\Psh(S)$ is left exact with right
adjoint $\Ran_i$, and $\Ran_i$ is fully faithful since $i$ is, so
restriction is the inverse image of a geometric embedding; a sieve
$R\hookrightarrow y(c)$ is inverted by restriction iff $R$ contains every
path from a vertex of $S$ into $c$, i.e.\ iff $R\supseteq D_S(c)$, so the
embedding is $\Sh(J_S)$ \cite[A4.3, A4.5]{Elephant}. The closure of $A$
for $J_S$ consists of the $x$ whose sieve $\{f:x\cdot f\in A\}$ is
covering, which is the displayed formula; and $c_Dc_E(A)=\{x:x\cdot f\in
c_E(A)\ \forall f\in D\}=\{x:x\cdot f\cdot g\in A\ \forall f\in D,g\in E\}
=c_{D\circ E}(A)$, with $f\circ g$ the path $g$ followed by $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}\label{lem:alt}
A path $f$ lies in $(D_S\circ D_T)^k$ iff there are vertices
$t_1,s_1,\dots,t_k,s_k$ occurring along $f$ in this order, positions
non-decreasing, with $t_i\in T$ and $s_i\in S$. A path through a vertex of
$S\cap T$ lies in $(D_S\circ D_T)^k$ for every $k$. Hence the chain
$(D_S\circ D_T)^k$ stabilises iff the number of alternations ``a visit to
$T$, then a visit to $S$'' along paths avoiding $S\cap T$ is bounded, and
then $(D_S\circ D_T)^{a+1}=D_{S\cap T}$ for the bound $a$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
$f\in(D_S\circ D_T)^k$ iff $f=u_kv_k\cdots u_1v_1$ with $v_i\in D_T$, $u_i\in
D_S$, the splitting points being arbitrary vertices along $f$ and the
factors possibly empty; this is the stated condition. At a common vertex
$x$ all factors may be taken empty. If the alternation along paths
avoiding $S\cap T$ is bounded by $a$, a path in level $a+1$ passes through
$S\cap T$, and conversely such paths are in every level; if it is
unbounded, the chain drops at infinitely many levels.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}\label{thm:cut}
Let $G$ be a directed graph and $S,T\subseteq V$. In
$\Same(\Psh(\cP(G)))$,
\[
W_{J_S}\vee W_{J_T}=W_{J_S\vee J_T}
\]
iff the number of alternations between visits to $T$ and visits to $S$
along paths of $G$ avoiding $S\cap T$ is bounded, and then $J_S\vee
J_T=J_{S\cap T}$, $\Sh(J_S)\cap\Sh(J_T)\simeq\Psh(S\cap T)$. If $G$ is
finite this holds iff no closed walk of $G$ avoiding $S\cap T$ meets both
$S$ and $T$, i.e.\ iff no strongly connected component of the induced
subgraph $G-(S\cap T)$ contains both a vertex of $S$ and a vertex of $T$;
the bound is then at most $\min(|S\setminus T|,|T\setminus S|)$, and the
condition is decided in time $O(|V|+|E|)$ by a decomposition into strongly
connected components. In particular, for disjoint $S,T$ on a finite graph
the join is preserved iff no strongly connected component of $G$ meets both
$S$ and $T$.
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
Let $c=c_S$, $c'=c_T$ and let $\bar c$ be the least closure operator
above both, the closure operator of the join topology; by
\cite[Thm.~B]{Fijoin}, $W_{J_S}\vee W_{J_T}=W_{\bar c}$ iff $(cc')^k=\bar c$
for some $k$. If the alternation is bounded by $a$, then by
Lemmas~\ref{lem:JS} and~\ref{lem:alt} $(cc')^{a+1}=c_{D_{S\cap T}}$ is
idempotent, hence a closure operator above $c$ and $c'$, and every closure
operator above both is above $cc'$ and so above $(cc')^{a+1}$; thus $\bar
c=(cc')^{a+1}=c_{S\cap T}$, the criterion holds, and the join topology is
$J_{S\cap T}$. If the alternation is unbounded, the chain of ideals
$(D_S\circ D_T)^k$ does not stabilise by Lemma~\ref{lem:alt}, and an ideal
$D$ is determined by $c_D$: for $X=y(c)$, $D(c)$ is the least sieve $A$ on
$c$ with $\mathrm{id}_c\in c_D(A)(c)$. Since $c,c'\le\bar c$ and $\bar c$ is
idempotent, $(cc')^k\le\bar c$ for all $k$ and $c\bar c=c'\bar c=\bar c$.
If $\bar c=(cc')^k$ for some $k$, then $(cc')^m=(cc')^{m-k}\bar c=\bar
c=(cc')^k$ for all $m\ge k$, so $(D_S\circ D_T)^m=(D_S\circ D_T)^k$ for all
$m\ge k$ and the chain stabilises, a contradiction; so the criterion fails.
For finite $G$: if a closed walk avoiding $S\cap T$ meets $s\in S$ and
$t\in T$, going round it $m$ times gives alternation $\ge m$; conversely a
path avoiding $S\cap T$ with alternation $\ge|S\setminus T|+1$ repeats an
$S$-vertex, $s_i=s_j$ with $i<j$, with a $T$-visit $t_{i+1}$ strictly
between (a visit to $S$ and one to $T$ at the same position would be a
vertex of $S\cap T$), and the subwalk from $s_i$ to $s_j$ is a closed walk
avoiding $S\cap T$ meeting $S$ and $T$; likewise with $T\setminus S$ and
the $t_i$. Two vertices lie on a common closed walk of $G-(S\cap T)$ iff
they lie in the same strongly connected component, and the components are
computed in linear time \cite{Tarjan}.
\end{proof}

\begin{ex}\label{ex:graphs}
(a) $G$: $s\to t\to s'$, $S=\{s,s'\}$, $T=\{t\}$. The alternation is at most
one, the join is preserved, and $J_S\vee J_T=J_\emptyset$ is the degenerate
topology: a $J_S$-sheaf has $X(t)\cong X(s)$, a $J_T$-sheaf has
$X(s)=1$ and $X(s')\cong X(t)$, so a common sheaf is terminal.
(b) $G$: the two-cycle $s\rightleftarrows t$, $S=\{s\}$, $T=\{t\}$. Going
round the cycle gives unbounded alternation, and $W_{J_S}\vee
W_{J_T}\subsetneq W_{J_S\vee J_T}$; adding the vertex $s$ to $T$ restores
preservation, as every closed walk then meets $S\cap T=\{s\}$.
(b$'$) $G$: $s\rightleftarrows u\rightleftarrows t$, $S=\{s\}$, $T=\{t\}$.
The simple cycles $s\to u\to s$ and $u\to t\to u$ each meet only one of
the two sets, but the closed walk $s\to u\to t\to u\to s$ meets both,
the alternation is unbounded and the join is not preserved: the condition
of Theorem~\ref{thm:cut} is on closed walks, equivalently on strongly
connected components, not on simple cycles.
(c) $G$ the infinite path $0\to1\to2\to\cdots$, $S$ the even and $T$ the
odd vertices: no cycles, unbounded alternation, and the failure of
\cite{Topjoin}.
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
s\arrow[r] & t\arrow[r] & s'
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
s\arrow[r,bend left] & t\arrow[l,bend left]
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
s\arrow[r,bend left] & u\arrow[l,bend left]\arrow[r,bend left] &
t\arrow[l,bend left]
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
0\arrow[r] & 1\arrow[r] & 2\arrow[r] & \cdots
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\end{ex}

\begin{rem}
A finite acyclic $G$ has a finite path category, and \cite{Finitejoin}
gives preservation for all pairs of topologies on a finite category;
Theorem~\ref{thm:cut} locates the loops that break it. In the terms of
\cite{Bourdoncle}, a set of cut points is a set meeting every cycle; the
theorem concerns two such sets and asks that their common part meet every
closed walk carrying points of both; for one set, meeting every simple
cycle and meeting every closed walk are the same condition, for the pair
they are not (Example~\ref{ex:graphs}(b$'$)). The bound
$\min(|S\setminus T|,|T\setminus S|)$ on the alternation is the number of
mutual reconstructions, from the values at $S$ and from the
values at $T$, after which the reconstruction from $S\cap T$ is reached.
\end{rem}

\begin{rem}
A presheaf on $\cP(G)$ restricts against the direction of the edges,
$X(c)\to X(d)$ for a path $d\to c$, so the flows of
Section~\ref{sec:cut} are the backward ones; forward flows are presheaves
on $\cP(G^{\mathrm{op}})$, the graph with the edges reversed. Closed walks
and strongly connected components are the same for $G$ and
$G^{\mathrm{op}}$, so the criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:cut} and the
bound are the same in both directions.
\end{rem}

\begin{thm}\label{thm:topjoin}
Let $G$ be a directed graph and $S,T\subseteq V$. Say that a path
\emph{avoids} $S\cap T$ if none of its vertices, endpoints included, lies
in $S\cap T$, and call a vertex $d$ \emph{reconstructible} if it lies in
the least set $\mathcal R$ of vertices such that $d\in\mathcal R$ whenever
all vertices of $S$, or all vertices of $T$, joined to $d$ by a path
avoiding $S\cap T$ (the empty path included) lie in $\mathcal R$; so the
vertices of $S\cap T$ and the vertices with no $S$-vertex, or no
$T$-vertex, upstream are reconstructible. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $J_S\vee J_T=J_{S\cap T}$, i.e.\ $\Sh(J_S)\cap\Sh(J_T)=
\Sh(J_{S\cap T})$;
\item[(ii)] every vertex is reconstructible;
\item[(iii)] there is no infinite backward walk $v_0\leftarrow v_1\leftarrow
v_2\leftarrow\cdots$ in $G$ avoiding $S\cap T$ that visits $S$ and $T$
infinitely often.
\end{enumerate}
For finite $G$ they hold iff no strongly connected component of
$G-(S\cap T)$ meets both $S$ and $T$, the condition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cut}: on a finite graph the join topology is $J_{S\cap
T}$ exactly when the join of the two classes is preserved. In general,
bounded alternation implies (i)--(iii), and not conversely.
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
Since $D_{S\cap T}\subseteq D_S,D_T$, always $J_S,J_T\le J_{S\cap T}$, and
(i) says that $D_{S\cap T}(d)$ is a covering sieve of $J_S\vee J_T$ for
every vertex $d$.

(ii)$\Rightarrow$(i), by induction along the stages of the least fixed
point: suppose that all $S$-vertices $s$ joined to $d$ by a path avoiding
$S\cap T$ already have $D_{S\cap T}(s)$ a join-cover (the case of $T$ is
symmetric). $D_S(d)$ is a $J_S$-cover; for $h\in D_S(d)$ write $h=uv$
with $u\colon s\to d$, $s\in S$. If $u$ meets $S\cap T$ then $h\in
D_{S\cap T}(d)$ and $h^*D_{S\cap T}(d)$ is the maximal sieve. Otherwise
$u$ avoids $S\cap T$, $D_{S\cap T}(s)$ is a join-cover, and
$u^*D_{S\cap T}(d)\supseteq D_{S\cap T}(s)$, so $h^*D_{S\cap T}(d)=
v^*u^*D_{S\cap T}(d)$ is a join-cover by stability. By transitivity
$D_{S\cap T}(d)$ is a join-cover.

(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii). Let $B=V\setminus\mathcal R$. As $\mathcal R$ is the
least fixed point, every $d\in B$ has a vertex of $S\cap B$ and a vertex of
$T\cap B$ joined to $d$ by paths avoiding $S\cap T$. Call a path a
\emph{$B$-path} if it starts in $B$ and avoids $S\cap T$, and let $K(d)$
be the set of sieves $R$ on $d$ such that for every $e\in B$ and every
path $f\colon e\to d$ there is a $B$-path $g$ into $e$ with $fg\in R$.
$K$ is a Grothendieck topology: it is upward closed and contains the
maximal sieves; it is stable, since for $h\colon d'\to d$ and $f\colon
e\to d'$ a $B$-path $g$ with $hfg\in R$ gives $fg\in h^*R$; it is
transitive, since if $Q\in K(d)$ and $h^*R\in K(\mathrm{dom}\,h)$ for all
$h\in Q$, then for $f\colon e\to d$ with $e\in B$ there is a $B$-path
$g_1\colon e_1\to e$ with $fg_1\in Q$, and applying $(fg_1)^*R\in K(e_1)$
to the identity of $e_1\in B$ a $B$-path $g_2$ into $e_1$ with
$fg_1g_2\in R$, where $g_1g_2$ is a $B$-path. $K$ contains $J_S$: for
$f\colon e\to d$ with $e\in B$ take $s\in S\cap B$ and a path $g\colon
s\to e$ avoiding $S\cap T$; $fg$ passes through $s$, so $fg\in D_S(d)$.
Likewise $K\supseteq J_T$, so $J_S\vee J_T\le K$. If $d\in B$ then
$D_{S\cap T}(d)\notin K(d)$, because for $f=\mathrm{id}_d$ a $B$-path $g$
avoids $S\cap T$ and is not in $D_{S\cap T}(d)$. So $B\ne\emptyset$
implies $J_{S\cap T}\not\le K$ and (i) fails.

(ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii). $B$ is the greatest set of vertices outside
$S\cap T$ in which every vertex has an $S$-vertex and a $T$-vertex of the
set upstream along paths avoiding $S\cap T$. From $d\in B$, choosing
alternately such a $T$-vertex and such an $S$-vertex produces a walk as in
(iii); conversely the vertices of such a walk form a set with the
property, hence lie in $B$. For finite $G$ a walk as in (iii) visits some
$s\in S$ twice with a visit to $T$ in between, giving a closed walk
avoiding $S\cap T$ through $S$ and $T$, and such a closed walk, traversed
repeatedly, is a walk as in (iii); so the condition is that of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cut}. Finally, bounded alternation gives
$(D_S\circ D_T)^{a+1}=D_{S\cap T}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:alt}, so
$c_{S\cap T}=(c_Sc_T)^{a+1}$ is the least closure operator above $c_S$
and $c_T$ and the join topology is $J_{S\cap T}$; Example~\ref{ex:topjoin}
shows that the converse fails.
\end{proof}

\begin{ex}\label{ex:topjoin}
(a) For the two-cycle of Example~\ref{ex:graphs}(b) the comma category
of paths from $s$ into $t$ has the edge $s\to t$ as terminal object, so a
$J_S$-sheaf is a presheaf whose restriction along $s\to t$ is a bijection,
a $J_T$-sheaf one whose restriction along $t\to s$ is a bijection, and the
common sheaves are the pairs of bijections between two sets, not only the
terminal presheaf of $\Sh(J_\emptyset)$: $J_S\vee J_T\ne J_{S\cap T}$,
as no vertex is reconstructible. (b) On the infinite path
$0\to1\to2\to\cdots$ with $S$ the even and $T$ the odd vertices every
vertex is reconstructible, by induction along the path, so $J_S\vee
J_T=J_\emptyset$ is degenerate, as computed in \cite{Topjoin}, although
the alternation is unbounded and the join of the two classes is not
preserved. (c) On the reversed path $\cdots\to2\to1\to0$ with the same
$S,T$ no vertex is reconstructible, and the common sheaves are the
sequences of bijections $X(0)\cong X(1)\cong X(2)\cong\cdots$.
\end{ex}

\begin{rem}
Theorems~\ref{thm:cut} and~\ref{thm:topjoin} separate two questions that
coincide on finite graphs: whether the join of the two topologies is the
topology of $S\cap T$, a condition on infinite backward walks, and
whether the join of the two classes of inverted maps is the class of the
join topology, a uniform bound on finite paths. The first is a
co-inductive condition (the greatest set $B$ of the proof), the second
an inductive one.
\end{rem}

\subsection*{Iterated reconstruction.}
For a presheaf $X$ the $J_S$-sheafification $a_SX=\Ran_i(X|_S)$ is the
reconstruction of $X$ from its values at $S$: $(a_SX)(c)$ is the set of
compatible families $(x_u)_{u\colon s\to c,\ s\in S}$ with $x_u\in X(s)$
and $x_{ug}=x_u\cdot g$ for every path $g$ between vertices of $S$, and
the unit sends $x\in X(c)$ to $(x\cdot u)_u$. Alternating the
reconstructions from $T$ and from $S$ gives the tower $X\to a_Sa_TX\to
(a_Sa_T)^2X\to\cdots$. The question of \cite{Fijoin}, whether the
sheafification for the join is a finite composite of the two
sheafifications and in how many steps, has here a complete answer.

The computation of the iterates rests on a relabelling of compatible
families, which we isolate.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:relabel}
Let $\mathcal K$ be a small category, $E$ and $F$ sets of objects, and
$X$ a presheaf on $\mathcal K$. Let $L'$ be a set of morphisms with
domains in $F$, closed under postcomposition with arbitrary morphisms
and under precomposition with morphisms between objects of $F$, and
write $\lim_{L'}X$ for the presheaf
\begin{multline*}
c\mapsto\{(z_w)_{w\in L'(c)}:\ z_w\in X(\mathrm{dom}\,w),\\
z_{wh}=z_w\cdot h\text{ for all }h\text{ between objects of }F\},
\end{multline*}
$L'(c)$ being the members with codomain $c$, with restriction along
$f\colon d\to c$ given by $(z_w)_w\mapsto(z_{fw'})_{w'}$. Let $L$ be the
set of composites $uw$ with $u$ of domain in $E$ and $w\in L'$, and
suppose that for every $W\in L$ the factorisations $W=uw$ of this form
are connected under the relation $(ug,w')\sim(u,gw')$, $g$ a morphism
between objects of $E$. Then $a_E(\lim_{L'}X)\cong\lim_LX$, naturally in
$X$ and compatibly with the restriction maps, by $(y_u)_u\mapsto(z_W)_W$
with $z_{uw}=(y_u)_w$, the inverse sending $(z_W)_W$ to
$y_u=(z_{uw})_{w}$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
$a_E$ is the right Kan extension along $A_E\hookrightarrow\mathcal K$,
so for $Y=\lim_{L'}X$ an element of $(a_EY)(c)$ is a family
$(y_u)_{u\colon e\to c,\ e\in E}$ with $y_u\in Y(e)$ and $y_{ug}=y_u\cdot
g$ for $g\colon e'\to e$ between objects of $E$; writing
$y_u=(z^u_w)_{w\in L'(e)}$ the last condition reads
$z^{ug}_{w'}=z^u_{gw'}$. Hence $z^u_w$ depends only on the class of
$(u,w)$ under $\sim$, which by hypothesis is the fibre over $W=uw$, and
$z_W:=z^u_w$ is well defined; it satisfies $z_{Wh}=z^u_{wh}=z^u_w\cdot
h=z_W\cdot h$, so $(z_W)_W\in(\lim_LX)(c)$. Conversely $(z_W)_W$ defines
$y_u:=(z_{uw})_w$, which lies in $Y(e)$ since $z_{uwh}=z_{uw}\cdot h$,
and satisfies $(y_{ug})_{w'}=z_{ugw'}=(y_u\cdot g)_{w'}$. The two
assignments are inverse to each other, natural in $X$, and commute with
the restriction maps, which act by precomposition on both sides.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}\label{thm:iter}
Let $S,T\subseteq V$ and $k\ge1$. For a vertex $c$ let $L_k(c)$ be the
set of paths $w\colon t\to c$ with $t\in T$ along which there are
vertices $t=t_1,s_1,t_2,s_2,\dots,t_k,s_k$ in this order, positions
non-decreasing, with $t_i\in T$ and $s_i\in S$, and let $L'_k(c)$ be the
set of those with a further vertex $t_{k+1}\in T$ at or after $s_k$.
Then, naturally in $X$,
\begin{multline*}
((a_Sa_T)^kX)(c)\cong\{(z_w)_{w\in L_k(c)}:\ z_w\in X(t)\text{ for }
w\colon t\to c,\\
z_{wg}=z_w\cdot g\text{ for all }g\colon t'\to t\text{ with }t'\in T\},
\end{multline*}
the compatible families of values at $T$-vertices along the paths with
$k$ alternations, and $(a_T(a_Sa_T)^kX)(c)$ is the same set with
$L'_k(c)$ in place of $L_k(c)$. If the alternation along paths avoiding
$S\cap T$ is bounded by $a$, then $(a_Sa_T)^{a+1}X\cong a_{S\cap T}X$,
the sheafification for the join topology, naturally in $X$ and
compatibly with the units: $a+1$ rounds of alternating reconstruction
from $T$ and from $S$ reconstruct from $S\cap T$. If some path avoiding
$S\cap T$ has $a$ alternations, as is the case when $a$ is the least
bound, then $(a_Sa_T)^a$ is not naturally isomorphic to $a_{S\cap T}$:
it does not invert the $J_{S\cap T}$-dense monomorphism $D_{S\cap
T}(c)\hookrightarrow y(c)$ at the end vertex $c$ of such a path. So,
unless $S=T=V$, the least number of rounds after which the alternating
reconstruction is the join sheafification is exactly $a+1$, the number
of iterations of $c_Sc_T$ needed to reach the closure operator of the
join (Lemma~\ref{lem:alt}).
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
The sets $L_k$ and $L'_k$ are closed under postcomposition with
arbitrary paths and under precomposition with paths between
$T$-vertices, and in the notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:relabel} the
displayed set is $(\lim_{L_k}X)(c)$. With $L'_0(c)$ the set of all paths
from $T$-vertices into $c$, $\lim_{L'_0}X=a_TX$ by definition of the
right Kan extension. The composites $uw$ with $u$ from an $S$-vertex and
$w\in L'_k$ are exactly the paths of $L_{k+1}$, and the composites $uw$
with $u$ from a $T$-vertex and $w\in L_k$ are exactly the paths of
$L'_k$; the factorisations of a path $W$ of this form are the positions
along $W$ of the $S$-vertices (resp.\ $T$-vertices) at or after the
required pattern, and two consecutive positions $s',s$ give
factorisations $W=u'w'=uw$ with $u'=ug$, $w=gw'$ for the segment
$g\colon s'\to s$ of $W$, so the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:relabel}
holds. The lemma gives $a_S\lim_{L'_k}X\cong\lim_{L_{k+1}}X$ and
$a_T\lim_{L_k}X\cong\lim_{L'_k}X$, and induction on $k$ gives the first
two claims; the bijections are compatible with the units, which send
$x\in X(c)$ to $(x\cdot W)_W$ on both sides.

If the alternation along paths avoiding $S\cap T$ is bounded by $a$,
every path of $L_{a+1}(c)$ passes through $S\cap T$, and every path from
a vertex of $S\cap T$ lies in $L_{a+1}(c)$ (all $t_i,s_i$ at that
vertex). Restriction of families is a map $\lim_{L_{a+1}(c)}X\to
(a_{S\cap T}X)(c)$, the latter being the families over the paths from
$S\cap T$. It is bijective: given a family $(x_v)$ over the paths $v$
from $S\cap T$ into $c$, a path $W\in L_{a+1}(c)$ factors as $W=vg$ with
$v$ from a vertex $x\in S\cap T$ on $W$ and $g$ into $x$, and
$z_W:=x_v\cdot g$ does not depend on the choice of $x$, since for a
later vertex $x'$ of $S\cap T$ on $W$, with $v=v'h$, $x_v=x_{v'}\cdot h$;
this $z$ is compatible and restricts to $x$, and it is the only such
extension. The join topology is $J_{S\cap T}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cut}), so $a_{S\cap T}$ is its sheafification, and the
composite of the units $X\to(a_Sa_T)^{a+1}X$, $x\mapsto(x\cdot W)_W$,
restricts to the unit $x\mapsto(x\cdot v)_v$ of $a_{S\cap T}$.

For the last claim let $w\colon t\to c$ avoid $S\cap T$ and have $a$
alternations, and let $A=D_{S\cap T}(c)\hookrightarrow y(c)$, the least
$J_{S\cap T}$-covering sieve on $c$, which $a_{S\cap T}$ inverts. If
$a=0$ then $(a_Sa_T)^0$ is the identity and $A\ne y(c)$ as $c\notin
S\cap T$. If $a\ge1$, $(a_Sa_T)^aA\to(a_Sa_T)^ay(c)$ is, by the first
part, the inclusion of the families $(z_v)_{v\in L_a(c)}$ with $z_v\in
A(t)$ into those with $z_v\in y(c)(t)$; the family $z_v=v$, the image of
$\mathrm{id}_c$ under the unit, is not in the first set, because $w\in
L_a(c)$ avoids $S\cap T$ and so $w\notin A(t)$. Thus $(a_Sa_T)^a$ does
not invert $A\hookrightarrow y(c)$, and a natural isomorphism
$(a_Sa_T)^a\cong a_{S\cap T}$ is impossible. If $S=T=V$ both
sheafifications are the identity and no round is needed; otherwise some
vertex is outside $S\cap T$, the least bound $a$ is attained by some
path, and the least number of rounds is $a+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:iter} contrasts with the commuting pairs of
\cite{Fijoin}, where the closure operators compose in one step,
$cc'=\bar c$, but $a_ca_{c'}$ is not the join sheafification. For
cut-point topologies the sheafification tower and the closure tower have
the same length. In the language of program analysis: summarising a flow
at the cut points $T$, then at $S$, and repeating, yields after $a+1$
rounds the summary at the common cut points, where $a$ is the maximal
number of alternations along the control flow outside $S\cap T$; on a
finite graph satisfying the condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:cut},
$a\le\min(|S\setminus T|,|T\setminus S|)$.
\end{rem}

\begin{ex}\label{ex:loops}
Let $G$ be the control-flow graph of two nested loops: an entry $e$, the
outer header $h_1$, the inner header $h_2$, the inner body $b$, the inner
exit $x$ and a final vertex $z$, with edges $e\to h_1$, $h_1\to h_2$,
$h_2\to b$, $b\to h_2$, $h_2\to x$, $x\to h_1$ and $x\to z$.
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
e\arrow[r] & h_1\arrow[r] & h_2\arrow[r]\arrow[d,bend left] &
x\arrow[r]\arrow[ll,bend right=40] & z\\
& & b\arrow[u,bend left] &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The set $S=\{h_1,h_2\}$ of loop headers is a cut set in the sense of
\cite{Bourdoncle}; let $T=\{h_2,x\}$, so $S\cap T=\{h_2\}$. Every closed
walk passes through $h_2$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:cut} applies: a flow
that is determined by its values at the headers and also by its values
at $\{h_2,x\}$ is determined by its value at $h_2$, and the join of the
two classes of inverted maps is the class of maps bijective at $h_2$.
The paths avoiding $h_2$ are $e\to h_1$, $x\to h_1$, $x\to z$ and the
empty ones, with at most one alternation ($x\in T$ then $h_1\in S$), so
$a=1$ and, by Theorem~\ref{thm:iter}, two rounds of reconstruction from
$\{h_2,x\}$ and then from the headers reconstruct from $h_2$, while one
round does not. With $T=\{x\}$ instead, the outer closed walk $h_1\to
h_2\to x\to h_1$ meets $S$ and $T$ and avoids $S\cap T=\emptyset$: the
join of the two classes is not the class of the join topology, and no
finite number of rounds of alternating reconstruction is the join
sheafification compatibly with the units, since the pullback of
$(a_Sa_T)^nA\subseteq(a_Sa_T)^nX$ along the unit is $(c_Sc_T)^n(A)$ and
the operators $(c_Sc_T)^n$ never reach the closure operator of the join
(proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cut}).
\end{ex}

\section{Idempotents, the Karoubi envelope and transfer functions}\label{sec:kar}

The cut-point topologies of Section~\ref{sec:cut} are generated by
objects. This section places them in the general setting of ideal
topologies, through the Karoubi envelope, and extends the three theorems
to flows with idempotent transfer functions.

\subsection{Ideal topologies as cut-point topologies.}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a small category. A two-sided ideal $D$ of
$\mathcal C$ is a set of morphisms closed under composition with
arbitrary morphisms on either side; it is idempotent if $D=D\circ D$.
For an idempotent ideal $D$ the sieves containing $D(c)=\{f\in D$ with
codomain $c\}$ form a Grothendieck topology $J_D$, the \emph{ideal
topology} of $D$, with closure operator $c_D(A)=\{x:x\cdot f\in A\
\forall f\in D\}$; conversely a topology in which every object has a
least covering sieve $D(c)$ arises in this way, these are the topologies
of the essential subtoposes of Kelly and Lawvere \cite{KL89}, and on a
finite category every topology is of this form
\cite{KL89,Marques25}. For two idempotent ideals $D,D'$ the criterion
of \cite[Thm.~B]{Fijoin} is the stabilisation of the chain
$(DD')^k$ \cite[Cor.~1.3]{Stabjoin}, and $c_Dc_{D'}=c_{DD'}$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:JS}. For a set $E$ of idempotents of $\mathcal C$
(identities included) let $D_E$ be the ideal of morphisms factoring
through a member of $E$; it is idempotent, since $f=ue\,v=(ue)(ev)$. The
Karoubi envelope $\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C)$ has as objects the pairs
$(c,e)$ with $e$ an idempotent on $c$ and as morphisms $(c,e)\to(c',e')$
the $f\colon c\to c'$ with $e'fe=f$; the functor $c\mapsto(c,\mathrm{id}_c)$
is fully faithful and induces an equivalence
$\Psh(\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C))\simeq\Psh(\mathcal C)$, a presheaf $X$
extending by $X(c,e)=\{x\in X(c):x\cdot e=x\}$. Let $A_E$ be the full
subcategory of $\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C)$ on the objects $(c,e)$, $e\in E$.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:kar}
$\Sh(J_{D_E})\simeq\Psh(A_E)$, by $X\mapsto\big((c,e)\mapsto\{x\in
X(c):x\cdot e=x\}\big)$, with inverse the right Kan extension along
$A_E\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C)$. The ideal topologies
generated by idempotents are thus the cut-point topologies of
Section~\ref{sec:cut} on the Karoubi envelope, and $W_{J_{D_E}}$ is the
class of maps $f$ with $f_c$ bijective on the fixed points of every
$e\in E$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
Restriction $\Psh(\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C))\to\Psh(A_E)$ is left exact
with fully faithful right adjoint, so it is the inverse image of a
subtopos, whose topology on $\mathcal C$ has as covering sieves on $c$
the sieves $R\hookrightarrow y(c)$ inverted by restriction
\cite[A4.3, A4.5]{Elephant}. The extension of $R$ to $(c',e')$ is
$\{f\in R(c'):f=fe'\}$, so $R$ is inverted iff $R$ contains every
$f\colon c'\to c$ with $f=fe'$, $e'\in E$; these generate the sieve
$D_E(c)$, as $f=ue'v=(ue')v$ with $ue'=(ue')e'$. The last statement is
the description of the maps inverted by restriction.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}\label{rem:finite}
In a finite category every idempotent ideal is generated by the
idempotents it contains \cite[\S2]{Finitejoin}, by the pigeonhole
argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:idem} below, and every topology is an ideal
topology; so every subtopos of the presheaf topos of a finite category is
the presheaf topos of a full subcategory of its Karoubi envelope, the
\emph{gross} objects $(c,e)$ with $e\in D$ in the terminology of
\cite{Friedman}, where this is proved for finite categories without
non-identity endomorphisms, for which $\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C)\simeq
\mathcal C$; in the language of \cite{Marques25} every topology on a
finite Cauchy complete category is rigid. The join of two such topologies
is the ideal topology of the idempotents of $D_E\cap D_F$
\cite[\S2]{Finitejoin}, and the join of the classes is always preserved
\cite{Finitejoin,Stabjoin}, the chain $(D_ED_F)^k$ being a decreasing
chain in a finite set. The content of Section~\ref{sec:cut} is what
happens on infinite categories, where the chain need not stabilise, and
the identification of the join topology and of the number of rounds in
the free case.
\end{rem}

\subsection{Flows with idempotent transfer functions.}
Let $G$ be a directed graph and $\Lambda\subseteq V$ a set of vertices,
and let $\mathcal C_\Lambda$ be the quotient of the path category of $G$
with one additional loop $\varepsilon_v$ at each $v\in\Lambda$ by the
relations $\varepsilon_v\varepsilon_v=\varepsilon_v$. A presheaf on
$\mathcal C_\Lambda$ is a flow on $G$ with an idempotent transfer function
$X(\varepsilon_v)$ at each $v\in\Lambda$, a normalisation or a stuttering
step; the value $X(v,\varepsilon_v)=\{x\in X(v):x\cdot\varepsilon_v=x\}$
of the extension to the Karoubi envelope is the set of normalised states.
The \emph{marks} are the vertices $v$ and the loops $\varepsilon_v$, the
objects $(v,\mathrm{id})$ and $(v,\varepsilon_v)$ of
$\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C_\Lambda)$; let $\mathcal K$ be the full
subcategory of $\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C_\Lambda)$ on the marks. For sets
$E,F$ of marks let $E\wedge F$ be the set of marks $m$ with
$\mathrm{id}_m\in D_E\cap D_F$. Let $G_\Lambda$ be the graph obtained
from $G$ by adding, for each $v\in\Lambda$, a vertex $v'$ and edges
$i_v\colon v'\to v$, $p_v\colon v\to v'$.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:words}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Every morphism of $\mathcal C_\Lambda$ has a unique
\emph{reduced} word, a word in the edges of $G$ and the letters
$\varepsilon_v$ with no two consecutive equal letters $\varepsilon_v$,
and the reduced word of a composite is the concatenation of the reduced
words with one merging of two equal adjacent letters $\varepsilon_v$ if
they occur at the junction. The \emph{mark sequence} of a morphism lists
the vertices visited by its reduced word and, at each letter
$\varepsilon_v$, the mark $\varepsilon_v$ between two occurrences of $v$.
\item[(b)] The idempotents of $\mathcal C_\Lambda$ are the identities and
the loops $\varepsilon_v$.
\item[(c)] A morphism factors through the mark $v$ iff its mark sequence
contains $v$, and through $\varepsilon_v$ iff it contains
$\varepsilon_v$. Hence $\mathrm{id}_v\in D_E$ iff $v\in E$, and
$\varepsilon_v\in D_E$ iff $v\in E$ or $\varepsilon_v\in E$, so that
$E\wedge F$ consists of the vertices of $E\cap F$ and the loops
$\varepsilon_v$ such that $\{v,\varepsilon_v\}$ meets both $E$ and $F$;
$D_{E\wedge F}$ is the ideal generated by the idempotents of $D_E\cap
D_F$.
\item[(d)] $\mathcal K$ is the quotient of the path category of
$G_\Lambda$ by the relations $p_vi_v=\mathrm{id}_{v'}$, the vertex $v'$
being the mark $\varepsilon_v$ (a retract of $v$, with $i_vp_v=
\varepsilon_v$); its morphisms $m\to m'$ are the paths of $G_\Lambda$
from $m$ to $m'$ containing no subpath $v'\to v\to v'$, with composition
concatenation followed by the deletion of such subpaths; for a morphism
between vertices the vertex sequence of its normal form is the mark
sequence of (a). A morphism of $\mathcal K$ factors through the mark
$v'$ iff its vertex sequence contains $v'$, and through the mark $v$ iff
it contains $v$ or $v'$, since $v'$ is a retract of $v$: the
factorisations $f=uw$ of $f$ through $v'$ are the positions of $v'$ in
its vertex sequence, and the factorisations through $v$ are the
positions of $v$ together with the positions of $v'$, a position
$A\to v'\to B$ giving the factorisation $f=(p_vB)\circ(Ai_v)$ through
$p_vi_v=\mathrm{id}_{v'}$, which is hidden in the normal form; in
particular $\mathrm{id}_{v'}$ factors through $v$ exactly once.
Formally, list the \emph{positions} of a morphism with normal form
$x_0\to x_1\to\cdots\to x_n$ as the marks $x_j$ in order, each
$x_j=v'$ preceded by the hidden mark $v$; the factorisation at the mark
$x_j$ splits the path at $x_j$, and the factorisation at the hidden mark
before $x_j=v'$ is $f=(p_vB)\circ(Ai_v)$ with $A$ the path up to $x_j$
and $B$ the path from $x_j$. For two consecutive positions with marks
$m',m$ in this order the \emph{segment} $g\colon m'\to m$ is the
morphism of $\mathcal K$ represented by the path between them, namely
the subpath of the normal form between two marks $x_j,x_{j'}$, the loop
$i_vp_v=\varepsilon_v$ from a mark $x_j=v$ to the hidden mark before
$x_{j+1}=v'$, and $p_v$ from the hidden mark $v$ to the mark $v'$ at the
same $x_j$ (then $i_v$ from $x_j=v'$ to $x_{j+1}=v$). The two
factorisations $W=u'w'$ at $m'$ and $W=uw$ at $m$ satisfy $u'=ug$,
$w=gw'$. $\Psh(\mathcal K)\simeq\Psh(\mathcal
C_\Lambda)$, and under this equivalence $J_E$ is the cut-point topology
of the set $E$ of objects of $\mathcal K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
(a) The rewriting rule $\varepsilon_v\varepsilon_v\to\varepsilon_v$
shortens words and its only overlaps are the words
$\varepsilon_v\varepsilon_v\varepsilon_v$, which reduce to
$\varepsilon_v$ either way, so it is terminating and confluent and
normal forms are unique; the reduced word of a composite is as stated
because the only possible new redex is at the junction. (b) If $f$ is a
non-identity endomorphism with reduced word of length $\ell\ge1$, the
reduced word of $ff$ has length $2\ell$ or $2\ell-1$, so $ff=f$ forces
$\ell=1$ and the letter to be some $\varepsilon_v$; edges of $G$ that are
loops satisfy no relation. (c) A factorisation $f=u\,\mathrm{id}_v\,w$
or $f=u\varepsilon_vw$ puts $v$, resp.\ $\varepsilon_v$, in the mark
sequence of $f$ by (a), and conversely a mark in the sequence gives a
factorisation by splitting the reduced word there, at a letter
$\varepsilon_v$ into the prefix ending and the suffix beginning with
that letter, whose composite is $f$ by merging. The identity $\mathrm
{id}_v$ has mark sequence $v$ and $\varepsilon_v$ has mark sequence
$v,\varepsilon_v,v$, which gives the membership statements, and with (b)
the description of $E\wedge F$; the ideal generated by the idempotents
of $D_E\cap D_F$ is $D_{E\wedge F}$ by definition. (d) A morphism
$(v,\varepsilon_v)\to(c,\mathrm{id})$ of the Karoubi envelope is a
morphism $f\colon v\to c$ with $f\varepsilon_v=f$, i.e.\ a reduced word
beginning with $\varepsilon_v$, and dually for morphisms into
$(v,\varepsilon_v)$; the letter $\varepsilon_v$ in a reduced word, read
as $i_vp_v$, and the identity of $(v,\varepsilon_v)$, read as
$p_vi_v$, give the stated presentation, in which the normal forms are the
paths without the subpath $v'\to v\to v'$ (the rule $v'\to v\to v'\ \to\
\mathrm{id}_{v'}$ is again terminating and confluent). Let $f=uw$ be a
factorisation through $v$, with normal forms $P_w$ ending and $P_u$
beginning at $v$. Either no deletion occurs at the junction of $P_wP_u$,
and the junction is a position of $v$ in the normal form of $f$; or
$P_w$ ends with $i_v$ and $P_u$ begins with $p_v$, the subpath $v'\to
v\to v'$ at the junction is deleted, and the junction becomes a position
of $v'$ in the normal form of $f$, with $w=Ai_v$, $u=p_vB$. Conversely
every position of $v$ or $v'$ gives a factorisation of this form,
including the end positions $x_0$ and $x_n$, which give $(u,w)=(f,
\mathrm{id})$ and $(\mathrm{id},f)$, and the hidden position before
$x_0=v'$, which gives $u=p_vf$, $w=i_v$ (for $f=\mathrm{id}_{v'}$ this is
$\mathrm{id}_{v'}=p_vi_v$); distinct positions give distinct
factorisations, the position being recovered from the number of edges
of the normal form of $w$ (the mark $x_j=v'$, the hidden mark before it
and the mark $x_{j-1}=v$ give $w$ of lengths $\ell$, $\ell+1$ and
$\ell-1$, where $\ell$ is the length of the path up to $x_j$). For a factorisation
through $v'$ the normal forms are joined at $v'$, where no deletion can
occur, since $P_w$ ends with $p_v$ and $P_u$ begins with $i_v$. The
factorisations are thus indexed by the positions. For the segments, let
$x_j=v$ be followed by $x_{j+1}=v'$, so that the normal form is
$A'p_vB'$ with $A'$ ending at $x_j$: the factorisation at $x_j$ is
$(u',w')=(p_vB',A')$ and the hidden one before $x_{j+1}$ is
$(u,w)=(p_vB',A'p_vi_v)$ (as $B'$ begins with $i_v$ or is empty), so
$w=\varepsilon_vw'$ and $u'=u\varepsilon_v$, the latter by deleting
$v'\to v\to v'$; the factorisation at the mark $x_{j+1}=v'$ is
$(u'',w'')=(B',A'p_v)$, with $w''=p_vw$ and $u=u''p_v$; and from
$x_{j+1}=v'$ to $x_{j+2}=v$, with $B'=i_vB''$, the factorisation at
$x_{j+2}$ is $(B'',A'p_vi_v)$, related by $i_v$. Between two marks
$x_j,x_{j'}$ with no $v'$ in between the subpath relates the two
factorisations directly.
$\mathcal C_\Lambda\to\mathcal K\to\mathrm{Kar}(\mathcal C_\Lambda)$ are
fully faithful and the composite induces an equivalence of presheaf
categories, hence so does each; Lemma~\ref{lem:kar} identifies $J_E$ with
the cut-point topology of $E$ on $\mathcal K$, whose least covering sieve
at $m$ consists of the morphisms into $m$ factoring through $E$.
\end{proof}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:words}(d), the lattice of topologies on $\mathcal
C_\Lambda$ is that of $\mathcal K$, and $J_E\vee J_F$ may be computed on
$\mathcal K$, whose objects are the marks. For a morphism of $\mathcal K$ let its
\emph{mark sequence} be the vertex sequence of its normal form, in which
an occurrence of $v'$ counts as an occurrence of $v'$ and of $v$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:words}(d)); for a morphism between vertices this is the
mark sequence of Lemma~\ref{lem:words}(a). The \emph{alternation} of a
morphism is the largest $k$ such that its mark sequence contains marks
$t_1,s_1,\dots,t_k,s_k$ in this order, positions non-decreasing, with
$t_i\in F$, $s_i\in E$, a mark of $E\wedge F$ counting as $t_i$ and
$s_i$ for all $i$ ($k=\infty$).

\begin{thm}\label{thm:transfer}
Let $E,F$ be sets of marks of $\mathcal C_\Lambda$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] A morphism lies in $(D_E\circ D_F)^k$ iff its alternation is
at least $k$. The chain $(D_E\circ D_F)^k$ stabilises iff the
alternation of the morphisms with no mark in $E\wedge F$ is bounded,
and then $(D_E\circ D_F)^{a+1}=D_{E\wedge F}$ for the bound $a$.
\item[(ii)] $W_{J_E}\vee W_{J_F}=W_{J_E\vee J_F}$ iff the alternation of
the morphisms with no mark in $E\wedge F$ is bounded, and then $J_E\vee
J_F=J_{E\wedge F}$ and, for the bound $a$, $(a_Ea_F)^{a+1}\cong
a_{E\wedge F}$ naturally and compatibly with the units, $a+1$ being the
least such number unless $a_E=a_F=\mathrm{id}$. For finite $G$ the
condition is that no closed walk of $G_\Lambda$ avoiding the marks of
$E\wedge F$ meets $E$ and $F$, i.e.\ no strongly connected component of
$G_\Lambda-(E\wedge F)$ meets both, decidable in linear time, and then
$a\le\min(|E\setminus F|,|F\setminus E|)$.
\item[(iii)] $J_E\vee J_F=J_{E\wedge F}$ iff every mark is reconstructible,
i.e.\ lies in the least set $\mathcal R$ of marks such that $m\in\mathcal
R$ whenever all marks of $E$, or all marks of $F$, joined to $m$ by a
morphism with no mark in $E\wedge F$ lie in $\mathcal R$; iff there is
no infinite backward walk in $G_\Lambda$ avoiding $E\wedge F$ visiting
$E$ and $F$ infinitely often.
\end{enumerate}
In particular a loop $\varepsilon_v$ in one of the sets whose vertex $v$
is in the other lies in $E\wedge F$ and behaves as a common cut point:
the cycle $v\to v'\to v$ of $G_\Lambda$ never obstructs the preservation
of the join, although in the path category of $G_\Lambda$ it would, by
Example~\ref{ex:graphs}(b).
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
(i) If $f=u_kv_k\cdots u_1v_1$ with $v_i\in D_F$, $u_i\in D_E$, then by
Lemma~\ref{lem:words}(a) the mark sequence of $f$ is the concatenation
of those of the factors with consecutive equal marks merged at the
junctions, which keeps, for each $i$, an $F$-mark of $v_i$ at a position
not later than an $E$-mark of $u_i$ (the positions may coincide after a
merging, which the convention of non-decreasing positions allows); so
the alternation of $f$ is at least $k$.
Conversely, if the mark sequence of $f$ contains $t_1,s_1,\dots,t_k,s_k$,
split the reduced word at these marks as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:words}(c): the pieces alternately contain an $F$-mark and
an $E$-mark, and compose to $f$. A morphism with a mark $m\in E\wedge F$
lies in every level: $f=u\,\mathrm{id}_m\,w$ with $\mathrm{id}_m\in
D_E\cap D_F$, and $\mathrm{id}_m=\mathrm{id}_m^{\,2k}\in(D_ED_F)^k$; for
$m=\varepsilon_v$ with $v\in E$, $\varepsilon_v\in F$, this is
$\varepsilon_v=\varepsilon_v^{\,k}$ with each factor in $D_F$ and the
identity of $v$ between the factors in $D_E$. For the chain: if the
alternation of the morphisms without marks in $E\wedge F$ is bounded by
$a$, every morphism of level $a+1$ has a mark in $E\wedge F$, so level
$a+1$ is $D_{E\wedge F}$, which lies in every level; if it is unbounded,
for every $k$ there is a morphism of alternation exactly $k$ (truncate
the reduced word of a morphism of alternation $\ge k$ at the first
position where the alternation of the prefix reaches $k$: the
alternation of a prefix grows by at most one at each new mark), and the
chain drops at every level.

(ii) By Lemma~\ref{lem:words}(d) and Lemma~\ref{lem:kar}, $J_E$ and
$J_F$ are the ideal topologies of $D_E$ and $D_F$, $c_Ec_F=c_{D_ED_F}$,
and an ideal $D$ is determined by $c_D$, as in Section~\ref{sec:cut}. The
criterion of \cite[Thm.~B]{Fijoin} is the stabilisation of the chain,
which is the bounded alternation by (i), and then $c_{E\wedge F}=
(c_Ec_F)^{a+1}$ is the least closure operator above $c_E$ and $c_F$,
so $J_E\vee J_F=J_{E\wedge F}$, exactly as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cut}. For finite $G$: a morphism without marks in
$E\wedge F$ of alternation $\ge|E\setminus F|+1$ repeats a mark of
$E\setminus F$ with a mark of $F$ strictly between, and its reduced word
between the two occurrences is a closed walk of $G_\Lambda$ avoiding
$E\wedge F$ meeting $E$ and $F$; conversely, if such a closed walk $W$
exists, the morphism represented by $W^m$ has a mark sequence obtained
from that of $W^m$ by merging consecutive equal marks, which does not
reduce the alternation, since a merged mark is not in $E\wedge F$ and
so serves as at most one of $t_i,s_i$; so the alternation is unbounded.
Two marks lie on a common closed walk of $G_\Lambda-(E\wedge F)$ iff
they lie in the same strongly connected component. For the rounds,
apply Lemma~\ref{lem:relabel} on $\mathcal K$ with $L'_k$ (resp.\
$L_k$) the set of morphisms from marks of $F$ into marks whose mark
sequence contains the pattern $t_1,s_1,\dots,t_k,s_k,t_{k+1}$ (resp.\
$t_1,s_1,\dots,t_k,s_k$), which are closed under postcomposition and
under precomposition with morphisms between marks of $F$ (the mark
sequence of a composite contains those of the factors up to a merging
at the junction or the deletion of $v'\to v\to v'$ there, which leaves
$v'$, counting as $v$; so the pattern persists, and in the second case
the domain stays in $F$): the
composites $uw$ with $u$ from a mark of $E$ and $w\in L'_k$ are the
morphisms of $L_{k+1}$, their factorisations are the positions of marks
of $E$ at or after $t_{k+1}$, the hidden ones included, and consecutive
positions are related by the segments of Lemma~\ref{lem:words}(d), so
the hypothesis of the lemma holds; symmetrically for $a_F$. Hence
$((a_Ea_F)^kX)(c)$ is the set of compatible families over the morphisms
of alternation $\ge k$ from marks of $F$ into $c$, with values at the
starting marks. In the
bounded case these morphisms, for $k=a+1$, are those with a mark in
$E\wedge F$, each of which factors through its first such mark by
Lemma~\ref{lem:words}(c), and the restriction to the families over the
morphisms from marks of $E\wedge F$ is bijective by the argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:iter}; the sharpness argument applies to the least
covering sieve $D_{E\wedge F}(c)\hookrightarrow y(c)$ at the end $c$ of
a morphism of alternation $a$ without marks in $E\wedge F$, and if
$a_E=a_F=\mathrm{id}$ no round is needed.

(iii) The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:topjoin} is carried out on $\mathcal
K$, with marks as objects and the morphisms with no mark in $E\wedge F$
in place of the paths avoiding $S\cap T$; it uses that $D_{E\wedge F}(m)$
is the least $J_{E\wedge F}$-cover, that $D_E(m)$ is a $J_E$-cover whose
members $h=uv$ with $u$ from a mark of $E$ either have a mark in $E\wedge
F$ or come from an $E$-mark joined to $m$ by a morphism without such
marks, that $u^*D_{E\wedge F}(m)\supseteq D_{E\wedge F}(\mathrm{dom}\,u)$,
and, for the topology $K$ of that proof, that the composite of two
morphisms without marks in $E\wedge F$ has none, which holds since the
deletion of $v'\to v\to v'$ at a junction removes marks and creates
none; all of these hold by Lemma~\ref{lem:words}. For the reformulation
by backward walks: a morphism of $\mathcal K$ without marks in $E\wedge
F$ is represented by a path of $G_\Lambda$ with no vertex in $E\wedge F$
(note that $v\in E\wedge F$ implies $v'\in E\wedge F$), and the
concatenation of such paths, without deletion, is a walk of
$G_\Lambda$ with no vertex in $E\wedge F$; conversely the morphism
represented by a walk with no vertex in $E\wedge F$ visits, after the
deletions, the same set of marks, as a deletion of $v'\to v\to v'$
leaves $v'$, which counts as $v$. So the set $B$ of non-reconstructible
marks is non-empty iff there is an infinite backward walk in
$G_\Lambda$ avoiding $E\wedge F$ visiting $E$ and $F$ infinitely often,
exactly as in Theorem~\ref{thm:topjoin}.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
The flows of this section are set-valued, the transfer functions being
arbitrary maps between the sets of states, and the sheaf conditions
concern which states are determined by the states at the cut points.
The monotone transfer functions on lattices of data-flow analysis are
a different, enriched setting; the results above describe the
set-theoretic skeleton on which such an analysis is organised.
\end{rem}

\subsection{Finite action monoids.}
A deterministic transition system with action alphabet $\Sigma$ is a
coalgebra $X\to X^\Sigma$, i.e.\ a right $\Sigma^*$-set, and the presheaf
topos of right $M$-sets for a monoid $M$ is the general form; its ideal
topologies are the $J_D$ for the idempotent two-sided ideals $D\subseteq
M$, with $c_D(A)=\{x:x\cdot D\subseteq A\}$: a state is in the closure of
the invariant $A$ iff all its $D$-futures lie in $A$.

\begin{lem}[{\cite[\S2]{Finitejoin}}]\label{lem:idem}
In a finite monoid $M$, or a finite category, a two-sided ideal $D$ is
idempotent iff it is generated by idempotents, $D=M\,E\,M$ with
$E=\{e\in D:e^2=e\}$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
$(MeM)(MeM)\ni ue\,ev=uev$ gives $MEM\subseteq(MEM)^2\subseteq MEM$.
Conversely let $D=D^2$, so $D=D^n$ for all $n$, and write $d\in D$ as
$d=s_1\cdots s_n$ with $s_i\in D$ and $n>|M|$. Two prefixes coincide,
$s_1\cdots s_i=s_1\cdots s_j$ with $i<j$, so with $w=s_{i+1}\cdots s_j\in D$
the prefix $p=s_1\cdots s_i$ satisfies $p=pw^m$ for all $m$; some power
$e=w^m$ is idempotent, $e\in D$, and $d=pe\,s_{j+1}\cdots s_n\in MeM$.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:monoid}
On the right $M$-sets of a finite monoid $M$ the ideal topologies are
indexed by the ideals generated by sets $E$ of idempotents, with
$c_{MEM}(A)=\{x:x\,u\,e\in A\text{ for all }u\in M,e\in E\}$: a state is in
the closure of the invariant $A$ iff every future that passes through a
reset $e\in E$ lands in $A$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:kar} the sheaves are the
presheaves on the full subcategory of $\mathrm{Kar}(M)$ on $E$, for a
single idempotent the right $eMe$-sets. By \cite{Stabjoin},
$W_D\vee W_{D'}=W_{J_D\vee J_{D'}}$ for any two of them, with the
stabilisation rank of $(DD')^k$ as the bound on the alternation. For the
free monoid $\Sigma^*$, and more generally for a monoid with a length
function additive and positive on non-identity elements, the only
idempotent ideals are $\emptyset$ and $M$, so the deterministic systems
over a free alphabet have no non-trivial ideal topology.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
The first statement is Lemma~\ref{lem:idem} and the description of
$c_D$, as $x\cdot MeM\subseteq A$ iff $x\,u\,e\in A$ for all $u$ (the
right factor preserves $A$), with Lemma~\ref{lem:kar}. The second is
\cite{Stabjoin} together with finiteness. For the last, a shortest
non-identity element of an idempotent ideal $D\ne M$ is a product of two
elements of $D$ of larger length.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
Nontrivial ideal topologies on deterministic systems thus need relations
among the actions, typically idempotent actions (resets, normalisation
steps), which is the one-object case of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}. In a
band ($w^2=w$ for all $w$) every ideal is idempotent, and for the free
band on $\Sigma$ the ideals $D_a$ of words containing the letter $a$
satisfy $D_aD_b=D_a\cap D_b$: one alternation. The contracted cycle
monoids of \cite{Finitejoin}, with stabilisation rank growing with the
cycle, are the instances of unbounded alternation in this setting.
\end{rem}

\section{Questions}

\begin{que}
Which pairs of standard numerical abstract domains (intervals, congruences,
octagons, polyhedra, on $\cP(\mathbb Z^n)$) have unbounded alternation at
some element, so that the join of the two classes of collapsed pairs is
not the class of the least common abstraction? The pairs checked
(intervals and congruences) stabilise in two steps because the interval
hull of an infinite congruence class is a product of lines and points.
\end{que}

\begin{que}
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} treats quotients of the path category by
idempotent loops. For which other relations among the edges, e.g.\
commuting squares (independent actions, trace monoids), do the
alternation criterion, the identification of the join topology and the
count of rounds survive, and what replaces the linear order of the
factorisations through marks?
\end{que}
