\title[Joins of topologies and the sameness lattice]{Joins of Lawvere--Tierney topologies in the lattice of two-out-of-three classes: the finite-iteration criterion}
\author{Ryota Shibusawa}
\thanks{Corresponding author. E-mail: \texttt{r-shibusawa@daiichi-koudai.ac.jp}}
\address{Daiichi Institute of Technology}
\email{r-shibusawa@daiichi-koudai.ac.jp}
\keywords{Lawvere--Tierney topology, Grothendieck topology, subtopos, sheafification, local isomorphism,
  two-out-of-three, idempotent ideal, join of topologies, finite iteration}
\subjclass[2020]{18F10 (primary), 18B25, 18F20, 18A32, 20M12 (secondary)}

\begin{abstract}
In a Grothendieck topos $\cE$, every Lawvere--Tierney topology $j$ (equivalently,
every subtopos) defines the class $W_j$ of maps inverted by its sheafification,
an element of the complete lattice $\Same(\cE)$ of classes of morphisms closed
under composition and two-out-of-three. The map $j\mapsto W_j$ preserves all
meets; whether it preserves the join of two topologies was known to depend on
the topos. We describe the join $W_j\vee W_{j'}$ for arbitrary $j,j'$: it is
the class of maps whose image is reached from the codomain, and whose kernel
pair is reached from the diagonal, by a bounded number of alternating
applications of the two closure operators, and every such map is a composite
of maps of $W_j\cup W_{j'}$, so the join is the closure of the union under
composition alone. Consequently $W_j\vee W_{j'}=W_{j\vee j'}$ if and only if
$(jj')^k=j\vee j'$ for some $k$, the finite-iteration axiom that governs joins
of nuclei on a Heyting algebra and on the algebra of truth values of fuzzy
sets, now for the subobject classifier. For presheaf toposes and topologies
of ideal type, $J_D$ and $J_E$ for idempotent ideals $D,E$, the condition is
the stabilisation of the chain $DE\supseteq DEDE\supseteq\cdots$, which
contains the known positive answer for finite categories and the negative
answer on the natural numbers; for ideals generated by two sets of objects
$A,B$ it decides when the join of ``bijective at $A$'' and ``bijective at $B$''
is ``bijective at $A\cap B$''; on the natural numbers, the join of the classes
of maps bijective at the even and at the odd points is the class of maps of
towers that are bijective up to a uniform shift, a proper subclass of the
isomorphisms of pro-sets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $\cE$ be a Grothendieck topos. A Lawvere--Tierney topology $j$ on $\cE$,
equivalently a subtopos $\Sh_j(\cE)$, has a sheafification functor $a_j$, and
the class $W_j$ of maps inverted by $a_j$, the $j$-local isomorphisms, is a
\emph{sameness}: a class of morphisms containing the identities and closed
under composition and two-out-of-three. Samenesses form a complete lattice
$\Same(\cE)$ under inclusion, meets being intersections and the join of
two classes the least sameness containing both \cite{Same}. The assignment
$j\mapsto W_j$ preserves all meets \cite[Thm.~3.1]{Topjoin}, and the question
studied in \cite{Topjoin,Finitejoin} is whether it preserves binary joins:
whether every map inverted by the sheafification of $j\vee j'$ is obtained
from $j$-local and $j'$-local isomorphisms by finitely many compositions and
cancellations. For presheaf toposes the answer was known to be yes on every
finite category \cite{Finitejoin} and no on the poset $\mathbb N$
\cite[Thm.~7.1]{Topjoin}. This paper describes the join $W_j\vee W_{j'}$ for
arbitrary topologies on an arbitrary Grothendieck topos and gives the exact
criterion for it to equal $W_{j\vee j'}$.

A topology $j$ has a closure operator $c$ on subobjects, and a map
$f\colon X\to Y$ is in $W_j$ iff it is \emph{$j$-bidense}: the image of $f$
is $c$-dense in $Y$ and the kernel pair of $f$ lies in the closure of the
diagonal of $X$. For two topologies $j,j'$ with closure operators $c,c'$ let
$U_k$ be the class of maps $f\colon X\to Y$ with
\[
(cc')^k(\im f)=Y\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ker f\subseteq(cc')^k(\Delta_X),
\]
the maps that are bidense \emph{uniformly} with respect to the $k$-fold
alternation of the two closures.

\begin{thm}\label{thm:A}
For any Lawvere--Tierney topologies $j,j'$ on a topos $\cE$,
\[
W_j\vee W_{j'}=\bigcup_kU_k .
\]
$U_0$ is the class of isomorphisms, and for $k\ge1$ every map in $U_k$ is a
composite of $2k$ epimorphisms followed by $2k$ monomorphisms lying
alternately in $W_{j'}$ and $W_j$. In particular the join is the closure of
$W_j\cup W_{j'}$ under composition.
\end{thm}

\begin{thm}\label{thm:B}
Let $\cE$ be a Grothendieck topos. Then $W_j\vee W_{j'}=W_{j\vee j'}$ if and
only if $(jj')^k=j\vee j'$ as endomorphisms of the subobject classifier
$\Omega$ for some $k$; equivalently, $(cc')^k$ is the closure operator of
$j\vee j'$ on every subobject lattice.
\end{thm}

The closure operator of $j\vee j'$ is the transfinite iterate of $cc'$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:joinK}), so Theorem~\ref{thm:B} says that the join of the two
classes is the class of the join topology exactly when the transfinite
iteration stops at a finite stage uniformly. This is the condition
$(j_2j_1)^N=j_1\vee j_2$ that governs joins of nuclei on a Heyting algebra and
their joins in the sameness lattice of fuzzy sets \cite{Laxjoin,Fuzzysame}, now
for the internal frame $\Omega$. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} holds in every
elementary topos; Theorem~\ref{thm:B} uses countable coproducts and
transfinite unions of subobjects.

For a presheaf topos $\Presh(S)$ and Grothendieck topologies $J,J'$ on the
small category $S$ the conditions become conditions on sieves
(Lemma~\ref{lem:dict}): the image sieves $\{b:y\cdot b\in\im f\}$ and the
equaliser sieves $\{b:x\cdot b=x'\cdot b\}$, $f(x)=f(x')$, must be sent to the
maximal sieve by $(jj')^k$. For topologies of \emph{ideal type} the
criterion becomes a chain condition. A two-sided ideal $D$ of $S$ is a set of
morphisms closed under composition with arbitrary morphisms on either side;
it is \emph{idempotent} if every member is a composite of two members. Every
idempotent ideal $D$ defines a topology $J_D$ whose covering sieves on $c$
are the sieves containing $D(-,c)=\{a\colon x\to c\text{ in }D\}$
\cite[Thm.~5.1]{Postnikov} (for monoids, \cite[Thm.~2.9]{KM}: the weak
ideal topology of a two-sided ideal $I$ is a topology iff $I^2=I$); on a
finite category every topology is of this form (Kelly--Lawvere \cite{KL89},
\cite{Marques25}), on an infinite one not necessarily. For ideals $D,E$ write $DE=\{d\circ e:d\in D,e\in E\}$, again an
ideal, and $(DE)^k=DE\cdots DE$ ($k$ factors of each).

\begin{cor}\label{cor:ideal}
Let $D,E$ be idempotent ideals of $S$. Then $W_{J_D\vee J_E}=W_{J_D}\vee
W_{J_E}$ in $\Same(\Presh(S))$ if and only if the chain $DE\supseteq(DE)^2
\supseteq\cdots$ stabilises, i.e.\ $(DE)^m=(DE)^{m+1}$ for some $m$. In that
case $I':=(DE)^m$ is an idempotent ideal with $(ED)^{m+1}=I'$,
$J_D\vee J_E=J_{I'}$, $W_{J_D}\vee W_{J_E}=W_{J_{I'}}$, and every
$J_{I'}$-local isomorphism is a composite of $4m$ maps of
$W_{J_D}\cup W_{J_E}$. In every case $W_{J_D}\vee W_{J_E}$ is the class of
maps locally bijective along some power $(DE)^k$.
\end{cor}

On a finite category the chain stabilises for every pair, which recovers
\cite{Finitejoin} with a proof of a few lines in place of the separated
quotients and plus towers used there; on $\mathbb N$ with the ideals of the
even and of the odd points it is strictly decreasing, which recovers
\cite[Thm.~7.1]{Topjoin}. The criterion becomes concrete for ideals generated
by objects. For a set $A$ of objects, the ideal $D_A$ of morphisms factoring
through $A$ is idempotent, and $W_A=W_{J_{D_A}}$ is the class of maps
bijective at every object of $A$, the class inverted by restriction to $A$,
the sheafification for the essential subtopos
$\Presh(A)\hookrightarrow\Presh(S)$. Proposition~\ref{prop:objects} decides
when $W_A\vee W_B=W_{A\cap B}$: iff every morphism factoring through a long
enough alternating zigzag of objects of $B$ and $A$ factors through an object
of $A\cap B$, on a locally finite poset iff the strictly alternating chains
avoiding $A\cap B$ have bounded size. On $\mathbb N$ with the even and the odd
points the join is the class of maps of towers that are bijective up to a
uniform shift, a proper subclass of the isomorphisms of pro-sets
(Example~\ref{ex:pro}).

The proofs are elementary and use only the calculus of closure operators on
subobjects. The inclusion $U_k\subseteq W_j\vee W_{j'}$ is a peeling
argument: an epimorphism whose kernel pair lies in the $k$-fold alternating
closure of the diagonal is divided by the equivalence relation ``kernel pair
intersected with the $c'$-closure of the diagonal'', which is a $j'$-local
isomorphism, and the induced map needs one closure fewer; dually a
monomorphism is enlarged to the $c'$-closure of its image. The failure
direction uses, for a dense subobject $S$ of $Y$ that is not reached in $k$
steps, the fold map of two copies of $Y$ glued along $S$; the coproduct of
these maps over all $k$ is inverted by the join sheafification but lies in
no $U_k$.

\subsection*{What is known.}
Samenesses and $W_j$ are from \cite{Same}; that meets of topologies go to
meets of samenesses, the reduction lemma and the $\mathbb N$ counterexample are
from \cite{Topjoin}; the finite case, with the alternating separated
quotients, the plus tower and the retraction of representing bimodules, is
\cite{Finitejoin}. Lawvere--Tierney topologies, universal closure operators
and the bidense description of the maps inverted by sheafification are
standard \cite[V.1, V.3]{MLM}, \cite[A4.3--A4.4]{Elephant}; for presheaves
the description by image and equaliser sieves is \cite[III.7]{MLM}. Ideal
topologies, the correspondence with idempotent ideals on finite categories
and the local operators $R\mapsto\{a:a\cdot D\subseteq R\}$ are in
\cite{KL89,Marques25,Postnikov}. Joins of nuclei as transfinite iterates are
in \cite{Escardo}; for Lawvere--Tierney topologies, Khanjanzadeh and
Madanshekaf \cite{KM} observe that the composite $jj'$ is in general only a
\emph{weak} topology (not idempotent), show that the weak topologies on a
cocomplete topos form a complete residuated lattice, and compute the join of
two topologies as the topological reflection of their pointwise join, a
transfinite iterate; Lemma~\ref{lem:joinK} is this computation for the
composite, at the level of closure operators. The maps inverted by
sheafification, and joins of such classes, are not considered there. The
first version of this paper (archived at DOI
10.5281/zenodo.23467281) proved Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal} for ideal
topologies only, with the stabilising direction by the route of
\cite{Finitejoin}; the second version (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23468327) proved
Theorems~\ref{thm:A} and~\ref{thm:B} for presheaf toposes by sieve
arguments.

\subsection*{What is new.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, the description of the join for arbitrary topologies
on arbitrary toposes and the fact that no cancellations are needed;
Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, the finite-iteration criterion, which decides the
question for every pair of subtoposes of every Grothendieck topos, whereas
\cite{Finitejoin} proves the positive answer under a finiteness hypothesis;
Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal} with its short proof; Proposition~\ref{prop:objects}
and Example~\ref{ex:pro}. The results do not follow from the theory of
localisations or of the subtopos lattice alone: the meet of the two
subtoposes, i.e.\ the join topology, is determined by $j$ and $j'$, but the
join of the two classes in $\Same(\cE)$ is a finer invariant (the uniform
version of bidensity), and Example~\ref{ex:pro} exhibits two subtoposes whose
meet is the degenerate subtopos while the join of their classes is a proper
sameness.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection*{Closure operators.}
Let $\cE$ be a topos. A Lawvere--Tierney topology $j\colon\Omega\to\Omega$
corresponds to a \emph{universal closure operator}: for each object $X$ a
map $A\mapsto c(A)$ on $\Sub(X)$ that is inflationary, monotone, idempotent,
preserves finite intersections, and is stable under pullback,
$g^{-1}(c(B))=c(g^{-1}(B))$ for $g\colon X\to Y$ and $B\subseteq Y$; the
closure of $A$ is classified by $j\circ\chi_A$ \cite[V.1]{MLM}. Topologies
are ordered pointwise, $j\le j'$ iff $c(A)\subseteq c'(A)$ for all $A$, and
the correspondence with closure operators is an order isomorphism. A
subobject $A\subseteq X$ is \emph{$c$-dense} if $c(A)=X$. We write $\im f$
for the image of $f\colon X\to Y$, $\ker f\subseteq X\times X$ for its kernel
pair, $\Delta_X\subseteq X\times X$ for the diagonal, and $g(A)$ for the image
of a subobject $A\subseteq X$ under $g$.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:calc}
Let $c$ be a universal closure operator, $g\colon X\to Y$, $A,K\subseteq X$,
$B\subseteq Y$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $g(c(A))\subseteq c(g(A))$.
\item[(ii)] $c(A)\cap K\subseteq c(A\cap K)$.
\item[(iii)] $c(A\times B)=c(A)\times c(B)$ in $\Sub(X\times Y)$.
\item[(iv)] $c(\Delta_X)$ is an equivalence relation on $X$.
\end{enumerate}
The same holds for every composite $w$ of universal closure operators,
which is again inflationary, monotone, pullback-stable and
intersection-preserving.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
(i) $A\subseteq g^{-1}(g(A))$, so $c(A)\subseteq c(g^{-1}(g(A)))=
g^{-1}(c(g(A)))$. (ii) Let $m\colon K\hookrightarrow X$; then $A\cap K=
m(m^{-1}(A))$ and $c(A)\cap K=m(m^{-1}(c(A)))=m(c_K(m^{-1}(A)))\subseteq
c(m(m^{-1}(A)))$ by (i), where $c_K$ is the closure on $\Sub(K)$, the
pullback of $c$. (iii) $A\times B=\pi_1^{-1}(A)\cap\pi_2^{-1}(B)$, and $c$
preserves intersections and pullbacks. (iv) Reflexivity and symmetry are
clear (the twist map preserves $\Delta_X$ and commutes with $c$). The
composite of $c(\Delta_X)$ with itself is $\pi_{13}\bigl(\pi_{12}^{-1}c(\Delta_X)
\cap\pi_{23}^{-1}c(\Delta_X)\bigr)=\pi_{13}\bigl(c(\pi_{12}^{-1}\Delta_X\cap
\pi_{23}^{-1}\Delta_X)\bigr)\subseteq c(\pi_{13}(\delta))=c(\Delta_X)$, where
$\delta\subseteq X^3$ is the image of the triple diagonal, by (i).
The last sentence is clear.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Local isomorphisms.}
Let $a_j$ be the sheafification for $j$ and $W_j$ the class of maps inverted
by $a_j$. A map $f\colon X\to Y$ is \emph{$j$-bidense} if $\im f$ is
$c$-dense in $Y$ and $\ker f\subseteq c(\Delta_X)$, i.e.\ the diagonal
$X\to X\times_YX$ is $c$-dense. A map is in $W_j$ iff it is $j$-bidense
\cite[A4.3]{Elephant}; in particular a monomorphism is in $W_j$ iff it is
$c$-dense, and an epimorphism is in $W_j$ iff its kernel pair lies in
$c(\Delta_X)$. For $\cE=\Presh(S)$ and a Grothendieck topology $J$ this is
the description of $J$-local isomorphisms as locally injective and locally
surjective maps \cite[III.7, Cor.~6]{MLM}; see Lemma~\ref{lem:dict}.

\subsection*{Words.}
Fix topologies $j,j'$ with closure operators $c,c'$. A \emph{word} is a
composite $w=c_1c_2\cdots c_n$ with each $c_i\in\{c,c'\}$, $c_n$ applied
first; $n$ is its length. We abbreviate $(cc')^k$ as $c^{(k)}$, a word of
length $2k$ ending with $c'$; $c^{(0)}$ is the identity. Since the closures
are inflationary, $w(A)\subseteq w'(A)$ whenever $w$ is obtained from $w'$ by
deleting letters; in particular $c^{(k)}(A)\subseteq c^{(k+1)}(A)$, and
$c(A),c'(A)\subseteq c^{(1)}(A)$.

\subsection*{The join topology.}
Let $\cE$ be a Grothendieck topos, so that every $\Sub(X)$ is a frame and
unions of subobjects are stable under pullback. Let $j\vee j'$ be the join
in the lattice of topologies and $\bar c$ its closure operator. Define the
transfinite iterates $c^{(0)}(A)=A$, $c^{(\alpha+1)}(A)=c^{(1)}(c^{(\alpha)}
(A))$ and $c^{(\lambda)}(A)=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}c^{(\alpha)}(A)$ for
limit $\lambda$; they increase with $\alpha$, are constant from some ordinal
$\gamma_A$ on since $\Sub(X)$ is a set, commute with pullback, and
$c^{(\alpha)}(c^{(\beta)}(A))=c^{(\beta+\alpha)}(A)$.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:joinK}
$\bar c(A)=\bigcup_\alpha c^{(\alpha)}(A)$ for every subobject $A$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
Write $c^\infty(A)$ for the union. Since $c,c'\le\bar c$ and $\bar c$ is
monotone and idempotent, $c^{(\alpha)}(A)\subseteq\bar c(A)$ for all $\alpha$
by induction, with unions at limits. Conversely $c^\infty$ is a universal
closure operator: it is inflationary and monotone; $c^\infty(A)=c^{(\gamma)}
(A)$ for $\gamma=\gamma_A$ is a fixed point of $c^{(1)}$, hence of $c$ and
$c'$ (as $c'(B)\subseteq c(c'(B))=B$ for a fixed point $B$) and of $c^\infty$;
it commutes with pullback because each $c^{(\alpha)}$ does and pullback
preserves unions; and it preserves binary intersections because each
$c^{(\alpha)}$ does and, $\Sub(X)$ being a frame,
$\bigcup_\alpha c^{(\alpha)}(A)\cap\bigcup_\beta c^{(\beta)}(B)=
\bigcup_{\alpha,\beta}c^{(\alpha)}(A)\cap c^{(\beta)}(B)=\bigcup_\gamma
c^{(\gamma)}(A\cap B)$. It lies above $c$ and $c'$, so its topology lies
above $j$ and $j'$, hence above $j\vee j'$, and $\bar c\le c^\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
The transfinite iterate is the ``topological reflection'' of
\cite[Cor.~4.2, Prop.~4.3]{KM}, applied to the weak topology $jj'$ instead
of the pointwise join $j\vee_{\mathrm w}j'$; since $j\vee_{\mathrm w}j'\le
jj'\le j\vee j'$, the two iterations have the same limit.
\end{rem}

\section{Uniform bidensity and the join}\label{sec:uniform}

Fix topologies $j,j'$ on a topos $\cE$ with closure operators $c,c'$, and
write $V:=W_j\vee W_{j'}$ and, when $\cE$ is a Grothendieck topos,
$K:=j\vee j'$ with closure operator $\bar c$.

\begin{defi}
For $k\ge0$ let $U_k$ be the class of maps $f\colon X\to Y$ with
$c^{(k)}(\im f)=Y$ and $\ker f\subseteq c^{(k)}(\Delta_X)$. Put
$U=\bigcup_kU_k$.
\end{defi}

$U_0$ is the class of isomorphisms (an epimorphism whose kernel pair is the
diagonal is an isomorphism). A map in $U_k$ is bidense for the join topology
\emph{uniformly}: the ordinal of Lemma~\ref{lem:joinK} needed for its image
and its kernel pair is the finite number $k$.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:U}
$U_k\subseteq U_{k+1}$, $W_j\cup W_{j'}\subseteq U_1$, $U$ is a sameness, and
in a Grothendieck topos $U\subseteq W_K$. Hence $V\subseteq U$, and
$V\subseteq U\subseteq W_K$ in a Grothendieck topos.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
The first two assertions follow from $c^{(k)}\le c^{(k+1)}$ and
$c,c'\le c^{(1)}$, the fourth from $c^{(k)}\le\bar c$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:joinK}).
Identities lie in $U_0$. Let $h\colon X\to Y$ and $g\colon Y\to Z$; we use
Lemma~\ref{lem:calc} throughout, for the words $c^{(k)}$.

\emph{Composition.} Let $h\in U_k$, $g\in U_l$. Then $\im(gh)=g(\im h)$ and
$c^{(k+l)}(\im gh)\supseteq c^{(l)}\bigl(g(c^{(k)}(\im h))\bigr)=c^{(l)}(g(Y))
=c^{(l)}(\im g)=Z$ by (i). And $\ker(gh)=(h\times h)^{-1}(\ker g)\subseteq
(h\times h)^{-1}(c^{(l)}(\Delta_Y))=c^{(l)}((h\times h)^{-1}\Delta_Y)=
c^{(l)}(\ker h)\subseteq c^{(l+k)}(\Delta_X)$.

\emph{Cancellation of $g$.} Let $gh\in U_k$ and $g\in U_k$. Then
$\ker h\subseteq\ker(gh)\subseteq c^{(k)}(\Delta_X)$. Let $B:=g^{-1}(\im gh)
\subseteq Y$; then $c^{(k)}(B)=g^{-1}(c^{(k)}(\im gh))=g^{-1}(Z)=Y$. Moreover
$B=\pi_1\bigl(\ker g\cap(Y\times\im h)\bigr)$, and
\begin{align*}
\pi_1\bigl(\ker g\cap(Y\times\im h)\bigr)&\subseteq
\pi_1\bigl(c^{(k)}(\Delta_Y)\cap(Y\times\im h)\bigr)\\
&\subseteq\pi_1\bigl(c^{(k)}(\Delta_Y\cap(Y\times\im h))\bigr)\\
&\subseteq c^{(k)}\bigl(\pi_1(\Delta_Y\cap(Y\times\im h))\bigr)=c^{(k)}(\im h)
\end{align*}
by (ii) and (i). Hence $Y=c^{(k)}(B)\subseteq c^{(2k)}(\im h)$.

\emph{Cancellation of $h$.} Let $gh\in U_k$ and $h\in U_k$. Then
$\im g\supseteq\im(gh)$, so $c^{(k)}(\im g)=Z$. Since $h\times h\colon
X\times X\to\im h\times\im h$ is an epimorphism,
$\ker g\cap(\im h\times\im h)=(h\times h)(\ker gh)\subseteq
(h\times h)(c^{(k)}(\Delta_X))\subseteq c^{(k)}(\Delta_Y)$ by (i); and
$c^{(k)}(\im h\times\im h)=Y\times Y$ by (iii). So
$\ker g=\ker g\cap c^{(k)}(\im h\times\im h)\subseteq
c^{(k)}\bigl(\ker g\cap(\im h\times\im h)\bigr)\subseteq c^{(2k)}(\Delta_Y)$
by (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}[peeling]\label{lem:peel}
Let $w=c_1c_2\cdots c_n$ be a word with $c_i$ the closure operator of
$j_i\in\{j,j'\}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $f\colon X\to Y$ is an epimorphism with $\ker f\subseteq
w(\Delta_X)$, then $f=f_1\circ\cdots\circ f_n\circ u$ with $u$ an
isomorphism and $f_i$ an epimorphism in $W_{j_i}$.
\item[(b)] If $f\colon X\to Y$ is a monomorphism with $w(X)=Y$, then
$f=f_1\circ\cdots\circ f_n\circ u$ with $u$ an isomorphism and $f_i$ a
monomorphism in $W_{j_i}$.
\end{enumerate}
For $n\ge1$ the isomorphism $u$ can be absorbed into $f_n$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
Induction on $n$; for $n=0$ the hypothesis says that $f$ is an isomorphism.
Write $w=w'c_n$.
(a) Let $R:=\ker f\cap c_n(\Delta_X)$, an equivalence relation on $X$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:calc}(iv), and let $q\colon X\twoheadrightarrow X/R$ be its
quotient, so that $\ker q=R\subseteq c_n(\Delta_X)$ and $q\in W_{j_n}$. Since
$R\subseteq\ker f$, $f=\bar f\circ q$ with $\bar f$ an epimorphism, and
$\ker\bar f=(q\times q)(\ker f)$ because $q\times q$ is an epimorphism and
$\ker f=(q\times q)^{-1}(\ker\bar f)$. Now
\begin{align*}
(q\times q)(\ker f)&=(q\times q)\bigl(w'(c_n\Delta_X)\cap\ker f\bigr)
\subseteq(q\times q)\bigl(w'(c_n\Delta_X\cap\ker f)\bigr)\\
&=(q\times q)(w'(R))\subseteq w'\bigl((q\times q)(R)\bigr)=w'(\Delta_{X/R})
\end{align*}
by Lemma~\ref{lem:calc}(ii) and (i), as $(q\times q)(\ker q)=\Delta_{X/R}$.
Induction applied to $\bar f$ and $w'$ gives the factorisation.
(b) Regard $X$ as a subobject of $Y$ and let $Z:=c_n(X)\subseteq Y$. The
inclusion $X\hookrightarrow Z$ is $c_n$-dense (the closure of $X$ in $Z$ is
$c_n(X)\cap Z=Z$ by pullback stability), hence in $W_{j_n}$, and
$w'(Z)=w(X)=Y$. Induction applied to $Z\hookrightarrow Y$ and $w'$ gives the
factorisation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
Let $C$ be the closure of $W_j\cup W_{j'}$ under composition. Then
$C\subseteq V\subseteq U$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:U}. Let $f\in U_k$, $k\ge1$, and
factor $f=m\circ e$ with $e\colon X\twoheadrightarrow\im f$ and $m\colon\im f
\hookrightarrow Y$. Then $\ker e=\ker f\subseteq c^{(k)}(\Delta_X)$ and
$c^{(k)}(\im f)=Y$, and $c^{(k)}$ is a word of length $2k$ ending with $c'$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:peel}, $e$ is a composite of $2k$ epimorphisms and $m$ a
composite of $2k$ monomorphisms, alternately in $W_{j'}$ and $W_j$,
beginning with $W_{j'}$. So $U\subseteq C$, and $C=V=U$.
\end{proof}

$n$ maps of $W_j\cup W_{j'}$ lies in $U_n$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:U}, and a map of

hold in every elementary topos.
\end{rem}

\section{The criterion}\label{sec:criterion}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:witness}
Let $S\subseteq Y$ be a subobject, let $X_S:=Y\amalg_SY$ be the pushout of
$Y\hookleftarrow S\hookrightarrow Y$ with injections $\iota_1,\iota_2$, and let
$f_S\colon X_S\to Y$ be the fold map. For every word $w$ (and for $\bar c$),
$\ker f_S\subseteq w(\Delta_{X_S})$ iff $w(S)=Y$. Hence $f_S\in U_k$ iff
$c^{(k)}(S)=Y$, and in a Grothendieck topos $f_S\in W_K$ iff $S$ is
$\bar c$-dense in $Y$.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
In a topos the pushout of a monomorphism along a monomorphism is a pullback
square and its injections are monomorphisms \cite[A2.4]{Elephant}; so
$\iota_1,\iota_2$ are monomorphisms with intersection $S$, and the equaliser
of $\iota_1,\iota_2$ is $S$. The map $f_S$ is an epimorphism, as $\iota_1,
\iota_2$ are jointly epimorphic, so its image is $Y$. Since $f_S\iota_1=
f_S\iota_2=\mathrm{id}_Y$, the kernel pair of $f_S$ is the union of the
images of $(\iota_a,\iota_b)\colon Y\to X_S\times X_S$ over $a,b\in\{1,2\}$,
that is, of $\Delta_{X_S}$ and the images of $(\iota_1,\iota_2)$ and
$(\iota_2,\iota_1)$. Now $(\iota_1,\iota_2)^{-1}(\Delta_{X_S})$ is the
equaliser of $\iota_1,\iota_2$, i.e.\ $S$, so by pullback stability
$(\iota_1,\iota_2)^{-1}(w(\Delta_{X_S}))=w(S)$, and the image of
$(\iota_1,\iota_2)$ lies in $w(\Delta_{X_S})$ iff $w(S)=Y$; the same holds
for $(\iota_2,\iota_1)$ by symmetry. The last two assertions follow with
$w=c^{(k)}$ and $w=\bar c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
The identity $(jj')^k=j\vee j'$ on $\Omega$ is equivalent to
$c^{(k)}=\bar c$ on every $\Sub(X)$, since the closure of $A$ for a topology
$t$ is classified by $t\circ\chi_A$ and the generic subobject
$\top\colon1\to\Omega$ has $\chi_\top=\mathrm{id}_\Omega$. If $c^{(k)}=\bar c$,
a map in $W_K$ is $K$-bidense, hence in $U_k\subseteq V$, and $V\subseteq
W_K$ always. If $c^{(k)}\ne\bar c$ for every $k$, choose for each $k$ a
subobject $S_k\subseteq Y_k$ with $c^{(k)}(S_k)\ne\bar c(S_k)$; replacing
$Y_k$ by $\bar c(S_k)$ we may assume $S_k$ is $\bar c$-dense in $Y_k$ but
$c^{(k)}(S_k)\ne Y_k$, as $c^{(k)}\le\bar c$ and both commute with pullback
along $\bar c(S_k)\hookrightarrow Y_k$. Let $f_k:=f_{S_k}\colon X_k\to Y_k$ and
$F:=\coprod_kf_k$. Then $F\in W_K$: it is an epimorphism, and since
coproducts are disjoint and universal, $\ker F$ is the union of the images
of $\ker f_k$ under the monomorphisms $X_k\times X_k\hookrightarrow
(\coprod_kX_k)^2$, which lie in the images of $\bar c(\Delta_{X_k})$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}, hence in $\bar c(\Delta_{\coprod X_k})$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:calc}(i). But $F\notin U_k$ for every $k$: pulling back
along the summand $X_k\times X_k\hookrightarrow(\coprod X)^2$ and
$Y_k\hookrightarrow\coprod Y$, the conditions for $F$ restrict to those for
$f_k$ (pullback stability of $c^{(k)}$, disjointness of coproducts), and
$f_k\notin U_k$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}. So $F\in W_K\setminus U=
W_K\setminus V$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
Lemma~\ref{lem:joinK} and Theorem~\ref{thm:B} place the question in the
setting of \cite{Laxjoin,Fuzzysame}: there the join $j_1\vee j_2$ of two
nuclei on a Heyting algebra or frame $L$ is the transfinite iterate of
$j_2j_1$ \cite{Escardo}, and the identity $(j_2j_1)^N=j_1\vee j_2$ for some
$N$, the finite-iteration axiom of the lax logic of the join in
\cite{Laxjoin}, is exactly the condition under which the join of the two
reflections of $L$-fuzzy sets is generated at the level of inverted maps
\cite[Cor.~4.3]{Fuzzysame}. Theorem~\ref{thm:B} is the same statement for
the internal frame $\Omega$ of a Grothendieck topos with the
Lawvere--Tierney topologies $j,j'$: the join of two subtopos classes is the
class of the meet of the subtoposes iff the alternation of the two local
operators reaches the join uniformly in finitely many steps. The condition
is uniform in the subobject; it should be distinguished from the pointwise
condition that the iteration terminates at each element, which suffices in
$\Same(L)$ \cite{Fuzzysame} and which, for the relativized necessities
$\Box_C,\Box_{C'}$ on the frame of up-sets of a poset, is the absence of
infinite alternating chains characterising $\Box_C\vee\Box_{C'}=
\Box_{C\cap C'}$ \cite{Relnec}; a disjoint union of finite alternating chains
of unbounded length satisfies the pointwise condition but not the uniform
one.
\end{rem}

\section{Presheaf toposes and topologies of ideal type}\label{sec:ideal}

\subsection*{The sieve dictionary.}
Let $\cE=\Presh(S)$ for a small category $S$. A sieve on $c$ is a set $R$ of
morphisms with codomain $c$ closed under precomposition; for $b\colon x\to c$,
$b^*R=\{e:be\in R\}$. A Grothendieck topology $J$ assigns to each $c$ a set
$J(c)$ of covering sieves \cite[III.2]{MLM}, and corresponds to the
Lawvere--Tierney topology $j$ with $j(R)=\{b\colon x\to c:\ b^*R\in J(x)\}$ on
$\Omega(c)$, the set of sieves on $c$; the closure of a subpresheaf
$A\subseteq X$ is $c(A)(d)=\{x\in X(d):\{b:x\cdot b\in A\}\in J\}$
\cite[V.1, V.4]{MLM}. A sieve $R$ is covering iff $j(R)$ is the maximal sieve.
For a map $f\colon X\to Y$, $y\in Y(d)$ and $x,x'\in X(d)$ put
\[
\Ims_f(y)=\{b\colon d_0\to d:\ y\cdot b\in\im f_{d_0}\},\quad
\Eq(x,x')=\{b\colon d_0\to d:\ x\cdot b=x'\cdot b\}.
\]
Both are sieves, $b^*\Ims_f(y)=\Ims_f(y\cdot b)$ and $b^*\Eq(x,x')=
\Eq(x\cdot b,x'\cdot b)$. For a word $w$ in $j,j'$ call a sieve $R$ on $d$
\emph{$w$-reachable} if $\mathrm{id}_d\in w(R)$, i.e.\ $w(R)$ is maximal, and
\emph{$k$-reachable} if $(jj')^k$-reachable.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:dict}
Let $w$ be a word in $c,c'$ and $\tilde w$ the corresponding word in $j,j'$.
Then $y\in w(\im f)$ iff $\Ims_f(y)$ is $\tilde w$-reachable, and
$(x,x')\in w(\Delta_X)$ iff $\Eq(x,x')$ is $\tilde w$-reachable. Hence
$f\in U_k$ iff all image sieves of $f$ and all equaliser sieves $\Eq(x,x')$
with $f(x)=f(x')$ are $k$-reachable, and $f\in W_J$ iff these sieves are
$J$-covering.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
By induction on the length of $w$, using that for a subpresheaf $A$ of $X$
and $x\in X(d)$ the sieve $\{b:x\cdot b\in A\}$ is, for $A=w_0(\im f)$,
the sieve $\{b:\Ims_f(y\cdot b)\text{ is }\tilde w_0\text{-reachable}\}=
\{b:b^*\Ims_f(y)\text{ is }\tilde w_0\text{-reachable}\}=\tilde w_0(\Ims_f(y))$
by the induction hypothesis and pullback stability of $\tilde w_0$; so
$y\in c_1(w_0(\im f))$ iff $\tilde w_0(\Ims_f(y))\in J_1$ iff
$\mathrm{id}\in j_1\tilde w_0(\Ims_f(y))$. The same computation applies to
$\Eq$ with $A=w_0(\Delta_X)$ and the pairs $(x\cdot b,x'\cdot b)$. The last
assertion is the bidense description, with $w=c$ and $c'$, and is the
classical one \cite[III.7, Cor.~6]{MLM}.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Ideal topologies.}
For an ideal $A$ the sets $A(-,c)$ are sieves and $A(-,c)\cdot b\subseteq
A(-,x)$ for $b\colon x\to c$.
\begin{lem}\label{lem:ideal}
Let $D,E$ be idempotent ideals.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $J_D$ is a topology, $D(-,c)$ is its least covering sieve on $c$,
and $J_D\le J_E$ iff $D\supseteq E$.
\item[(ii)] The closure operator of $J_D$ is $j_D(S)=\{b:\ b\cdot D\subseteq
S\}$, where $b\cdot D=\{b\circ d:d\in D(-,\operatorname{dom}b)\}$; hence
$(j_Dj_E)^k(S)=\{b:\ b\cdot(DE)^k\subseteq S\}$, and $S$ is $k$-reachable for
$J_D,J_E$ iff $S\supseteq(DE)^k(-,c)$. A map is in $U_k$ iff it is
\emph{locally bijective along $(DE)^k$}: $f(x)=f(x')$ implies $x\cdot a=
x'\cdot a$ for all $a\in(DE)^k(-,c)$, and $y\cdot a\in\im f$ for all $y\in Y(c)$
and $a\in(DE)^k(-,c)$.
\item[(iii)] For every $k$ and $c$ the sieve $(DE)^k(-,c)$ is
$(J_D\vee J_E)$-covering.
\item[(iv)] If $(DE)^m=(DE)^{m+1}=:I'$ then $I'$ is idempotent,
$I'\subseteq D\cap E$, $DI'=I'D=EI'=I'E=I'$, $(ED)^{m+1}=I'$, every
alternating product of at least $2(m+1)$ factors $D,E$, in either order,
equals $I'$, and $J_D\vee J_E=J_{I'}$. (The chain $(ED)^k$ need not have
stabilised at index $m$: Example~\ref{ex:ED}.)
\end{enumerate}
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
(i) is \cite[Thm.~5.1]{Postnikov}: the maximal sieve contains $D(-,c)$;
pullback of a sieve containing $D(-,c)$ along $b\colon x\to c$ contains
$D(-,x)$ because $D$ is a left ideal; and if $R\supseteq D(-,c)$ and each
$a^*R'$ with $a\in R$ contains $D(-,\operatorname{dom}a)$, then for
$d\in D(-,c)$, $d=d_1d_2$ with $d_i\in D$ by idempotency, $d_1\in R$ and
$d_2\in d_1^*R'$, so $d\in R'$. (ii) $b\in j_D(S)$ iff $b^*S\supseteq
D(-,\operatorname{dom}b)$ iff $b\cdot D\subseteq S$; then $b\in j_Dj_E(S)$ iff
$(b\circ d)\cdot E\subseteq S$ for all $d$ iff $b\cdot DE\subseteq S$, and
inductively for $(DE)^k$; $\mathrm{id}_c\cdot(DE)^k=(DE)^k(-,c)$. For a map
$f$, $\Ims_f(y)\supseteq(DE)^k(-,c)$ says $y\cdot a\in\im f$ for $a\in(DE)^k
(-,c)$, and $\Eq(x,x')\supseteq(DE)^k(-,c)$ says $x\cdot a=x'\cdot a$; now
apply Lemma~\ref{lem:dict}. (iii)
follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:joinK}: $(DE)^k(-,c)$ is $k$-reachable, and a
$k$-reachable sieve is $K$-covering since $(jj')^k\le j_K$. (iv) $I'I'=(DE)^{2m}
=I'$; $I'\subseteq DE\subseteq D\cap E$; $DI'=DD\,E(DE)^{m-1}=(DE)^m=I'$ as
$DD=D$, and similarly $I'E=I'$; $I'D\supseteq I'I'=I'$ as $I'\subseteq D$, and
$I'D\subseteq I'$ as $I'$ is a right ideal, so $I'D=I'$, and likewise $EI'=I'$;
then $(ED)^{m+1}=E(DE)^mD=EI'D=I'$. An alternating product of at least
$2(m+1)$ factors contains $(DE)^{m+1}$ or $(ED)^{m+1}$ as a block of
consecutive factors; the block equals $I'$, and multiplying $I'$ by $D$ or $E$
on either side returns $I'$. For the join, $J_{I'}\ge J_D,J_E$ since
$I'\subseteq D,E$, and any topology above $J_D$ and $J_E$ has
$I'(-,c)=(DE)^m(-,c)$ covering by (iii), hence is above $J_{I'}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(ii) and Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, $W_{J_D}\vee W_{J_E}$
is the class of maps locally bijective along some $(DE)^k$. If
$(DE)^m=(DE)^{m+1}=I'$, then $K=J_{I'}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(iv), a
$K$-covering sieve contains $I'(-,c)=(DE)^m(-,c)$ and is therefore
$m$-reachable; Theorem~\ref{thm:B} gives $V=W_K=W_{J_{I'}}$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} gives the factorisation into $4m$ maps, since
$W_{J_{I'}}=U_m$. If the chain does not stabilise, then $(DE)^k\ne(DE)^{k+1}$
for every $k$ (once two consecutive terms agree, all later ones do), so there
are $c_k$ with $(DE)^k(-,c_k)\not\subseteq(DE)^{k+1}(-,c_k)$; the sieve
$S_k=(DE)^{k+1}(-,c_k)$ is $K$-covering by Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(iii) and not
$k$-reachable by Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(ii), so Theorem~\ref{thm:B} gives
$V\ne W_K$. The witnesses of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} are here two
copies of $y(c_k)$ glued along $(DE)^{k+1}(-,c_k)$, whose fold map is locally
bijective along $(DE)^{k+1}$ but identifies the two identities, which
restrict along a morphism of $(DE)^k\setminus(DE)^{k+1}$ into $c_k$ to
distinct elements.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
The first version of this paper proved the stabilising direction by the
route of \cite{Finitejoin}: $2r$ alternating separated quotients, $r=m+1$,
followed by $4r$ alternating plus constructions, with a retraction of the
representing bimodule $I'\otimes I'$ onto the alternating tensor power.
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} replaces this by the single observation
$W_{J_{I'}}=U_m\subseteq V$ and improves the bound to $4m$ maps. The plus
tower is nevertheless of independent interest, as it need not stabilise even
when the chain does \cite[\S7]{Finitejoin}.
\end{rem}

\begin{ex}\label{ex:ED}
The two chains $(DE)^k$ and $(ED)^k$ need not stabilise at the same index.
Let $S$ be the category with two objects $0,1$ and one non-identity arrow
$a\colon0\to1$, and let $D=\{\mathrm{id}_0,a\}$, $E=\{\mathrm{id}_1,a\}$ be the
ideals of arrows factoring through $0$, respectively through $1$; both are
idempotent. Then $DE=\emptyset$ (no arrow of $E$ has codomain $0$), while
$ED=\{a\}$. So $(DE)^1=(DE)^2=\emptyset=I'$, $m=1$, but $(ED)^1=\{a\}\ne I'$,
and only $(ED)^2=\emptyset=I'$. The join $J_D\vee J_E=J_\emptyset$ is the
degenerate topology, as the empty sieve on $0$ is $J_E$-covering and the
sieve $\{a\}$ on $1$ is $J_D$-covering.
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}\label{ex:N}
On a finite category every chain of ideals stabilises, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal} contains \cite[Thm.~1.1]{Finitejoin}. On
$S=(\mathbb N,\le)$ with $D$ and $E$ the ideals of arrows $n\to m$ passing
through an even, respectively an odd, point, $(DE)^k$ consists of the arrows
$n\to m$ admitting $k$ alternating even and odd points between $n$ and $m$,
a strictly decreasing chain with empty intersection; the join topology is
degenerate and $W_K$ is everything, while $V$ is not \cite[Thm.~7.1]{Topjoin}.
Example~\ref{ex:pro} describes $V$.
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}
For a monoid $M$, viewed as a category with one object, ideals are the
two-sided ideals of $M$ and the criterion is the stabilisation of the
alternating products $DE\supseteq DEDE\supseteq\cdots$ in the quantale of
ideals of $M$. Every finite monoid satisfies it, with $m$ at most the number
of elements. For infinite monoids the chain can decrease forever, and then
the join of the two classes of local isomorphisms of $M$-sets is the class
of maps locally bijective along some $(DE)^k$, strictly smaller than the
class for the join topology.
\end{ex}

\section{Ideals generated by objects}\label{sec:objects}

For a set $A$ of objects of $S$ let $D_A$ be the set of morphisms factoring
through an object of $A$. It is an ideal, idempotent since $f=g\circ h$ with
$h\colon x\to a$, $g\colon a\to y$ has $g,h\in D_A$; on a finite category
every idempotent ideal is of this form after Cauchy completion
\cite[\S2]{Finitejoin}, but not in general: on $(\mathbb Q,\le)$ the ideal of
strict inequalities is idempotent and contains no identity. Write
$W_A:=W_{J_{D_A}}$.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:objects}
Let $A,B$ be sets of objects of $S$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $W_A$ is the class of maps $f$ with $f_a$ bijective for every
$a\in A$; $(D_AD_B)^k$ is the set of morphisms factoring through a chain
$b_1\to a_1\to b_2\to\cdots\to b_k\to a_k$ with $b_i\in B$, $a_i\in A$, and
contains $D_{A\cap B}$. The join $W_A\vee W_B$ is the class of maps $f$ such
that, for some $k$, $f$ is bijective along every such chain: if $f(x)=f(x')$
then $x\cdot g=x'\cdot g$, and every $y\cdot g$ lies in the image, for every
morphism $g$ factoring through a chain of length $k$.
\item[(b)] $W_A\vee W_B=W_{A\cap B}$ if and only if for some $N$ every
morphism factoring through such a chain of length $N$ factors through an
object of $A\cap B$, i.e.\ $(D_AD_B)^N\subseteq D_{A\cap B}$.
\item[(c)] If $S=P$ is a poset whose intervals are finite, the condition in
(b) holds if and only if the strictly alternating chains
$b_1<a_1<\cdots<b_k<a_k$ ($b_i\in B$, $a_i\in A$) contained in intervals
disjoint from $A\cap B$ have a bounded number of elements; if they do not,
the chain $(D_AD_B)^k$ does not stabilise and $W_A\vee W_B\subsetneq
W_{J_{D_A}\vee J_{D_B}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
(a) If $f_a$ is bijective for $a\in A$, then for $x,x'\in X(c)$ with
$f(x)=f(x')$ and $g=g_1g_2\in D_A(-,c)$ with $g_1\colon a\to c$, $a\in A$, we
get $f_a(x\cdot g_1)=f_a(x'\cdot g_1)$, so $x\cdot g_1=x'\cdot g_1$ and
$x\cdot g=x'\cdot g$; and for $y\in Y(c)$, $y\cdot g_1=f_a(z)$ for some $z$,
so $y\cdot g=f(z\cdot g_2)$. Thus $f$ is locally bijective along $D_A$, and
$f\in W_A$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(ii). Conversely $\mathrm{id}_a\in
D_A(-,a)$ for $a\in A$, so local bijectivity along $D_A$ gives $f_a$
bijective. A morphism of $D_AD_B$ is $d\circ e$ with $e$ factoring through
some $b\in B$ and $d$ through some $a\in A$, i.e.\ a morphism factoring
through a chain $b\to a$; iterating gives the description of $(D_AD_B)^k$,
and a morphism through $c\in A\cap B$ factors through the chain
$c\to c\to\cdots\to c$ of identities. The description of the join is
Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal}.
(b) If $(D_AD_B)^N\subseteq D_{A\cap B}$ then
$(D_AD_B)^N\subseteq D_{A\cap B}\subseteq(D_AD_B)^{N+1}\subseteq(D_AD_B)^N$,
so the chain stabilises at $I'=D_{A\cap B}$ and Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal}
gives $W_A\vee W_B=W_{J_{I'}}=W_{A\cap B}$. Conversely, $D_{A\cap B}\subseteq
D_A\cap D_B$ gives $J_{D_{A\cap B}}\ge K:=J_{D_A}\vee J_{D_B}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(i), hence $W_{A\cap B}\supseteq W_K\supseteq
W_A\vee W_B$. If $W_A\vee W_B=W_{A\cap B}$, then $W_A\vee W_B=W_K$, the chain
stabilises at some $I'=(D_AD_B)^m$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal}, and
$W_{J_{I'}}=W_K=W_{J_{D_{A\cap B}}}$. A topology is determined by the class
of maps inverted by its sheafification, since the sheaves are the objects
local with respect to that class (a reflective subcategory is recovered from
the maps its reflector inverts) and subtoposes of $\Presh(S)$ correspond
bijectively to topologies \cite[Chap.~V]{MLM}; so $J_{I'}=J_{D_{A\cap B}}$,
$I'=D_{A\cap B}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(i), and $N=m$ works.
(c) In a poset, $(n\le m)\in(D_AD_B)^k$ iff there are $n\le b_1\le a_1\le
\cdots\le b_k\le a_k\le m$ with $b_i\in B$, $a_i\in A$; if the interval
$[n,m]$ is disjoint from $A\cap B$, an equality $b_i=a_i$ or $a_i=b_{i+1}$
would produce a point of $A\cap B$, so the chain is strict. Hence
$(D_AD_B)^k\setminus D_{A\cap B}$ consists of the pairs $n\le m$ with $[n,m]$
disjoint from $A\cap B$ and containing a strictly alternating chain with
$2k$ elements. If these chains have at most $L$ elements, then
$(D_AD_B)^k=D_{A\cap B}$ for $2k>L$. If their sizes are unbounded,
$(D_AD_B)^k\ne D_{A\cap B}$ for every $k$, while $\bigcap_k(D_AD_B)^k=
D_{A\cap B}$ because a chain with more elements than the finite interval
$[n,m]$ is not strict; a descending chain whose members all differ from its
intersection does not stabilise, and Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal} gives the
strict inclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{ex}
On $P=(\mathbb N,\le)$ let $A$ be the even numbers and $B$ the odd numbers
together with the multiples of $4$, so $A\cap B=4\mathbb N$. An interval
disjoint from $4\mathbb N$ lies in some $[4j+1,4j+3]$, whose longest strictly
alternating chain is $4j+1<4j+2<4j+3$; so $L=3$, $(D_AD_B)^2=D_{4\mathbb N}$,
and a map of presheaves on $\mathbb N$ lies in the join of ``bijective at the
even points'' and ``bijective at the odd points and at the multiples of $4$''
if and only if it is bijective at the multiples of $4$.
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}\label{ex:pro}
On $P=(\mathbb N,\le)$ let $A$ be the even and $B$ the odd numbers. A presheaf
is a tower $\cdots\to X(2)\to X(1)\to X(0)$ of sets, and
$(D_AD_B)^k(-,c)$ consists of the $n\le c$ with $c-n\ge2k-1$, or $\ge2k$
when $n$ is even. By Proposition~\ref{prop:objects}(a), $W_A\vee W_B$ is the
class of maps of towers $f\colon X\to Y$ that are \emph{bijective up to a
shift}: for some $s$, $f_c(x)=f_c(x')$ implies $x|_{c-s}=x'|_{c-s}$, and every
$y\in Y(c)$ has $y|_{c-s}\in\im f_{c-s}$ (for $c\ge s$; for $c<s$ the
conditions are void). Such a map becomes an isomorphism of pro-objects: it
has the inverse up to shift $g_n\colon Y(n+2s)\to X(n)$, $y\mapsto x|_n$ for
any $x\in X(n+s)$ with $f(x)=y|_{n+s}$, which is well defined and compatible
with restriction. The converse fails: a pro-isomorphism of towers only needs,
for each $n$, \emph{some} later stage $m(n)$ at which the inverse is defined,
and the join requires the uniform bound $m(n)=n+2s$. For instance let
$X(n)=\{0,1\}$ for all $n$, with the restriction $X(n+1)\to X(n)$ constant
with value $0$ when $n$ is a power of $2$ and the identity otherwise, and let
$f\colon X\to1$ be the map to the terminal tower. The section $1\to X$
picking $0$ is a pro-inverse, since for every $n$ the composite restriction
from some later stage to $n$ is constant; but on an interval
$[n,n+s]$ containing no power of $2$ the two elements of $X(n+s)$ are
identified by $f$ and remain distinct at $n$, so $f$ is bijective up to no
uniform shift and $f\notin W_A\vee W_B$ (although $f\in W_K$, as $W_K$ is the
class of all maps). So in $\Same(\Presh(\mathbb N))$ the join of ``bijective
at the even points'' and ``bijective at the odd points'' is a uniform
version of pro-isomorphism, strictly between the two classes and the
pro-isomorphisms. The obstruction $\lim_{\mathbb N^{\mathrm{op}}}$ used in
\cite[Thm.~7.1]{Topjoin} inverts every pro-isomorphism, and more (the
projection $\{m\ge n\}\times\{0,1\}\to\{m\ge n\}$ has empty limits on both
sides and is no pro-isomorphism), so it separates the join from $W_K$
without describing it: $W_A\vee W_B\subsetneq W_{\text{pro-iso}}\subsetneq
W_{\lim}\subseteq W_K$.
\end{ex}

\begin{rem}
For a set $A$ of objects, the restriction functor along the full inclusion
$i\colon A\hookrightarrow S$ preserves limits and has a fully faithful right
adjoint, the right Kan extension $\mathrm{Ran}_i$ (and a left adjoint, the
left Kan extension). Its essential image is a reflective subcategory whose
reflector inverts exactly $W_A$, hence it is the subcategory of
$J_{D_A}$-sheaves: $\Sh(S,J_{D_A})\simeq\Presh(A)$, an essential
subtopos of $\Presh(S)$ in the sense of \cite{KL89}. The meet of the
subtoposes $\Presh(A)$ and $\Presh(B)$ is the subtopos of
$(J_{D_A}\vee J_{D_B})$-sheaves. Proposition~\ref{prop:objects}(b) says that
$W_A\vee W_B=W_{A\cap B}$ iff this meet is equivalent to $\Presh(A\cap B)$
\emph{and} is reached by a bounded alternation; the first condition alone
does not suffice: for the even and the odd numbers on $\mathbb N$ the meet is
equivalent to the degenerate subtopos $\Presh(\emptyset)$, yet the join of
the samenesses is not $W_\emptyset$ but the class of maps bijective up to a
uniform shift.
\end{rem}

\section{Remarks and questions}\label{sec:rem}

\begin{rem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} holds in every elementary topos. Theorem~\ref{thm:B}
uses, for the identification of the closure operator of $j\vee j'$ with the
transfinite iterate, that subobject lattices are frames and unions are
pullback-stable, and for the necessity of a uniform $k$ a countable
coproduct of witnesses; in an elementary topos one still has, for each
fixed $k$, that $W_{j\vee j'}\subseteq U_k$ iff $c^{(k)}$ is the closure
operator of $j\vee j'$, by the same proof.
\end{rem}

\begin{que}
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} reduces join preservation to the identity
$(jj')^k=j\vee j'$ on $\Omega$. Which pairs of topologies on a given infinite
category satisfy it? For ideal topologies this is the chain condition of
Corollary~\ref{cor:ideal} and for object-generated ideals the zigzag condition
of Proposition~\ref{prop:objects}; a structural description for topologies
that are not of ideal type, for instance in terms of the two subtoposes,
is open.
\end{que}
