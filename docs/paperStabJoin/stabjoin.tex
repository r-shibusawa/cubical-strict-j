\title[Joins of ideal topologies and the sameness lattice]{Joins of Grothendieck topologies of ideal type in the lattice of two-out-of-three classes: the stabilisation criterion}
\author{Ryota Shibusawa}
\thanks{Corresponding author. E-mail: \texttt{r-shibusawa@daiichi-koudai.ac.jp}}
\address{Daiichi Institute of Technology}
\email{r-shibusawa@daiichi-koudai.ac.jp}
\keywords{Grothendieck topology, idempotent ideal, sheafification, local isomorphism,
  two-out-of-three, separated presheaf, plus construction, join of topologies}
\subjclass[2020]{18F10 (primary), 18F20, 18A32, 20M12 (secondary)}

\begin{abstract}
For a small category $S$, every idempotent two-sided ideal $D$ of morphisms
defines a Grothendieck topology $J_D$, covering sieves being those containing
$D(-,c)$, and every topology $J$ defines the class $W_J$ of maps of presheaves
inverted by sheafification, an element of the complete lattice
$\Same(\Presh(S))$ of classes of morphisms closed under composition and
two-out-of-three. The map $J\mapsto W_J$ preserves all meets; it preserves the
join of two topologies on every finite category, but not on the poset of
natural numbers. We prove the exact criterion for topologies of ideal type on
an arbitrary small category: $W_{J_D\vee J_E}=W_{J_D}\vee W_{J_E}$ if and only
if the descending chain of alternating ideal products $DE\supseteq DEDE
\supseteq\cdots$ stabilises. When it stabilises at $I'$, the join topology is
$J_{I'}$ and the proof of the finite case goes through without finiteness;
when it does not, a presheaf obtained by gluing two copies of a representable
along a sieve, one for each level of the chain, is inverted by the join
sheafification but by no bounded alternation of the two local operators. The
join of the two classes is contained in the class of maps that are locally
bijective along some power $(DE)^k$, a uniform version of locality for the
join topology, and the two agree in the stabilising case. For ideals
generated by two classes of objects $A,B$ the criterion reads: the join of the
classes of maps bijective at the objects of $A$, respectively of $B$, is the
class of maps bijective at the objects of $A\cap B$ if and only if every
morphism factoring through a sufficiently long alternating zigzag of objects
of $B$ and $A$ factors through an object of $A\cap B$; on a locally finite
poset, if and only if the strictly alternating chains avoiding $A\cap B$ have
bounded length.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $S$ be a small category and $\Presh(S)$ its category of presheaves. A
Grothendieck topology $J$ on $S$ has a sheafification functor $a_J$, and the
class $W_J$ of maps inverted by $a_J$, the $J$-local isomorphisms, is a
\emph{sameness}: a class of morphisms containing the identities and closed
under composition and two-out-of-three. Samenesses form a complete lattice
$\Same(\Presh(S))$ under inclusion, meets being intersections and the join of
two classes the least sameness containing both \cite{Same}. The assignment
$J\mapsto W_J$ preserves all meets \cite[Thm.~3.1]{Topjoin}, and the question
studied in \cite{Topjoin,Finitejoin} is whether it preserves binary joins:
whether every map inverted by the sheafification of $J\vee J'$ is obtained
from $J$-local and $J'$-local isomorphisms by finitely many compositions and
cancellations. The answer was known to be yes for every finite category
\cite{Finitejoin} and no for the poset $\mathbb N$ \cite[Thm.~7.1]{Topjoin}.
This paper gives the exact criterion for topologies of \emph{ideal type}.

A two-sided ideal $D$ of $S$ is a set of morphisms closed under composition
with arbitrary morphisms on either side; it is \emph{idempotent} if every
member is a composite of two members. Every idempotent ideal $D$ defines a
topology $J_D$ whose covering sieves on $c$ are the sieves containing
$D(-,c)=\{a\colon x\to c\text{ in }D\}$ \cite[Thm.~5.1]{Postnikov}; on a finite
category every topology is of this form (Kelly--Lawvere \cite{KL89},
\cite{Marques25}), on an infinite one not necessarily. For ideals $D,E$ write
$DE=\{d\circ e:d\in D,e\in E\}$, again an ideal, and
$(DE)^k=DE\cdots DE$ ($k$ factors of each); the chain
$DE\supseteq(DE)^2\supseteq\cdots$ decreases, since $DE\subseteq D\cap E$.

\begin{thm}\label{thm:main}
Let $S$ be a small category and $D,E$ idempotent ideals of $S$. Then
\[
W_{J_D\vee J_E}=W_{J_D}\vee W_{J_E}\quad\text{in }\Same(\Presh(S))
\]
if and only if the chain $(DE)^k$ stabilises, i.e.\ $(DE)^m=(DE)^{m+1}$ for
some $m$. In that case $I':=(DE)^m$ is an idempotent ideal with
$(ED)^{m+1}=I'$, $J_D\vee J_E=J_{I'}$, and $W_{J_D}\vee W_{J_E}=W_{J_{I'}}$.
\end{thm}

On a finite category the chain stabilises for every pair, which recovers
\cite{Finitejoin}; on $\mathbb N$ with the ideals of the even and of the odd
points it is strictly decreasing, which recovers \cite[Thm.~7.1]{Topjoin}.
The criterion is the same as for nuclei on a Heyting algebra and their joins
in the sameness lattice of fuzzy sets \cite{Fuzzysame}, and as the
finite-iteration axiom of the lax logic of the join of two nuclei
\cite{Laxjoin}: in all three settings the join of two reflections is generated
at the level of inverted maps exactly when the alternation of the two
reflections reaches the join in a uniformly bounded number of steps.

The proof has two halves. For the stabilising case, the argument of
\cite{Finitejoin}, which used finiteness through the stabilisation of the
chain and through a description of ideals by objects of the Cauchy
completion, is rewritten without the second ingredient: the idempotency of
$I'$ replaces the identities of generating objects. For the non-stabilising
case we introduce, for each $k$, the class $U_k$ of maps that are
\emph{locally bijective along $(DE)^k$}, show that the union $U=\bigcup_kU_k$
is a sameness containing $W_{J_D}$ and $W_{J_E}$, and exhibit, from a
morphism in $(DE)^k\setminus(DE)^{k+1}$, a map that is locally bijective
along $(DE)^{k+1}$ but not along $(DE)^k$: the fold map of two copies of a
representable glued along the sieve $(DE)^{k+1}(-,c)$. The coproduct of these
maps over all $k$ is inverted by the join sheafification but lies in no $U_k$,
hence not in the join of the two classes.

The criterion becomes concrete for ideals generated by objects. For a set $A$
of objects, the ideal $D_A$ of morphisms factoring through $A$ is idempotent,
and the class $W_A=W_{J_{D_A}}$ consists of the maps of presheaves that are
bijective at every object of $A$: it is the class inverted by restriction to
$A$, the sheafification for the essential subtopos $\Presh(A)\hookrightarrow
\Presh(S)$. Two such classes $W_A,W_B$ have the obvious upper bound
$W_{A\cap B}$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:objects} applies
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to decide when the bound is attained: $W_A\vee W_B=
W_{A\cap B}$ iff every morphism factoring through a long enough alternating
zigzag of objects of $B$ and $A$ factors through an object of $A\cap B$; on a
locally finite poset, iff the strictly alternating chains avoiding $A\cap B$
have bounded length. The question is natural because ``isomorphism on a
class of objects'' is the most elementary sameness on a presheaf category,
and the answer shows that the join of two of them is in general not of the
same kind, even when the corresponding subtoposes meet in $\Presh(A\cap B)$.

\subsection*{What is known.}
Samenesses and $W_J$ are from \cite{Same}; meets of topologies always go to
joins of samenesses and the $\mathbb N$ counterexample are from
\cite{Topjoin}; the finite case, with the alternating separated quotients,
the plus tower and the retraction of representing bimodules, is
\cite{Finitejoin}. Ideal topologies, the correspondence with idempotent
ideals on finite categories and the local operators $R\mapsto\{a:a\cdot D
\subseteq R\}$ are in \cite{KL89,Marques25,Postnikov}. Local isomorphisms as
locally injective and locally surjective maps are standard
\cite[III.7]{MLM}.

\subsection*{What is new.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: the necessary and sufficient condition on an
arbitrary small category, which decides the question for every pair of
ideal topologies, whereas \cite{Finitejoin} proves the positive answer under
the finiteness hypothesis; in particular the failure direction, with the
uniform locality classes $U_k$ (Section~\ref{sec:uniform}) and the glued
representables as witnesses (Section~\ref{sec:fail}); the object-free
form of the stabilising direction (Section~\ref{sec:stab}); and the
specialisation to ideals generated by objects, Proposition~\ref{prop:objects},
where the join of ``bijective on $A$'' and ``bijective on $B$'' is compared
with ``bijective on $A\cap B$''.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

Presheaves are functors $S^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}$; for $a\colon x\to c$
and $v\in X(c)$ we write $v\cdot a:=X(a)v$. A sieve on $c$ is a set of
morphisms into $c$ closed under precomposition, i.e.\ a subpresheaf of the
representable $y(c)=S(-,c)$. For an ideal $A$ the sets $A(-,c)$ are sieves
and $A(-,c)\cdot b\subseteq A(-,x)$ for $b\colon x\to c$.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:ideal}
Let $D,E$ be idempotent ideals.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $J_D$ is a topology, $D(-,c)$ is its least covering sieve on $c$,
and $J_D\le J_E$ iff $D\supseteq E$.
\item[(ii)] A map $f\colon X\to Y$ is in $W_{J_D}$ iff it is
\emph{locally bijective along $D$}: for all $c$, if $f(x)=f(x')$ for
$x,x'\in X(c)$ then $x\cdot a=x'\cdot a$ for all $a\in D(-,c)$, and for all
$y\in Y(c)$ and $a\in D(-,c)$, $y\cdot a\in\im f$.
\item[(iii)] For every $k$ and $c$ the sieve $(DE)^k(-,c)$ is
$(J_D\vee J_E)$-covering.
\item[(iv)] If $(DE)^m=(DE)^{m+1}=:I'$ then $I'$ is idempotent,
$I'\subseteq D\cap E$, $DI'=I'D=EI'=I'E=I'$, $(ED)^{m+1}=I'$, every
alternating product of at least $2(m+1)$ factors $D,E$, in either order,
equals $I'$, and $J_D\vee J_E=J_{I'}$. (The chain $(ED)^k$ need not have
stabilised at index $m$: Example~\ref{ex:ED}.)
\end{enumerate}
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
(i) is \cite[Thm.~5.1]{Postnikov}: the maximal sieve contains $D(-,c)$;
pullback of a sieve containing $D(-,c)$ along $b\colon x\to c$ contains
$D(-,x)$ because $D$ is a left ideal; and if $R\supseteq D(-,c)$ and each
$a^*R'$ with $a\in R$ contains $D(-,\operatorname{dom}a)$, then for
$d\in D(-,c)$, $d=d_1d_2$ with $d_i\in D$ by idempotency, $d_1\in R$ and
$d_2\in d_1^*R'$, so $d\in R'$. (ii) is the description of local
isomorphisms as locally injective and locally surjective maps
\cite[III.7, Cor.~6]{MLM}, with the sieves of the definition replaced by the
least covering sieve. (iii) Let $K=J_D\vee J_E$. The sieve $D(-,c)$ is
$K$-covering, and for $d\in D(-,c)$ the pullback $d^*\bigl((DE)(-,c)\bigr)$
contains $E(-,\operatorname{dom}d)$; by transitivity $(DE)(-,c)$ is
$K$-covering. Inductively, $(DE)^k(-,c)$ is $K$-covering: it is covered by
$(DE)(-,c)$ relative to the sieves $(DE)^{k-1}(-,x)$. (iv) $I'I'=(DE)^{2m}
=I'$; $I'\subseteq DE\subseteq D\cap E$; $DI'=DD\,E(DE)^{m-1}=(DE)^m=I'$ as
$DD=D$, and similarly $I'E=I'$; $I'D\supseteq I'I'=I'$ as $I'\subseteq D$, and $I'D\subseteq I'$ as $I'$
is a right ideal, so $I'D=I'$, and likewise $EI'=I'$; then $(ED)^{m+1}=E(DE)^mD=EI'D=I'$.
An alternating product of at least $2(m+1)$ factors contains $(DE)^{m+1}$ or
$(ED)^{m+1}$ as a block of consecutive factors; the block equals $I'$, and
multiplying $I'$ by $D$ or $E$ on either side returns $I'$. For the
join, $J_{I'}\ge J_D,J_E$ since $I'\subseteq D,E$, and any topology above
$J_D$ and $J_E$ has $I'(-,c)=(DE)^m(-,c)$ covering by (iii), hence is above
$J_{I'}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}[join theorem, {\cite[Thm.~4.1]{Postnikov}}]\label{thm:join}
Let $U,V$ be samenesses on a category $C$ whose join contains the
isomorphisms, and let $P\colon C\to C$ be a functor with a natural
transformation $\pi\colon\mathrm{id}\Rightarrow P$ such that $\pi_X\in U\vee V$
for all $X$ and $P$ inverts $U$ and $V$. Then $U\vee V=W_P$, the class of
maps inverted by $P$.
\end{thm}

\section{Uniform locality along the chain}\label{sec:uniform}

Fix idempotent ideals $D,E$, write $K=J_D\vee J_E$ and
$V=W_{J_D}\vee W_{J_E}$.

\begin{defi}
For $k\ge1$ let $U_k$ be the class of maps $f\colon X\to Y$ that are locally
bijective along the ideal $(DE)^k$ in the sense of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(ii): $f(x)=f(x')$ implies $x\cdot a=x'\cdot a$ for all
$a\in(DE)^k(-,c)$, and $y\cdot a\in\im f$ for all $y\in Y(c)$ and
$a\in(DE)^k(-,c)$. Put $U=\bigcup_kU_k$.
\end{defi}

\begin{lem}\label{lem:U}
$U_k\subseteq U_{k+1}$, $U$ is a sameness, $W_{J_D}\cup W_{J_E}\subseteq U_1$,
and $U\subseteq W_K$. Hence $V\subseteq U\subseteq W_K$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
$U_k\subseteq U_{k+1}$ because $(DE)^{k+1}\subseteq(DE)^k$. Identities lie in
every $U_k$. Let $h\colon X\to Y$ and $g\colon Y\to Z$.

\emph{Composition.} If $h\in U_k$ and $g\in U_l$ then $gh\in U_{k+l}$: every
$a\in(DE)^{k+l}(-,c)$ is $a=be$ with $b\in(DE)^l$, $e\in(DE)^k$. If
$gh(x)=gh(x')$ then $h(x)\cdot b=h(x')\cdot b$, i.e.\ $h(x\cdot b)=h(x'\cdot b)$,
so $x\cdot be=x'\cdot be$. For $z\in Z(c)$, $z\cdot b=g(y)$ for some $y$, and
$y\cdot e=h(x)$ for some $x$, so $z\cdot a=gh(x)$.

\emph{Cancellation of $g$.} If $gh\in U_k$ and $g\in U_k$ then $h\in U_{2k}$.
Local injectivity of $h$ along $(DE)^k$ follows from that of $gh$. For
$y\in Y(c)$ and $a=be$ with $b,e\in(DE)^k$: $g(y)\cdot b=gh(x)$ for some $x$,
so $g(y\cdot b)=g(h(x))$ and, $g$ being locally injective along $(DE)^k$,
$y\cdot be=h(x)\cdot e\in\im h$.

\emph{Cancellation of $h$.} If $gh\in U_k$ and $h\in U_k$ then $g\in U_{2k}$.
Local surjectivity of $g$ along $(DE)^k$ follows from that of $gh$. If
$g(y)=g(y')$ and $a=be$ with $b,e\in(DE)^k$, choose $x,x'$ with
$h(x)=y\cdot b$ and $h(x')=y'\cdot b$; then $gh(x)=g(y)\cdot b=g(y')\cdot b
=gh(x')$, so $x\cdot e=x'\cdot e$ and $y\cdot a=h(x\cdot e)=h(x'\cdot e)
=y'\cdot a$.

Hence $U$ is a sameness. $W_{J_D}\subseteq U_1$: a $J_D$-local isomorphism
is locally bijective along $D\supseteq DE$. $W_{J_E}\subseteq U_1$: $DE\subseteq
E$ because $E$ is a left ideal. Finally $U_k\subseteq W_K$: a map in $U_k$ is
$K$-locally injective and $K$-locally surjective because $(DE)^k(-,c)$ is
$K$-covering (Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(iii)).
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
$U$ is the class of maps that are $K$-local isomorphisms \emph{uniformly}:
the covering sieves witnessing local injectivity and surjectivity can be
taken to be $(DE)^k(-,c)$ for one $k$ independent of the element. $W_K$ only
requires, for each element, a $K$-covering sieve, and $K$-covering sieves are
built from the sieves $(DE)^k(-,c)$ by transitivity with unbounded $k$.
\end{rem}

\section{Failure without stabilisation}\label{sec:fail}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:witness}
Suppose $a\in(DE)^k\setminus(DE)^{k+1}$, $a\colon x\to c$. Let $X$ be the
presheaf obtained from two copies $y(c)^{(1)},y(c)^{(2)}$ of the representable
by identifying $b^{(1)}$ with $b^{(2)}$ for every $b\in(DE)^{k+1}(-,c)$, and
let $f\colon X\to y(c)$ be the fold map. Then $f\in U_{k+1}\setminus U_k$.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
The identification is compatible with restriction because $(DE)^{k+1}(-,c)$
is a sieve, so $X$ is a presheaf, the pushout of $y(c)\leftarrow(DE)^{k+1}(-,c)
\rightarrow y(c)$, and $f$ is a map of presheaves, surjective. If $f(u)=f(u')$
with $u\ne u'$ then $u=b^{(1)}$, $u'=b^{(2)}$ for one $b\notin(DE)^{k+1}$, and
for $e\in(DE)^{k+1}(-,\operatorname{dom}b)$ we have $u\cdot e=(be)^{(1)}
=(be)^{(2)}=u'\cdot e$ since $be\in(DE)^{k+1}$. So $f\in U_{k+1}$. On the
other hand $\mathrm{id}_c\notin(DE)^{k+1}$, for otherwise
$a=\mathrm{id}_c\circ a\in(DE)^{k+1}$; so $\mathrm{id}_c^{(1)}\ne
\mathrm{id}_c^{(2)}$ in $X(c)$, both map to $\mathrm{id}_c$, and their
restrictions along $a\in(DE)^k(-,c)$ are $a^{(1)}\ne a^{(2)}$. Hence
$f\notin U_k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, failure direction]
Suppose the chain does not stabilise, so that $(DE)^k\supsetneq(DE)^{k+1}$
for every $k$ (once two consecutive terms agree, all later ones do). For
each $k$ choose $a_k\in(DE)^k\setminus(DE)^{k+1}$ and let $f_k\colon X_k\to
y(c_k)$ be the map of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness}. Let
$F=\coprod_kf_k\colon\coprod_kX_k\to\coprod_ky(c_k)$. Then $F\in W_K$: it is
surjective, and if $F(u)=F(u')$ then $u,u'$ lie in the same summand $X_k$
and agree on the $K$-covering sieve $(DE)^{k+1}(-,c)$. But $F\notin U_k$ for
every $k$: a map in $U_k$ has all its coproduct components in $U_k$, since
restrictions, images and identifications of elements stay within a summand,
and the component $f_k$ is not in $U_k$. So $F\in W_K\setminus U\subseteq
W_K\setminus V$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:U}, and $V\ne W_K$.
\end{proof}

\section{The stabilising case}\label{sec:stab}

Assume now $(DE)^m=(DE)^{m+1}=I'$ and put $r:=m+1$, so that by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(iv) $I'$ is idempotent, $(DE)^r=(ED)^r=I'$, every
product of at least $2r$ alternating factors $D,E$ in either order equals
$I'$, and $K=J_{I'}$. (The shift from $m$ to $r$ is needed: $(ED)^m$ may be
strictly larger than $I'$, Example~\ref{ex:ED}.) We show $V=W_{J_{I'}}$ by
the route of \cite{Finitejoin}, replacing the generating objects used there
by the idempotency of $I'$.

\subsection*{Separated quotients.}
For an ideal $A$ and $x,x'\in X(c)$ write $x\approx_Ax'$ if $x\cdot a=x'\cdot a$
for all $a\in A(-,c)$; this is a congruence of presheaves since $A$ is a left
ideal, and $Q_AX:=X/{\approx_A}$. The quotient map $q\colon X\to Q_AX$ is
locally bijective along $A$: it is surjective, and $q(x)=q(x')$ means exactly
that $x,x'$ agree on $A(-,c)$. For $A=D$ idempotent, $q\in W_{J_D}$.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:quotient}
Let $D_1=D$, $D_2=E$, $D_3=D,\dots$, $X_0=X$ and $X_{k+1}=Q_{D_{k+1}}X_k$.
Two elements of $X(c)$ have the same image in $X_k$ iff they agree on
$(D_k\cdots D_1)(-,c)$; hence $X_{2r}=Q_{I'}X$, the composite
$X\twoheadrightarrow Q_{I'}X$ lies in $V$, and $Q_{I'}X$ is $J_{I'}$-separated.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
Induction on $k$: $x,x'$ have the same image in $X_{k+1}$ iff $x\cdot b$ and
$x'\cdot b$ have the same image in $X_k$ for all $b\in D_{k+1}(-,c)$, i.e.\
iff $x\cdot ba=x'\cdot ba$ for all such $b$ and all
$a\in(D_k\cdots D_1)(-,\operatorname{dom}b)$, and these $ba$ are the
elements of $(D_{k+1}D_k\cdots D_1)(-,c)$. For $k=2r$ this ideal is
$(ED)^r=I'$.
Each quotient map lies in $W_{J_{D_{k+1}}}\subseteq V$. If two elements of
$Q_{I'}X(c)$ agree on $I'(-,c)$, their representatives $x,x'$ satisfy
$x\cdot a\approx_{I'}x'\cdot a$ for $a\in I'(-,c)$, i.e.\ $x\cdot ab=x'\cdot ab$
for $a,b\in I'$; as $I'=I'I'$ this says $x\approx_{I'}x'$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The plus tower.}
An $S$--$S$-bimodule is a functor $S^{\mathrm{op}}\times S\to\mathbf{Set}$;
ideals are sub-bimodules of $\Hom_S$, and bimodules have a tensor product
$(A\otimes B)(x,z)=\coprod_yA(y,z)\times B(x,y)/\sim$, with
$\Hom(A\otimes B,X)\cong\Hom(A,\Hom(B,X))$ for the presheaf
$\Hom(A,X)(c)=\Hom_{\Presh(S)}(A(-,c),X)$. Since $D(-,c)$ is the least
$J_D$-covering sieve, the plus construction for $J_D$ is $X^{+D}=\Hom(D,X)$
with unit $x\mapsto(d\mapsto x\cdot d)$, and the alternating plus tower is
$\Phi_kX=\Hom(T_k,X)$, $T_k=D_k\otimes\cdots\otimes D_1$, with unit
$u_k\colon X\to\Phi_kX$ a composite of maps of $W_{J_D}\cup W_{J_E}$; and
$a_{I'}X=X^{+I'+I'}=\Hom(P,X)$, $P=I'\otimes I'$, with unit $\eta$. None of
this uses objects of $S$. The two bimodule lemmas of \cite[Lemmas~2.2,
2.3]{Finitejoin} are reproved here in the present generality.

\begin{lem}\label{lem:bimod}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The map $m_k\colon P\otimes T_k\to P$,
$[i,i',d_k,\dots,d_1]\mapsto[i,\,i'd_k\cdots d_1]$, is a well-defined map of
bimodules, $m_k^*=a_{I'}(u_k)$ under
$a_{I'}\Phi_kX=\Hom(P,\Hom(T_k,X))=\Hom(P\otimes T_k,X)$, and $m_k$ is an
isomorphism.
\item[(b)] Let $k\ge4r$ and $2r\le j\le k-2r$. The map $r_k\colon T_k\to P$,
$[d_k,\dots,d_1]\mapsto[d_k\cdots d_{j+1},\ d_j\cdots d_1]$, is a well-defined
map of bimodules, and $r_k\circ\varepsilon_k=m_k$, where
$\varepsilon_k\colon P\otimes T_k\to T_k$, $[p,t]\mapsto\bar p\cdot t$
($\bar p=ii'$ for $p=[i,i']$), is the bimodule map inducing the
sheafification unit $\Phi_kX\to a_{I'}\Phi_kX$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the comparison $\iota_k:=(m_k^*)^{-1}\circ\varepsilon_k^*\colon
\Phi_kX\to a_{I'}X$, the sheafification unit of $\Phi_kX$ followed by
$(a_{I'}u_k)^{-1}$, satisfies $\iota_k\circ r_k^*=\mathrm{id}$, so is
surjective for $k\ge4r$, and $\iota_k\circ u_k=\eta_X$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
(a) The value $i'd_k\cdots d_1$ lies in $I'$ because $I'$ is a right ideal;
a tensor move inside $[d_k,\dots,d_1]$ or between $i$ and $i'$ does not change
the image, and a move $[i,i'n,d_k,\dots]\sim[i,i',nd_k,\dots]$ gives
$[i,i'nd_k\cdots d_1]$ either way. Compatibility with the actions of $S$ is
clear. For $\psi\in\Hom(P(-,c),X)$, the element $a_{I'}(u_k)(\psi)=u_k\circ
\psi$ sends $p$ to $(t\mapsto\psi(p)\cdot\bar t)=(t\mapsto\psi(p\cdot\bar t))$,
and $p\cdot\bar t=m_k[p,t]$; so $a_{I'}(u_k)=m_k^*$. Since
$u_k\in V\subseteq W_{J_{I'}}$, $a_{I'}(u_k)$ is bijective for every $X$ and
$c$, hence $m_k(-,c)$ is an isomorphism of presheaves by the Yoneda lemma,
for every $c$. (b) The two blocks $d_k\cdots d_{j+1}$ and $d_j\cdots d_1$ are
alternating products of $k-j\ge2r$ and $j\ge2r$ factors, hence lie in $I'$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(iv). A tensor move inside a block does not change
its product, and a move across the boundary,
$[\dots,d_{j+1}n,d_j,\dots]\sim[\dots,d_{j+1},nd_j,\dots]$, changes the pair
of products from $(an,b)$ to $(a,nb)$ with $a,b\in I'$, which are identified
in $P=I'\otimes I'$. So $r_k$ is well defined, and it is a bimodule map.
The unit $y\mapsto(p\mapsto y\cdot\bar p)$ of $a_{I'}$ at $Y=\Phi_kX$ sends
$\varphi$ to $(p\mapsto(t\mapsto\varphi(\bar p\cdot t)))=\varepsilon_k^*
(\varphi)$. For $p=[i,i']$ and $t=[d_k,\dots,d_1]$,
\[
r_k\varepsilon_k[p,t]=[ii'd_k\cdots d_{j+1},\,d_j\cdots d_1]
=[i,\,i'd_k\cdots d_1]=m_k[p,t],
\]
the middle equality being the tensor move of $i'd_k\cdots d_{j+1}$ across
the tensor sign of $P$, legitimate as $i$ and $d_j\cdots d_1$ lie in $I'$.
Finally $\iota_kr_k^*=(m_k^*)^{-1}(r_k\varepsilon_k)^*=(m_k^*)^{-1}m_k^*
=\mathrm{id}$, and $\iota_ku_k=\eta_X$ is the naturality square
$\eta_{\Phi_kX}\circ u_k=a_{I'}(u_k)\circ\eta_X$ of the unit $\eta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lem}\label{lem:plus}
Let $Y$ be $J_{I'}$-separated. Then $Y^{+D}$ and $Y^{+E}$ are
$J_{I'}$-separated. Hence every $\Phi_kY$ is $J_{I'}$-separated, $\iota_k$ is
injective, bijective for $k\ge4r$, and $\eta_Y\in V$.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varphi,\psi\in Y^{+D}(c)=\Hom(D(-,c),Y)$ agree on $I'(-,c)$, i.e.\
$\varphi\cdot p=\psi\cdot p$ for all $p\in I'(-,c)$, where
$(\varphi\cdot p)(n)=\varphi(pn)$ for $n\in D(-,\operatorname{dom}p)$. Let
$n\in D(-,c)$, $n\colon x\to c$, and $b\in I'(-,x)$. Then $nb\in DI'=I'=I'I'$,
so $nb=pq$ with $p,q\in I'$ and $q\in D(-,\operatorname{dom}p)$, and
\[
\varphi(n)\cdot b=\varphi(nb)=\varphi(pq)=(\varphi\cdot p)(q)=(\psi\cdot p)(q)
=\psi(nb)=\psi(n)\cdot b.
\]
As $Y$ is $J_{I'}$-separated, $\varphi(n)=\psi(n)$; so $\varphi=\psi$. The
same holds for $E$. Hence all $\Phi_kY$ are $J_{I'}$-separated, and $\iota_k$,
the sheafification unit of a separated presheaf followed by an isomorphism,
is injective \cite[III.5]{MLM}; it is surjective for $k\ge4r$ by the
section $r_k^*$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:bimod}. So $\iota_k$ is an isomorphism, and
$\eta_Y=\iota_ku_k\in V$, as isomorphisms lie in $W_{J_D}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, stabilising direction]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:join} with $U=W_{J_D}$, $V'=W_{J_E}$, $P=a_{I'}$ and
$\pi=\eta$; $a_{I'}$ inverts both classes since $J_D,J_E\le J_{I'}$. For a
presheaf $X$ the unit factors as
$X\twoheadrightarrow Q_{I'}X\xrightarrow{\eta}a_{I'}Q_{I'}X\cong a_{I'}X$,
the first map in $V$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:quotient}, the second by
Lemma~\ref{lem:plus}, and the isomorphism induced by the quotient map,
which $a_{I'}$ inverts. So $\eta_X\in V$ for all $X$, and $V=W_{a_{I'}}
=W_{J_{I'}}=W_K$.
\end{proof}

\section{Examples and remarks}\label{sec:ex}

\begin{ex}
On a finite category every chain of ideals stabilises, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} contains \cite[Thm.~1.1]{Finitejoin}, with the bound
$2r$ quotients and $4r$ plus constructions, $r=m+1$ (in \cite{Finitejoin}
the index is chosen so that both chains $(DE)^k$ and $(ED)^k$ have
stabilised, which is the same bound). On $S=(\mathbb N,\le)$ with
$D$ and $E$ the ideals of arrows $n\to m$ passing through an even,
respectively an odd, point, $(DE)^k$ consists of the arrows $n\to m$ admitting
$k$ alternating even and odd points between $n$ and $m$, a strictly
decreasing chain with empty intersection; the join topology is degenerate
and $W_K$ is everything, while $V$ is not \cite[Thm.~7.1]{Topjoin}. The
witnesses of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} are here the fold maps of two
copies of $y(c)$ glued below $c-2k-1$, and their coproduct is a variant of the
presheaf $n\mapsto\{x\ge n\}$ used there.
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}\label{ex:ED}
The two chains $(DE)^k$ and $(ED)^k$ need not stabilise at the same index.
Let $S$ be the category with two objects $0,1$ and one non-identity arrow
$a\colon0\to1$, and let $D=\{\mathrm{id}_0,a\}$, $E=\{\mathrm{id}_1,a\}$ be the
ideals of arrows factoring through $0$, respectively through $1$; both are
idempotent. Then $DE=\emptyset$ (no arrow of $E$ has codomain $0$), while
$ED=\{a\}$. So $(DE)^1=(DE)^2=\emptyset=I'$, $m=1$, but
$(ED)^1=\{a\}\ne I'$, and only $(ED)^2=\emptyset=I'$. Accordingly, for the
constant presheaf $X$ with value a two-element set, the second separated
quotient $Q_EQ_DX=Q_{ED}X$ still has two elements at $1$, whereas
$Q_{I'}X=Q_\emptyset X$ is terminal: the bound $2m=2$ of quotients is
insufficient, and Lemma~\ref{lem:quotient} uses the bound $2r=4$ (here three
quotients already suffice, as $DED=\emptyset$; $2r$ is a bound, not the
minimum). The join $J_D\vee J_E=J_\emptyset$ is the degenerate
topology, as the empty sieve on $0$ is $J_E$-covering and the sieve $\{a\}$
on $1$ is $J_D$-covering.
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}
For a monoid $M$, viewed as a category with one object, ideals are the
two-sided ideals of $M$ and the criterion is the stabilisation of the
alternating products $DE\supseteq DEDE\supseteq\cdots$ in the quantale of
ideals of $M$. Every finite monoid satisfies it, with $m$ at most the number
of elements; \cite[\S7]{Finitejoin} gives monoids of order $8$ and $12$ on
which the plus tower alone does not stabilise although the chain does. For
infinite monoids the chain can decrease forever, and then
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} says that the join of the two classes of local
isomorphisms of $M$-sets is strictly smaller than the class for the join
topology.
\end{ex}

\subsection*{Ideals generated by objects.}
For a set $A$ of objects of $S$ let $D_A$ be the set of morphisms factoring
through an object of $A$. It is an ideal, idempotent since $f=g\circ h$ with
$h\colon x\to a$, $g\colon a\to y$ has $g,h\in D_A$; on a finite category
every idempotent ideal is of this form after Cauchy completion
\cite[\S2]{Finitejoin}, but not in general: on $(\mathbb Q,\le)$ the ideal of
strict inequalities is idempotent and contains no identity. Write
$W_A:=W_{J_{D_A}}$.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:objects}
Let $A,B$ be sets of objects of $S$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $W_A$ is the class of maps $f$ with $f_a$ bijective for every
$a\in A$; $(D_AD_B)^k$ is the set of morphisms factoring through a chain
$b_1\to a_1\to b_2\to\cdots\to b_k\to a_k$ with $b_i\in B$, $a_i\in A$, and
contains $D_{A\cap B}$.
\item[(b)] $W_A\vee W_B=W_{A\cap B}$ if and only if for some $N$ every
morphism factoring through such a chain of length $N$ factors through an
object of $A\cap B$, i.e.\ $(D_AD_B)^N\subseteq D_{A\cap B}$.
\item[(c)] If $S=P$ is a poset whose intervals are finite, the condition in
(b) holds if and only if the strictly alternating chains
$b_1<a_1<\cdots<b_k<a_k$ ($b_i\in B$, $a_i\in A$) contained in intervals
disjoint from $A\cap B$ have a bounded number of elements; if they do not,
the chain
$(D_AD_B)^k$ does not stabilise and $W_A\vee W_B\subsetneq
W_{J_{D_A}\vee J_{D_B}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
(a) If $f_a$ is bijective for $a\in A$, then for $x,x'\in X(c)$ with
$f(x)=f(x')$ and $g=g_1g_2\in D_A(-,c)$ with $g_1\colon a\to c$, $a\in A$, we
get $f_a(x\cdot g_1)=f_a(x'\cdot g_1)$, so $x\cdot g_1=x'\cdot g_1$ and
$x\cdot g=x'\cdot g$; and for $y\in Y(c)$, $y\cdot g_1=f_a(z)$ for some $z$,
so $y\cdot g=f(z\cdot g_2)$. Thus $f$ is locally bijective along $D_A$, and
$f\in W_A$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(ii). Conversely $\mathrm{id}_a\in
D_A(-,a)$ for $a\in A$, so local bijectivity along $D_A$ gives $f_a$
bijective. A morphism of $D_AD_B$ is $d\circ e$ with $e$ factoring through
some $b\in B$ and $d$ through some $a\in A$, i.e.\ a morphism factoring
through a chain $b\to a$; iterating gives the description of $(D_AD_B)^k$,
and a morphism through $c\in A\cap B$ factors through the chain
$c\to c\to\cdots\to c$ of identities.
(b) If $(D_AD_B)^N\subseteq D_{A\cap B}$ then
$(D_AD_B)^N\subseteq D_{A\cap B}\subseteq(D_AD_B)^{N+1}\subseteq(D_AD_B)^N$,
so the chain stabilises at $I'=D_{A\cap B}$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives
$W_A\vee W_B=W_{J_{I'}}=W_{A\cap B}$. Conversely, $D_{A\cap B}\subseteq
D_A\cap D_B$ gives $J_{D_{A\cap B}}\ge K:=J_{D_A}\vee J_{D_B}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(i), hence $W_{A\cap B}\supseteq W_K\supseteq
W_A\vee W_B$. If $W_A\vee W_B=W_{A\cap B}$, then $W_A\vee W_B=W_K$, the chain
stabilises at some $I'=(D_AD_B)^m$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and
$W_{J_{I'}}=W_K=W_{J_{D_{A\cap B}}}$. A topology is determined by the class
of maps inverted by its sheafification, since the sheaves are the objects
local with respect to that class (a reflective subcategory is recovered from
the maps its reflector inverts) and subtoposes of $\Presh(S)$ correspond
bijectively to topologies \cite[Chap.~V]{MLM}; so $J_{I'}=J_{D_{A\cap B}}$, $I'=D_{A\cap B}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ideal}(i), and $N=m$ works.
(c) In a poset, $(n\le m)\in(D_AD_B)^k$ iff there are $n\le b_1\le a_1\le
\cdots\le b_k\le a_k\le m$ with $b_i\in B$, $a_i\in A$; if the interval
$[n,m]$ is disjoint from $A\cap B$, an equality $b_i=a_i$ or $a_i=b_{i+1}$
would produce a point of $A\cap B$, so the chain is strict. Hence
$(D_AD_B)^k\setminus D_{A\cap B}$ consists of the pairs $n\le m$ with $[n,m]$
disjoint from $A\cap B$ and containing a strictly alternating chain with
$2k$ elements. If these chains have at most $L$ elements, then
$(D_AD_B)^k=D_{A\cap B}$ for $2k>L$. If their sizes are unbounded,
$(D_AD_B)^k\ne D_{A\cap B}$ for every $k$, while $\bigcap_k(D_AD_B)^k=
D_{A\cap B}$ because a chain with more elements than the finite interval
$[n,m]$ is not strict; a descending
chain whose members all differ from its intersection does not stabilise, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives the strict inclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{ex}
On $P=(\mathbb N,\le)$ let $A$ be the even numbers and $B$ the odd numbers
together with the multiples of $4$, so $A\cap B=4\mathbb N$. An interval
disjoint from $4\mathbb N$ lies in some $[4j+1,4j+3]$, whose longest strictly
alternating chain is $4j+1<4j+2<4j+3$; so $L=3$, $(D_AD_B)^2=D_{4\mathbb N}$,
and a map of presheaves on $\mathbb N$ lies in the join of ``bijective at the
even points'' and ``bijective at the odd points and at the multiples of $4$''
if and only if it is bijective at the multiples of $4$. With $B$ the odd
numbers alone, $A\cap B=\emptyset$, every interval is admissible, the chains
are unbounded, and the join is strictly smaller than $W_K$, which is the
class of all maps \cite[Thm.~7.1]{Topjoin}.
\end{ex}

\begin{rem}
For a set $A$ of objects, the restriction functor along the full inclusion
$i\colon A\hookrightarrow S$ preserves limits and has a fully faithful right
adjoint, the right Kan extension $\mathrm{Ran}_i$ (and a left adjoint, the
left Kan extension). Its essential image is a reflective subcategory whose
reflector inverts exactly $W_A$, hence it is the subcategory of
$J_{D_A}$-sheaves: $\mathrm{Sh}(S,J_{D_A})\simeq\Presh(A)$, an essential
subtopos of $\Presh(S)$ in the sense of \cite{KL89}. The meet of the
subtoposes $\Presh(A)$ and $\Presh(B)$ is the subtopos of
$(J_{D_A}\vee J_{D_B})$-sheaves. Proposition~\ref{prop:objects}(b) says that
$W_A\vee W_B=W_{A\cap B}$ iff this meet is equivalent to $\Presh(A\cap B)$ \emph{and} is
reached by a bounded alternation; the first condition alone does not
suffice: for the even and the odd numbers on $\mathbb N$ the meet is
equivalent to the degenerate subtopos $\Presh(\emptyset)$, yet the join of
the samenesses is
not $W_\emptyset$.
\end{rem}

\begin{rem}
Lemma~\ref{lem:U} and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} give three classes
$V\subseteq U\subseteq W_K$ with $V=W_K$ iff the chain stabilises, and then
$U=V$. Whether $V=U$ holds in general, so that the join of the two classes
is exactly the uniformly $K$-local maps, is open; the reduction lemma of
\cite[Lemma~4.1]{Topjoin}, which places every map inverted by $a_{J'}a_J$ in
$V$, is evidence in favour.
\end{rem}

\begin{que}
Is $W_{J_D}\vee W_{J_E}=U$ for all idempotent ideals $D,E$ on every small
category? Does the criterion extend to topologies that are not of ideal
type, with the alternation of the two local operators on $\Omega$ in place
of the ideal chain?
\end{que}
