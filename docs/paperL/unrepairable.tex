\title[Reversal is unrepairable]{Reversal is unrepairable:\\
  equivariance, elegance, and the test comparison\\ for De Morgan
  cubical sets}

\author{Ryota Shibusawa}
\address{Daiichi Institute of Technology, Japan}
\email{r-shibusawa@daiichi-koudai.ac.jp}

\subjclass[2020]{55U35; 18N40, 55U10, 06D30, 03B38}
\keywords{cubical sets, cubical type theory, model structure,
  equivariant fibration, test category, Eilenberg--Zilber category,
  relative elegance, reversal, De Morgan algebra}

\begin{abstract}
  A companion paper identified the quotient of the interval by the
  reversal as a $K(\mathbb{Z}/2,1)$ in the cubical-type model
  structure on De Morgan cubical sets, with contractible geometric
  realization.  This paper closes the two known repair strategies
  for such defects, and re-opens the question they were meant to
  settle.  First, we determine the automorphism groups of De
  Morgan and Kleene cubes to be the hyperoctahedral groups and show
  that the equivariant repair of
  Awodey, Cavallo, Coquand, Riehl and Sattler carries over: a
  Quillen model structure with hyperoctahedral-equivariant
  fibrations exists on De Morgan and Kleene cubical sets, models homotopy type theory, and
  makes every quotient $I^k/H$ contractible --- two modifications
  are required, since the species-level interval loses all its
  points and the vertex lemma must be re-proved over orbit
  groupoids, which in fact simplifies it.  But the repair
  \emph{overshoots}: by the freeness of the reversal action on
  cells, the category of elements of $I^k/H$ has the homotopy type
  of $BH$ for every reversal-containing $H$, so the equivariant
  model structure contracts objects that the test model structure
  does not, and --- in exact opposition to the cartesian case ---
  cannot present spaces via the test comparison.  Second, we prove
  that the De Morgan and Kleene cube categories admit no relatively
  elegant embedding into any Reedy category, extending the no-go
  theorem of Cavallo--Sattler beyond monotone cube maps: the
  connection-degeneracy freedom that dissolves their winding
  obstruction at the level of vertices is annihilated by a
  forcing-and-constancy analysis at the level of free algebras,
  machine-certified by finite constraint searches; the Boolean cube
  category, by contrast, stabilizes.  Finally, the folklore witness
  for the non-coincidence of the cubical-type and test model
  structures on De Morgan cubes evaporates: the test homotopy type
  of the reversal quotient is $B\mathbb{Z}/2$, in agreement with
  the cubical-type answer.  With both repairs closed and the
  witness void, whether CCHM cubical type theory presents spaces
  through the test comparison emerges as a genuinely open problem,
  for which we record a first positive fragment: the cubical-type
  weak equivalences form a localizer and hence contain the minimal
  one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The cube category $\cube$ of CCHM cubical type theory \cite{CCHM}
carries connections and a reversal, and its presheaves interpret
univalent type theory via the cubical-type model structure
\cite{SattlerEEP, CoqNote}.  A companion paper \cite{Reversal}
proved the folklore of \cite{CoqML, SatTalk}: the quotient
$\Ldash = \square^1/(x \sim \neg x)$ of the interval by the
reversal is not weakly contractible --- indeed it is a
$K(\mathbb{Z}/2, 1)$ --- although its geometric realization is
contractible.  The present paper asks whether this defect can be
\emph{repaired}, by either of the two technologies that have
succeeded on neighbouring cube categories:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{equivariance}: on cartesian cubes, requiring uniform
  box-filling structures to be equivariant under the automorphisms
  of the filling cube repairs the analogous defect (the symmetric
  quotients $I^n/H$) and yields a model structure that coincides
  with the test model structure and presents spaces
  \cite{ACCRS};
\item \emph{relative elegance}: on cartesian cubes with one
  connection, a generalized Eilenberg--Zilber theory supplies the
  cell decompositions on which the comparison with simplicial sets
  rests \cite{CavalloSattlerElegance}.
\end{itemize}

Our answer is that \emph{both} repairs fail for the reversal, each
for a structural reason rooted in the freeness theorem of
\cite{Reversal}; and that their failure re-opens, rather than
settles, the question of the test comparison.

\subparagraph*{The equivariant route.}
\sloppy We determine the symmetry groups first (Theorem~\ref{thm:aut}): in the De Morgan and Kleene
cube categories, the automorphism group of the $n$-cube is the
hyperoctahedral $\G_n = \Sigma_n \ltimes (\mathbb{Z}/2)^n$
--- for De Morgan by Birkhoff duality and a centralizer
computation, for Kleene because the unmixed-poset dual admits no
other automorphisms at all.  The equivariant machinery of
\cite{ACCRS} then transfers (Theorem~\ref{thm:eqms}): a
$\G$-equivariant Quillen model structure exists on De Morgan and
Kleene cubical sets, models homotopy type theory, and makes every
vertex inclusion $1 \to I^k/H$, $H \leq \G_k$, a trivial
cofibration --- in particular it contracts the reversal quotient
$\Ldash$.  Two modifications of \cite{ACCRS} are needed, and we
isolate them precisely: the symmetric-sequence interval loses all
its global points under the hyperoctahedral action (the reversal
moves every vertex), so the species-level premodel structure is no
longer cylindrical and must be demoted to a generation device,
with cylindricity, the equivalence extension property, and the
fibrant-universe argument running at the cubical-set level ---
which is possible because the $\delta$-contractor route to fibrant
universes is entirely point-free; and the vertex lemma of
\cite{ACCRS}, whose proof there requires the automorphism to
\emph{fix} the vertex, is replaced by a colimit over the orbit
groupoid, which needs no fixing and is in fact a simplification.

The repair succeeds --- and that is exactly its failure.  The test
model structure on presheaves over a test category has as weak
equivalences the maps inverted by the nerve of the category of
elements \cite{Cisinski, ACCRS}; and by the freeness theorem the
reversal acts freely on the \emph{cells} of the interval, so the
category of elements of $I^k/H$ is a free quotient of a
contractible category:
\[
  N\,\el(I^k/H) \;\simeq\; BH
  \qquad (\text{$H \leq \G_k$ acting freely on cells}).
\]
Thus the equivariant structure contracts objects whose test
homotopy type is $BH \not\simeq *$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:overshoot}): its localizer strictly exceeds the
test localizer, the two model structures do \emph{not} coincide ---
in exact opposition to the cartesian case
\cite[Thm.~6.3.6]{ACCRS} --- and the equivariant model cannot
present spaces through the test comparison.  The comparison with
the cartesian case is instructive: there, the symmetric quotients
are test-contractible because the \emph{diagonal cells are fixed}
by the symmetries, gluing the quotient category into a contractible
one; the reversal has no fixed cells --- that is the freeness
theorem --- and the same freeness that makes the covering of
\cite{Reversal} a clean fibration makes the equivariant repair
overshoot.  Equivariance repairs defects born of \emph{fixed}
cells; the reversal's defect is born of their absence.

\subparagraph*{The elegance route.}  For the second repair we prove
a no-go theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:crown}): \emph{the De Morgan and
Kleene cube categories admit no relatively elegant embedding into
any Reedy category}.  Cavallo--Sattler proved this for Dedekind
cubes \cite[Thm.~A.14]{CavalloSattlerElegance} by a winding-number
argument over crown posets; their proof uses that Dedekind cube
maps are monotone on vertices, and at the vertex level their
obstruction genuinely dissolves in $\cube$ --- De Morgan maps have
arbitrary Boolean vertex actions, so the crown factorizations exist
as vertex functions.  The obstruction resurfaces one level deeper:
the required factorization is an equation in free De Morgan
algebras, the three-branch structure of the crown extensions forces
the factorizing map into the crown fibers, the fibers are pairwise
incomparable antichains, and the apparent freedom in the
negative-literal directions is annihilated by a constancy argument
--- monotone maps from a connected poset into an antichain are
constant --- so the winding obstruction returns.  The finite core
of the argument is machine-certified: exhaustive constraint
searches over the crown regions ($768$ points for De Morgan, $312$
for Kleene) find no admissible factorization, while the Boolean
cube category --- where vertex actions determine everything ---
\emph{does} stabilize, consistently with its Karoubi envelope being
the Eilenberg--Zilber category of finite sets \cite{Campion}.

\subparagraph*{The test comparison, re-opened.}  Finally we revisit
the folklore itself.  The 2018 discussions record the claim that
the cubical-type and test weak equivalences differ on De Morgan
cubes \cite{CoqML}, with $\Ldash$ as the counterexample-in-waiting:
its realization is contractible, its cubical-type homotopy type is
not.  But the test model structure does not see the topological
realization; it sees the category of elements, and
\[
  N\,\el(\Ldash) \;\simeq\; B\mathbb{Z}/2
  \;\simeq\; \text{the cubical-type answer of \cite{Reversal}}.
\]
The presumed witness is no witness: on $\Ldash$, the cubical-type
and test structures \emph{agree} (and it is the topological
realization that is the outlier --- it is not a test functor for
$\cube$).  With the witness void and both repair routes closed, we
know of no proof that the cubical-type model structure on De
Morgan cubical sets differs from the test model structure; we
therefore regard the question
\begin{quote}
  \emph{does CCHM cubical type theory present spaces through the
  test comparison, i.e.\ is $\Wtype = \Wtest$ on $\cube$?}
\end{quote}
as open, and record a first fragment
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sandwich}): the cubical-type weak
equivalences form a Cisinski localizer, hence contain the minimal
localizer.  The corresponding question for cartesian cubes is
settled in the negative by Coquand's symmetric square
\cite{CoqNote} --- which is consistent, since there the test
structure \emph{does} contract the symmetric square (fixed
diagonal cells again) while the cubical-type structure does not;
the De Morgan situation is genuinely different because the
reversal's freeness aligns the two structures on the known test
objects.

\subparagraph*{Relation to the literature.}  Theorem~\ref{thm:eqms}
is an application of the architecture of \cite{ACCRS}, and we state
precisely which components are used unchanged and which are
modified; the overshoot phenomenon it runs into is the one
\cite{ACCRS} themselves flag for BCH cubes in §1.7.2 (``equivariance
is not a catch-all solution''), here given an exact combinatorial
diagnosis (cell-freeness) and a proof.
Theorem~\ref{thm:crown} extends \cite[App.~A]{CavalloSattlerElegance}
beyond monotone cube maps.  The freeness theorem, the fiber
combinatorics of free De Morgan and Kleene algebras, and the
identification of $\Ldash$ used throughout are from
\cite{DMFibers, Kleene, Reversal}.  All finite claims are
machine-verified (\S\ref{sec:machine}).

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

We freely use: the cube categories $\cube$, $\cubeK$ of the De
Morgan and Kleene interval theories, with morphisms
$[m] \to [n]$ the $n$-tuples of free-algebra elements, and the
literal-cube representations of the free algebras
\cite{DMFibers, Kleene}; the quotient
$\Ldash = \square^1/(t \sim \neg t)$ and the freeness theorem ---
no cell of the interval is fixed by the reversal --- of
\cite{Reversal}; the equivariant machinery of \cite{ACCRS}
(uniform box filling along generalized open boxes
$(c : C \rightarrowtail I^n,\ \zeta : I^n \to I^k)$, with chosen
lifts natural in pullback squares \emph{and} in automorphisms
$\sigma$ of the filling cube $I^k$; cubical species; Garner's
algebraic small object argument); the test model structure on
presheaves over a test category, with weak equivalences the maps
$f$ such that $N\,\el(f)$ is a weak homotopy equivalence
\cite{Cisinski, ACCRS}; the fact that $\cube$, $\cubeK$ and the
Boolean cube category are strict test categories
\cite{BuchholtzMorehouse}; and relative elegance
\cite{CavalloSattlerElegance}: a Reedy category $R$ is elegant
relative to a fully faithful $i : C \to R$ when the nerve
$N_i$ preserves pushouts of lowering spans, which yields cell
decompositions of all monomorphisms of $\mathrm{PSh}(C)$ and, via
\cite[Lem.~A.5]{CavalloSattlerElegance}, a strictly monotone
degree on the poset of principal sieves of each object of $C$.

\section{The automorphism groups are hyperoctahedral}
\label{sec:aut}

\begin{thm}
  \label{thm:aut}
  In both the De Morgan and the Kleene cube categories,
  $\mathrm{Aut}([n]) \cong \G_n = \Sigma_n \ltimes
  (\mathbb{Z}/2)^n$, generated by the coordinate permutations and
  the reversals $x_i \mapsto \neg x_i$.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
  An automorphism of $[n]$ is an automorphism of the free algebra.
  De Morgan case: by Birkhoff duality, lattice automorphisms of the
  free De Morgan algebra correspond to order-automorphisms of the
  literal cube $\{0,1\}^{2n}$, which are exactly the coordinate
  permutations $\Sigma_{2n}$; compatibility with the involution
  $\neg$ (complement composed with the pair swap $\tau$) amounts,
  since complementation is central, to centralizing $\tau$ --- a
  product of $n$ disjoint transpositions, whose centralizer in
  $\Sigma_{2n}$ is $\Sigma_n \ltimes (\mathbb{Z}/2)^n$: permute
  the pairs, swap within a pair, the latter realizing
  $x_i \mapsto \neg x_i$.  Kleene case: the dual object is the
  unmixed subposet, and a direct check (machine-verified at
  $n = 2$: all eight poset automorphisms commute with the
  involution) shows its automorphisms are exactly the same signed
  permutations; the general statement follows since an
  automorphism permutes the $2n$ atoms of the poset of principal
  up-sets respecting pairs, as in the De Morgan case.
\end{proof}

\section{The equivariant model structure exists}
\label{sec:eqms}

\begin{thm}
  \label{thm:eqms}
  On De Morgan and on Kleene cubical sets there is a Quillen model
  structure whose cofibrations are the monomorphisms and whose
  fibrations are the hyperoctahedral-equivariant uniform
  fibrations: maps with chosen lifts against the generalized open
  boxes, uniform in pullback squares and in all
  $\sigma \in \G_k$ acting on the filling cube.  It is cylindrical
  and cartesian closed, satisfies the Frobenius condition, the
  equivalence and fibration extension properties, and has univalent
  universes with fibrant bases --- a model of homotopy type
  theory.  Moreover, for every $k \geq 1$, every subgroup
  $H \leq \G_k$, and every vertex $v$ of $I^k$, the inclusion
  $v : 1 \to I^k/H$ is a trivial cofibration; in particular
  $\Ldash \to 1$ is a weak equivalence.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}[Proof (outline, with the two modifications)]
  The construction follows §§2--5 of \cite{ACCRS}, with the
  groupoid $\Sigma$ replaced by the hyperoctahedral groupoid
  $\G = \coprod_k \G_k$ (legitimate by
  Theorem~\ref{thm:aut}): $\G$-species, the symmetric-sequence
  interval $\I$ (tiny, since the cube categories have finite
  decidable homs --- free De Morgan and Kleene algebras on finitely
  many generators are finite), the generating category of
  boxes, Garner's algebraic small object argument, and the orbit
  transfer along $L \dashv \Delta$.  Two points do not transfer
  verbatim.

  \emph{First modification (pointless species interval).}  A global
  point of $\I$ chooses a $\G_k$-fixed vertex of $I^k$ for each
  $k$; under the hyperoctahedral action there are none (the
  reversal moves every vertex --- the freeness theorem at the
  vertex level), so the species-level premodel structure is not
  cylindrical, and the species-level model structure of
  \cite[§4.4]{ACCRS} is unavailable.  It is also unneeded: the
  cubical-set-level architecture of \cite[§5]{ACCRS} uses the
  species layer only as a generation device.  Cylindricity at the
  cubical-set level needs the endpoint inclusions
  $0, 1 : 1 \to I$ to be trivial cofibrations, which are the
  $k = 1$ vertex-triangle generators and require no equivariance;
  the pushout-product axiom transfers along $\Delta$ (which
  preserves Leibniz exponentials); the equivalence extension
  property is proved at the cubical-set level from cylindricity
  (axiomatic); Frobenius transfers along the
  pushforward-preserving $\Delta$; and the fibrant-universe
  argument runs through the $\delta$-contractor mechanism of
  \cite[§3.6]{ACCRS}, which is entirely point-free --- it uses
  only the generic point $\delta : \I \to \I \times \I$ in the
  slice, never an endpoint of $\I$.  The fibration extension
  property and two-out-of-three then follow as in
  \cite[§5.3]{ACCRS}.

  \emph{Second modification (orbit-groupoid vertex lemma).}  In
  \cite[Lem.~5.2.5]{ACCRS} the contractibility of $I^k/H$ rests on
  morphisms of vertex triangles
  $(\mathrm{id}, \sigma) : (\emptyset \rightarrowtail 1,
  \sigma^{-1}\vec{v}) \to (\emptyset \rightarrowtail 1, \vec{v})$,
  and their diagram is constant at one triangle, which requires
  $\sigma^{-1}\vec{v} = \vec{v}$ --- only the two poles are fixed
  by all permutations.  We instead organize the $H$-action as a
  diagram over its \emph{orbit groupoid}: objects the vertex
  triangles at the vertices of one orbit, morphisms the
  $(\mathrm{id}, \sigma)$, with no fixing condition (the
  constraint $\xi = \sigma^{-1}\zeta\alpha$ of the generating
  category is then the definition of the diagram, not a
  restriction).  The colimit of this diagram of generating trivial
  cofibrations has domain
  $\mathrm{colim}(1) = 1$ (connected groupoid) and codomain
  $I^k/H$, and coalgebra forgetful functors create colimits
  \cite{BourkeGarner}, so $v : 1 \to I^k/H$ is a trivial
  cofibration for \emph{every} vertex --- a simplification as well
  as a generalization of the original lemma.  For
  $\Ldash = I/\langle\neg\rangle$ this gives
  $1 \to \Ldash$ a trivial cofibration, whence
  $\Ldash \to 1 \in \Weq$ by two-out-of-three.
\end{proof}

\section{The repair overshoots}
\label{sec:overshoot}

\begin{lem}
  \label{lem:elBH}
  Let $H \leq \G_k$ act freely on the cells of $I^k$ (by the
  freeness theorem, any $H$ containing a reversal-component acts
  freely already through that component).  Then
  $N\,\el(I^k/H) \simeq BH$; in particular
  $N\,\el(\Ldash) \simeq B\mathbb{Z}/2$.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
  $\el(-)$ preserves colimits, so
  $\el(I^k/H) = \el(I^k)/H$, a quotient of a category with
  terminal object (the slice over a representable) by an action
  free on objects.  Freeness on objects makes the action free on
  all composable strings, so the quotient functor is a covering of
  categories and
  $N(\el(I^k)/H) = N(\el(I^k))/H$, the quotient of a contractible
  free $H$-complex: a $BH$.
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}
  \label{thm:overshoot}
  On De Morgan and Kleene cubical sets, the
  hyperoctahedral-equivariant model structure does not coincide
  with the test model structure:
  $\Ldash \to 1 \in \Weq \setminus \Wtest$.  Consequently the
  equivariant model structure does not present spaces through the
  test comparison --- in exact opposition to the cartesian case
  \cite[Thm.~6.3.6]{ACCRS}.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
  $\Ldash \to 1 \in \Weq$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:eqms};
  $N\,\el(\Ldash) \simeq B\mathbb{Z}/2 \not\simeq *$ by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:elBH}, so $\Ldash \to 1 \notin \Wtest$.  Since
  $\cube$ is a strict test category \cite{BuchholtzMorehouse}, the
  test model structure presents spaces \cite{Cisinski}; a model
  structure with the same cofibrations and a strictly larger class
  of weak equivalences on a contractible-realization object is a
  different localization.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
  The diagnosis, in one line: \emph{equivariance repairs defects
  born of fixed cells, and the reversal has none.}  On cartesian
  cubes the symmetric quotients $I^n/H$ are test-contractible ---
  their element categories are glued along the fixed diagonal
  cells --- so enlarging the localizer to contract them moves the
  cubical-type structure \emph{toward} the test structure, and
  \cite{ACCRS} shows it lands exactly on it.  On De Morgan cubes
  the freeness theorem makes the reversal quotients test-nontrivial
  ($BH$'s), so the same enlargement moves \emph{past} the test
  structure.  This realizes, with a proof, the caution of
  \cite[§1.7.2]{ACCRS} that ``equivariance is not a catch-all
  solution'': the BCH cube category was their suspected example;
  the De Morgan cube category is a proven one.
\end{rem}

\section{No relatively elegant embedding}
\label{sec:crown}

We now close the second route.  Recall from
\cite[App.~A]{CavalloSattlerElegance} the crown posets $\Crown_m$
(cyclic fences of $2m$ elements), the embeddings
$c_m : \Crown_m \rightarrowtail [1]^m$, the wrapping maps
$f_a : \Crown_{am} \to \Crown_m$ of winding number $a$, and the
extensions $\overline{f} : [1]^{am} \to [1]^{m}$ sending
$c_{am}(i) \mapsto c_m(f(i))$, $\bot \mapsto \bot$, and everything
else to $\top$; the three branches have disjoint values, so the
defining square is a pullback.  If some
$i : \mathcal{C} \to R$ were relatively elegant, principal sieves
on each object of $\mathcal{C}$ would carry a strictly monotone
degree \cite[Lem.~A.5]{CavalloSattlerElegance} --- the lemma's
fullness hypothesis is part of the definition of relative
elegance, so it applies to any $\mathcal{C}$ --- and the chain
$\langle\overline{f_2}\rangle \supseteq
\langle\overline{f_4}\rangle \supseteq \cdots$ of principal sieves
on $[1]^n$ would stabilize: some
$\overline{f_a} = \overline{f_b} \circ g$ with $a < b$ and
$g : [1]^{an} \to [1]^{bn}$ a map of $\mathcal{C}$.

\begin{thm}
  \label{thm:crown}
  For $\mathcal{C} \in \{\cube, \cubeK\}$, no such $g$ exists;
  hence the De Morgan and Kleene cube categories admit no
  relatively elegant embedding into any Reedy category.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
  A first phenomenon, absent from the Dedekind case: at the level
  of \emph{vertex actions} the factorization exists.  De Morgan
  cube maps have arbitrary Boolean vertex actions (disjunctive
  normal forms over literals are functionally complete), the three
  branches of $\overline{f_b}$ are disjoint, and a set-level
  section of $f_b$ over the crown extended by
  $\bot \mapsto \bot$, else $\top$, satisfies the vertex-level
  equation --- no monotonicity, hence no winding number, obstructs
  it.  The obstruction returns at the free-algebra level, where
  the required equation
  $\overline{f_b} \circ g = \overline{f_a}$ holds in
  free algebras, i.e.\ as monotone functions on the literal cube.
  Write $G$ for the induced monotone map on literal points and
  $\mathrm{pos}$ for the projection to positive literals.  Since
  $\overline{f_b}$ is negation-free, the composite's literal
  function is $\overline{f_b}$'s vertex action applied to $G$;
  since $\overline{f_b}^{-1}(\text{crown values}) = $ crown points
  exactly, $G$ is forced, on the region
  $\{\alpha : \mathrm{pos}(\alpha) \in c_{an}(\Crown_{an})\}$, to
  take values in $c_{bn}$ of the fibers
  $f_b^{-1}(f_a(i))$ --- antichains of $b$ points spaced $2n$
  apart in $\Crown_{bn}$, pairwise incomparable.  The region is
  the product of the crown subposet with the Boolean cube of
  negative literals; the negative directions are connected, and a
  monotone map from a connected poset into an antichain is
  constant, so $G$'s crown-region behaviour factors through a
  monotone $\rho : \Crown_{an} \to \Crown_{bn}$ with
  $f_b \rho = f_a$ --- and
  $b \cdot \mathrm{wind}(\rho) = a$ with $a < b$ has no integer
  solution.  (Machine certificates: the crown-region constraint
  problems --- $768$ points for De Morgan, $312$ for Kleene, both
  with domains of size $b = 4$ --- are exhaustively unsatisfiable;
  \S\ref{sec:machine}.)  For Kleene the same argument runs on the
  unmixed literal points, whose crown region remains connected in
  the negative directions.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
  The Boolean cube category escapes: there the free algebras are
  determined by vertex actions, the set-level section \emph{is} a
  cube map, the chain stabilizes immediately, and indeed the
  Boolean Karoubi envelope is the Eilenberg--Zilber category of
  finite nonempty sets \cite{Campion}.  The trichotomy
  Boolean/Kleene--De Morgan reproduces, at the level of elegance,
  the fiber trichotomy of \cite{Kleene}: what obstructs is
  precisely the monotone literal structure that the involution
  fails to collapse.
\end{rem}

\section{The test comparison, re-opened}
\label{sec:test}

\begin{prop}
  \label{prop:sandwich}
  On any of the three cube categories, the weak equivalences
  $\Wtype$ of the cubical-type model structure form a Cisinski
  localizer: they satisfy two-out-of-three, contain the maps with
  the right lifting property against all monomorphisms, and their
  monic members are closed under pushout and transfinite
  composition.  Consequently $\Wmin \subseteq \Wtype$, where
  $\Wmin$ is the minimal localizer.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  All three properties are theorems of model-category theory for a
  model structure whose cofibrations are the monomorphisms; the
  minimal localizer is contained in every localizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}[the folklore witness is void]
  \label{rem:void}
  The 2018 record asserts that the cubical-type and test weak
  equivalences differ on De Morgan (and Boolean) cubes
  \cite{CoqML}, with the reversal quotient as the underlying
  example.  But $\Ldash$ separates the cubical-type structure from
  the \emph{topological realization}, not from the test structure:
  by Lemma~\ref{lem:elBH} its test homotopy type is
  $B\mathbb{Z}/2$, which is precisely its cubical-type homotopy
  type \cite{Reversal}.  (It follows, incidentally, that the
  topological cocube on $\cube$ is not a test functor: it computes
  $*$ where the test structure computes $B\mathbb{Z}/2$.)  On the
  cartesian side the analogous question is genuinely settled in
  the negative --- Coquand's symmetric square is
  cubical-type-nontrivial \cite{CoqNote} yet test-trivial (the
  equivariant structure contracts it and coincides with the test
  structure \cite{ACCRS}) --- but that argument turns on the fixed
  diagonal cells, which the reversal lacks.  We know of no
  surviving proof that $\Wtype \neq \Wtest$ on $\cube$, and we
  regard the question --- \emph{does CCHM cubical type theory
  present spaces through the test comparison?} --- as open.
  Proposition~\ref{prop:sandwich} gives the lower bound; the
  missing steps for the upper bound
  $\Wtype \subseteq \Wtest$ are the test-triviality of the
  generalized open boxes (plausible: their element categories are
  glued from contractible pieces along fully faithful inclusions)
  and, for equality, a converse that would necessarily be as hard
  as the known open Dedekind case \cite{StreicherWeinberger,
  CavalloSattlerElegance}.
\end{rem}

\section{Machine verification}
\label{sec:machine}

Finite claims verified exhaustively (scripts accompany the
artifact): the automorphism counts of Theorem~\ref{thm:aut}
($8 = |\G_2|$ coordinate permutations commuting with the
involution for De Morgan at $n = 2$; all $8$ poset automorphisms
of the unmixed poset, all commuting, for Kleene); the freeness
census underlying Lemma~\ref{lem:elBH} ($0$ self-dual cells
through three generators in both theories, from
\cite{Reversal}); the non-commutation
$(\neg x) \wedge e \neq \neg(x \wedge e)$ showing
hyperoctahedral equivariance is not implied by connections (the
connection-contraction argument for automatic symmetric-group
equivariance \cite[Rem.~4.25]{CavalloSattlerElegance} commutes
with permutations only); and the crown-region constraint
problems of Theorem~\ref{thm:crown}: for
$(n, a, b) = (3, 2, 4)$, the De Morgan region has $768$ points
with $16{,}728$ order constraints and the Kleene region $312$
points, all domains of size $4$; backtracking with propagation
finds no solution in either, while the fiber-incomparability and
three-branch-disjointness lemmas are checked directly.

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

The results funnel further work toward one question: the upper
bound $\Wtype \subseteq \Wtest$ of Remark~\ref{rem:void}, whose
proof would need the test-triviality of the generalized open boxes
of $\cube$, and whose converse --- equality --- would settle
whether CCHM cubical type theory presents spaces.  Both halves are
now unobstructed by any known counterexample.  Independently, the
equivariant model structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:eqms} presents
\emph{some} homotopy theory --- a localization of the test theory
in which the classifying spaces $BH$ of reversal-containing
subgroups are contracted --- whose identification we leave open;
and the orbit-groupoid form of the vertex lemma may be of use
wherever the ACCRS machinery meets non-vertex-fixing symmetries.
