\title[Natural strict fillers do not exist]{Natural strict fillers
  do not exist:\\ a no-go theorem for De Morgan cubical sets\\ and
  the median correction structure}

\author{Ryota Shibusawa}
\address{Daiichi Institute of Technology, Japan}
\email{r-shibusawa@daiichi-koudai.ac.jp}

\subjclass[2020]{06D30; 18N40, 55U35, 03B38}
\keywords{cubical sets, De Morgan algebra, filling, algebraic weak
  factorization system, median, naturality, cubical type theory}

\begin{abstract}
  Over the free De Morgan algebras that govern the strict layer of
  CCHM cubical type theory, a boundary does not determine its
  filler: the fibers of the boundary map are Boolean lattices
  $2^{D}$, and a companion paper showed that the canonical minimal
  filler fails to commute with substitution in a controlled way.
  This paper closes the question of whether the failure can be
  repaired by a cleverer choice: it cannot.  \emph{No section of
  the boundary-fiber correspondence --- no rule assigning to every
  boundary one of its strict fillers --- is natural with respect to
  substitution}; the proof takes three lines from one
  uniquely-fillable boundary whose transports under a diagonal and
  under a connective collapse land at the top and the bottom of a
  single two-element fiber, and an exhaustive machine search
  certifies that the obstruction is ubiquitous rather than
  exceptional.  What replaces the impossible choice is
  \emph{structure}: the median interpolation of the companion
  papers is computed in fiber coordinates to be a graded Boolean
  median, its compositional coherence is witnessed by explicit
  \emph{iterated} median cylinders whose faces match on the nose,
  and every compatible sphere of corrections fills --- the system of
  corrections is strictly contractible.  As a consequence, any
  filling operation for De Morgan cubical sets that is natural in
  the cube category must take values outside the strict layer: the
  weak (Kan) layer is categorically necessary, not merely
  convenient.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

This paper resolves the last open question of a structure theory,
developed in \cite{DMFibers, StrictDM, Kleene}, of the \emph{strict
layer} of De Morgan cubical sets --- the presheaves on the cube
category $\cube$ of the CCHM interval theory \cite{CCHM}, restricted
to the reparametrizations that a proof-assistant kernel applies
judgmentally.  The theory so far: boundary fibers in the free De
Morgan algebra $\DM_n$ are Boolean intervals $2^{D}$ over
antichains of diagonal points, Sperner-extremal in size
\cite{DMFibers}; every fiber has a canonical least element,
reachable by the one-step \emph{defect formula}; and the least
element commutes with a substitution $f$ exactly when the
transported boundary avoids the defect antichain --- the
\emph{coherence dichotomy} \cite{StrictDM}.  The minimal filler is
thus not natural.  The question this paper answers:

\begin{quote}
  \emph{Is any choice of strict fillers natural?  Or is the failure
  of naturality intrinsic to the strict layer?}
\end{quote}

The answer is the strongest possible no-go.

\begin{thmstar}[Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}]
  There is no section of the boundary-fiber correspondence on the
  free De Morgan algebras --- no assignment $s$ of a filler
  $s(B) \in \mathrm{fib}(B)$ to every boundary $B$ --- such that
  $s(\bnd(f^{*}g)) = f^{*}(s(B))$ for all substitutions $f$ and all
  $g = s(B)$.  Already at two generators, and already for
  endomorphisms of $[2]$, the constraints are unsatisfiable.
\end{thmstar}

The proof is three lines (\S\ref{sec:nogo}): the element
$m = \neg x \wedge y \wedge \neg y$ is the \emph{unique} filler of
its boundary, so any natural $s$ is forced on it and on all its
transports; but the diagonal $x := y$ and the connective collapse
$x := x \wedge \neg x$ send $m$ to the top, respectively the
bottom, of one and the same two-element fiber --- two forced values
for one boundary.  The two substitutions realize the two faces of
the defect formula: one \emph{adds} the invisible diagonal point,
the other does not.  A machine census shows this is no isolated
accident: unique fillers force values on $42$ of the $54$
non-singleton fibers of $\DM_2$, with $142$ pairwise conflicts, and
an exhaustive search over all
$2^{38} \cdot 4^{16}$ sections, constrained by $121$
endo-substitutions, confirms unsatisfiability without using
higher cubes.

The no-go sharpens the slogan of \cite{StrictDM} --- \emph{without
Eilenberg--Zilber normal forms, thinness cannot be a property} ---
into a theorem about \emph{choices}: not only is there no
property singling out a filler, there is no natural \emph{choice
function} either.  What survives is the correction
\emph{structure}, and the second half of the paper
(\S\ref{sec:median}) shows it is as good as such a structure can
be.  In fiber coordinates the median interpolation
$\chi = (\varphi \wedge \neg k) \vee (k \wedge \varphi') \vee
(\varphi \wedge \varphi')$ of \cite{DMFibers} is precisely a graded
Boolean median on $2^{D}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:coords}); the
composition of corrections is witnessed by an \emph{iterated}
median cylinder
$\Xi = \chi_{l}(\chi_{k}(a, b), \cyl\, c)$ whose four faces are
exactly the three interpolations and one degeneracy, on the nose
(Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence}); and every compatible sphere of
corrections fills, by the sphere-filling theorem applied in
$\DM_{n+k}$ --- the correction system is \emph{strictly
contractible}.  In the language of algebraic weak factorization
systems \cite{Garner}, the strict layer supports pointed-endofunctor
algebra structures in abundance (any section will do) but no
monad-algebra structure; the contractible median system is the
canonical pseudo-structure interpolating between the two.

The consequence for cubical type theory is worth stating plainly
(Corollary~\ref{cor:weak}): \emph{any filling operation natural in
the cube category must take values outside the strict layer}.  Kan
composition is not a convenience bolted onto the
reparametrization calculus; it is forced by naturality itself.
This gives the strict--weak divide studied throughout the series
\cite{Const, Convexity, WordProblems} a purely categorical root.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

We freely use the setting of \cite{DMFibers, StrictDM}: $\DM_n$ is
the lattice of monotone functions (up-sets) on the literal cube
$\lc_n = \{0,1\}^{2n}$, coordinates in pairs $(x_i, \neg x_i)$;
\emph{face points} have some pair in $\{(0,1), (1,0)\}$,
\emph{diagonal points} have all pairs in $\{(0,0), (1,1)\}$; the
boundary $\bnd g$ is the tuple of the $2n$ substitutions
$x_i := 0, 1$; fibers of $\bnd$ are Boolean intervals
$[m, m \cup D] \cong 2^{D}$ with $D$ an antichain of diagonal
points; $A_g$ denotes the antichain of minimal diagonal points of
$g$, and $\rmin(g) = g \setminus A_g$ is the least element of the
fiber of $g$ (the defect formula).  A substitution
$f : [m] \to [n]$ of $\cube$ induces $f^{*} : \DM_n \to \DM_m$ and
the literal-cube map $F : \lc_m \to \lc_n$.  A \emph{section} is a
function $s$ from realizable boundaries to elements with
$\bnd(s(B)) = B$; it is \emph{natural} if
$s(\bnd(f^{*} s(B))) = f^{*} s(B)$ for all $f, B$ --- i.e.\ the
transport of a chosen filler is again chosen.

\section{The no-go theorem}
\label{sec:nogo}

\begin{thm}
  \label{thm:nogo}
  No natural section exists, already for $n \leq 2$ and already
  with respect to the endomorphisms of $[2]$.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
  Let $m = \neg x \wedge y \wedge \neg y \in \DM_2$, the up-set of
  the single point $\alpha = ((0,1), (1,1))$.  Its boundary fiber is
  a singleton: a diagonal point $\delta$ could be added only if
  every face point above $\delta$ already lay in $m$, and for each
  of the four diagonal points one checks a face point above it
  missing from $m$ (e.g.\ for $\delta = ((0,0),(1,1))$ the face
  point $((1,0),(1,1))$ is not in $m$).  So any natural section
  satisfies $s(\bnd m) = m$, and hence is forced on every
  transport: $s(\bnd(f^{*}m)) = f^{*}m$ for all $f$.  Now
  \[
    (x := y)^{*}\, m \;=\; y \wedge \neg y,
    \qquad
    (x := x \wedge \neg x)^{*}\, m
    \;=\; (x \vee \neg x) \wedge y \wedge \neg y .
  \]
  These two elements have the same boundary --- their common fiber
  is the two-element interval with
  $D = \{((0,0),(1,1))\}$, of which $y \wedge \neg y$ is the top
  and $(x \vee \neg x) \wedge y \wedge \neg y$ the bottom --- and
  they are distinct.  Two different values are forced at one
  boundary; contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
  The proof is the coherence dichotomy of \cite{StrictDM} weaponized:
  the diagonal substitution reaches the invisible diagonal point
  (its $F$ maps a face point onto it), the connective collapse does
  not, and a \emph{unique} filler upstream leaves no freedom to
  absorb the difference downstream.  Machine census
  (\S\ref{sec:machine}): unique fillers force values on $42$ of the
  $54$ non-singleton fibers of $\DM_2$, with $142$ pairwise
  conflicts of forced values; and independently of the forcing
  argument, an exhaustive backtracking search over all sections,
  constrained by $121$ endo-substitutions of $[2]$, finds no
  solution.  The no-go is thus robust: it does not hinge on one
  clever element, and it needs neither higher cubes nor
  degeneracies.
\end{rem}

\begin{cor}
  \label{cor:weak}
  Any filling operation for De Morgan cubical sets that is natural
  in $\cube$ --- in particular, any algebraic fibrancy structure on
  the representables, and any functorial fibrant replacement ---
  takes some values outside the strict layer.  Equivalently: in any
  cubical type theory over the De Morgan interval whose filling
  operation is substitution-stable, fillers cannot all be
  reparametrizations; a weak layer is categorically necessary.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
  A strict-valued natural filling operation restricts, on
  representables, to a natural section, which
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} forbids.
\end{proof}

\section{The median correction structure}
\label{sec:median}

If no filler can be chosen, all fillers must be carried --- with
the identifications between them.  The companion papers supply the
identifications; here we show they are computed by a graded Boolean
median and compose \emph{on the nose} via iterated medians.

\begin{prop}[fiber coordinates]
  \label{prop:coords}
  Let $[m, m \cup D]$ be a fiber and
  $\varphi = m \cup S$, $\varphi' = m \cup S'$ with
  $S, S' \subseteq D$.  Then the median interpolation in one fresh
  generator $k$ is, as an up-set of $\lc_{n+1}$,
  \begin{multline*}
    \chi(\varphi, \varphi')
    \;=\; \cyl\bigl(m \cup (S \cap S')\bigr)\\
    \cup\; \{(\alpha, \kappa) : \alpha \in S \setminus S',\
    \kappa \in \neg k\}
    \;\cup\; \{(\alpha, \kappa) : \alpha \in S' \setminus S,\
    \kappa \in k\},
  \end{multline*}
  i.e.\ the cylinder on the common part and the symmetric
  difference graded by the two half-cylinders of $k$: the Boolean
  median of $S$, $S'$ and the $k$-grading, computed pointwise on
  $D$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  Evaluate
  $(\varphi \wedge \neg k) \vee (k \wedge \varphi') \vee
  (\varphi \wedge \varphi')$ at a point $(\alpha, \kappa)$: for
  $\alpha \in m \cup (S \cap S')$ the third disjunct fires for all
  $\kappa$; for $\alpha \in S \setminus S'$ only the first, i.e.\
  $\kappa \in \neg k$; for $\alpha \in S' \setminus S$ only the
  second.  (Machine-checked on all pairs of a rank-two fiber of
  $\DM_2$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{thm}[coherence by iterated medians]
  \label{thm:coherence}
  Let $a, b, c$ lie in one fiber.  Then the iterated median
  cylinder
  \[
    \Xi \;:=\; \chi_{l}\bigl(\chi_{k}(a, b),\ \cyl_{k}(c)\bigr)
    \;\in\; \DM_{n+2}
  \]
  has faces, on the nose:
  \[
    \Xi|_{l=0} = \chi_{k}(a,b), \quad
    \Xi|_{l=1} = \cyl_{k}(c), \quad
    \Xi|_{k=0} = \chi_{l}(a,c), \quad
    \Xi|_{k=1} = \chi_{l}(b,c).
  \]
  More generally, every compatible sphere assembled from
  interpolations and cylinders over one fiber fills in
  $\DM_{n+j}$: the correction system is strictly contractible.
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
  The four face identities are absorption computations from
  Proposition~\ref{prop:coords} (each face substitutes an endpoint
  into one grading; machine-checked exactly on a rank-two fiber).
  For the general statement, observe that a compatible sphere of
  corrections over a fiber has, at each original face of $\lc_n$,
  the constant value of the common boundary --- so it is a
  compatible sphere in $\DM_{n+j}$, and the sphere-filling theorem
  of \cite[Thm.~4.1]{DMFibers} provides a filler, whose defect
  freedom is again a Boolean fiber.  Contractibility at every level
  follows by induction, exactly as in the strict-contractibility
  analysis of \cite{Convexity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}[the pseudo-structure]
  In the terminology of algebraic weak factorization systems
  \cite{Garner}, generated in $\widehat{\cube}$ by the boundary
  inclusions $\bnd\square^n \hookrightarrow \square^n$: any section
  equips the representables with an algebra structure for the
  \emph{pointed endofunctor} of the associated one-step
  construction, but Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} forbids the
  compatibilities required of a \emph{monad} algebra valued in
  strict fillers.  The data that does exist naturally is: the full
  Boolean fiber $2^{D}$ of fillers (structure, not property), the
  defect antichain measuring every transport
  \cite{StrictDM}, and the strictly contractible median system of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence} identifying all choices coherently.
  This is precisely a fibered, strictly contractible
  \emph{pseudo}-algebra --- and the no-go says pseudo is optimal.
  Steiner's anticipation that thin fillers in weak settings
  ``exist but not necessarily uniquely'' \cite{Steiner} is thus
  realized in the sharpest form: existence, canonical
  non-uniqueness, and provable impossibility of natural selection.
\end{rem}

\section{Machine verification}
\label{sec:machine}

Three computations back the theorems (scripts accompany the
artifact).  (1)~\emph{Forcing census}: over $\DM_2$, the unique
fillers (singleton fibers) force values, via endo-substitutions
from a generating set of $121$, on $42$ of the $54$ non-singleton
fibers, with $142$ pairwise conflicts; the specific core of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo} is one of them, chosen for the shortness of
its by-hand verification.  (2)~\emph{Exhaustive search}: a
backtracking search with constraint propagation over all sections
--- $2^{38} \cdot 4^{16}$ assignments for the $38$ two-element and
$16$ four-element fibers --- confirms unsatisfiability against the
same $121$ substitutions, independently of the forcing argument.
(3)~\emph{Median identities}: the fiber-coordinate formula of
Proposition~\ref{prop:coords} and all four face identities of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence} were verified exactly on a rank-two
fiber of $\DM_2$.  We remark that (1) alone proves the theorem, (2)
alone re-proves it, and the printed proof needs neither.

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

Two directions.  First, the \emph{quantitative geography of the
obstruction}: the forcing graph --- boundaries as vertices, forced
transports as edges --- was here used only for unsatisfiability,
but its structure (which fibers are forced from how many unique
fillers, and how conflicts distribute over the defect rank) should
refine the coherence dichotomy of \cite{StrictDM} into a
cohomological invariant of the strict layer; the Kleene case
\cite{Kleene}, with its two polar fibers, is the natural test bed,
and we expect its forcing graph to be consistent away from the
poles.  Second, the \emph{converse direction}: Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}
shows natural filling requires values beyond reparametrizations;
how far beyond?  The minimal natural filling operations valued in
Kan composites --- and whether the CCHM $\mathrm{hcomp}$ is initial
among them in a precise sense --- is, we believe, the right
formulation of the ``value of the weak layer'' question raised
across this series, and the strictly contractible median system of
\S\ref{sec:median} is the comparison datum such a formulation
needs.
